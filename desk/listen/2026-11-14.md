*Markets Desk, Saturday, November 14, 2026.*

## Egypt

**The day:** Foreigners bought a net EGP 101.5bn of Egyptian bonds and bills in the week to Thursday while selling EGP 365m of shares, which is why the pound firmed 0.9% to EGP 53.50 as the EGX30 rose 1.7%. Nothing since Friday changes the desk's calls; the EGX reopens Sunday.

**The close:** EGX30 down 0.91% at 52,023.37; EGX70 EWI up 0.43% at 18,527.43; USD/EGP down 0.22% at 53.50; Gold 24K, per gram up 0.37% at 6,825.

**Foreigners buy EGP 101.5bn of Egyptian debt in a week, sell EGP 365m of shares:** In the week to Thursday, foreigners bought a net EGP 101.54bn of Egyptian bonds and bills while Egyptians sold EGP 108.40bn, the EGX's weekly report shows. The desk's view: Foreign money is coming for the pound's yield, not for Egyptian companies: a one-year bill at 26.07% against 12.7% inflation pays more than any EGX30 dividend, and Friday's rise in US yields to 5.15% did not stop it.

**Bills drew EGP 282bn of bids on Thursday as the pound firmed to EGP 53.50:** The central bank sold EGP 114.03bn of 182- and 364-day bills on Thursday against a EGP 100bn target, from bids of EGP 282.19bn: the six-month bill yielded 26.07% and the one-year 25.02%. The desk's view: Demand at 2.8 times the amount sold, with yields steady near 25–26%, says the carry trade is holding even as US rates rise: foreign money is still being paid about 12 points over 12.7% inflation.

**Brent ends a second losing week at $95.84 as the IEA sees a surplus:** Brent settled at $95.84 on Friday, about 2.5% lower on the week, after the International Energy Agency said supply would run 1.7m barrels a day above demand this quarter, the first surplus since the war began. The desk's view: A surplus forecast caps oil near $90–100 even with mines in Hormuz, which helps Egypt's import bill; the desk keeps its view that Brent stays below $105, judged November 20.

**Juhayna's third-quarter profit rises 33%; stock slips 2.3%:** Juhayna's third-quarter revenue rose 25% to a record EGP 10bn and net profit 33% to EGP 654m, after a EGP 107m currency loss; EBITDA margin widened to 14.7% from 14.3% (earnings call, Investing.com transcript). The desk's view: The call confirmed the growth is volume and price, not one-offs, which supports the desk's EGP 27–28 projection by end of January; the fall looks like selling into the news, not a change in the case.

**The stocks:** No EGX session since Thursday, when Juhayna fell 2.3% and Talaat Moustafa 2.0%.

**The desk's views:** Higher rates protect CIB's margins: on track; Telecom Egypt holds its ground into MSCI's November 30 inclusion: open; The pound stays firmer than EGP 56 per dollar into the IMF review: on track; Gas rationing keeps fertilizer stocks under pressure through November: open.

**Coming up:** The EGX reopens Sunday; Palm Hills and Cleopatra report Monday.

## United States

**The day:** Chip stocks fell for a second day on Friday: AMD lost 8.8%, Broadcom 4.8% and Nvidia 2.0% to $162.85, pulling the Nasdaq down 0.91% and the S&P 500 0.40% to 7,561.67, while the Dow rose 0.10%. Producer prices rose less than forecast, but the 10-year yield closed at 5.15%.

**The close:** S&P 500 down 0.40% at 7,561.67; Nasdaq down 0.91% at 25,136.27; Dow Jones up 0.10% at 52,347.55.

**Chips fall for a second day: AMD drops 8.8%, Broadcom 4.8%:** AMD fell 8.8% and Broadcom 4.8% on Friday, giving back Thursday's rally after Jensen Huang's exit, and Nvidia lost 2.0% to $162.85, below $4tn in market value. The desk's view: This is money moving inside the market, not out of it: the equal-weight S&P 500 rose 0.2% while the index fell.

**Producer prices rise 0.3%, below forecast; December hike odds near 90%:** Producer prices rose 0.3% in October against 0.4% expected, and 6.5% from a year earlier, the most since November 2022; core prices rose 0.3%, as forecast. The desk's view: A hike in December is priced; the question is whether it is one and done.

**Staples lead as Walmart rises 1.5% and Costco 1.3%:** Consumer staples were the best S&P 500 sector on Friday, up 1.4%. The desk's view: The rotation from AI spenders to steady earners that the desk called on October 30 is working: Walmart is up 5.6% since then and Alphabet 4.2%.

**Brent holds at $95.84 as the IEA forecasts a surplus:** Brent settled at $95.84 on Friday and WTI at $88.56, both about flat, leaving Brent about 2.5% lower on the week after the IEA said supply would run 1.7m barrels a day above demand this quarter. The desk's view: A surplus forecast and 71m barrels of emergency stocks still to come cap oil, so the desk keeps Brent below $105 into November 20.

**The stocks:** Broadcom fell 4.8% to $347.01 and Nvidia 2.0% to $162.85 on Friday; Meta rose 2.9% to $641.69 and Walmart 1.5%.

**The desk's views:** Chip stocks stay under pressure into Apple's results: right; Money rotates from AI spenders to steady earners: on track; Brent stays below $105 while Iran talks continue: on track.
