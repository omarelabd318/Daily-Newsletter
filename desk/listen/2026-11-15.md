*Markets Desk, Sunday, November 15, 2026.*

## Egypt

**The day:** Suez Canal revenue nearly doubled to $724 million in October, the canal's best month since late 2023, adding a second dollar source to the foreign buying of Egyptian bills just as the last IMF review slips to online talks. The EGX reopens today from Thursday's 52,023.37, with the pound at EGP 53.50.

**The close:** EGX30 down 0.91% at 52,023.37; EGX70 EWI up 0.43% at 18,527.43; USD/EGP down 0.22% at 53.50; Gold 24K, per gram up 0.37% at 6,825.

**Suez Canal revenue jumps 94% to $724m in October:** The canal earned $724m in October against $373m a year earlier, as transits rose 32% to 1,490 ships and net tonnage 86% to 88m tons, the Suez Canal Authority said: its best month since Houthi attacks diverted shipping in late 2023. The desk's view: At October's pace the canal would earn about $4bn a year more than in 2025, a steady dollar inflow that does not depend on foreign bill buyers or the IMF.

**Egypt's last IMF review moves online with $2.3bn at stake:** The IMF mission for the eighth and last review of Egypt's $8bn program left Cairo on November 5 without a staff-level agreement, and talks continue online, EnterpriseAM and Masrawy reported. The desk's view: A delay is not a breakdown: the money matters less than the signal, since foreigners bought a net EGP 101.5bn of bills in the week to Thursday and Suez is earning again.

**Foreigners buy EGP 101.5bn of Egyptian debt in a week, sell EGP 365m of shares:** In the week to Thursday, foreigners bought a net EGP 101.54bn of Egyptian bonds and bills while Egyptians sold EGP 108.40bn, the EGX's weekly report shows. The desk's view: Foreign money is coming for the pound's yield, not for Egyptian companies: a one-year bill at 26.07% against 12.7% inflation pays more than any EGX30 dividend, and Friday's rise in US yields to 5.15% did not stop it.

**Talaat Moustafa's nine-month profit rises 19% to EGP 15bn:** Talaat Moustafa's consolidated net profit after tax rose 19% to EGP 15.01bn in the nine months to September from EGP 12.63bn, on revenue up 24.1% to EGP 47.55bn, the company said. The desk's view: Sales are strong, but profit is growing more slowly than revenue and the gross margin slipped, which is what high rates do to a developer: buyers still sign, the cost of carrying them rises.

**The stocks:** Talaat Moustafa's nine-month consolidated profit after tax rose 19% to EGP 15.01bn on revenue up 24.1%; the stock closed Thursday at EGP 83.95.

**The desk's views:** Higher rates protect CIB's margins: right; Telecom Egypt holds its ground into MSCI's November 30 inclusion: open; The pound stays firmer than EGP 56 per dollar into the IMF review: on track; Gas rationing keeps fertilizer stocks under pressure through November: open.

**Coming up:** Palm Hills, Cleopatra and Edita report Monday; EFG Holding Wednesday.

## United States

**The day:** Chip stocks fell for a second day on Friday: AMD lost 8.8%, Broadcom 4.8% and Nvidia 2.0% to $162.85, pulling the Nasdaq down 0.91% and the S&P 500 0.40% to 7,561.67, while the Dow rose 0.10%. Producer prices rose less than forecast, but the 10-year yield closed at 5.15%.

**The close:** S&P 500 down 0.40% at 7,561.67; Nasdaq down 0.91% at 25,136.27; Dow Jones up 0.10% at 52,347.55.

**Chips fall for a second day: AMD drops 8.8%, Broadcom 4.8%:** AMD fell 8.8% and Broadcom 4.8% on Friday, giving back Thursday's rally after Jensen Huang's exit, and Nvidia lost 2.0% to $162.85, below $4tn in market value. The desk's view: This is money moving inside the market, not out of it: the equal-weight S&P 500 rose 0.2% while the index fell.

**Producer prices rise 0.3%, below forecast; December hike odds near 90%:** Producer prices rose 0.3% in October against 0.4% expected, and 6.5% from a year earlier, the most since November 2022; core prices rose 0.3%, as forecast. The desk's view: A hike in December is priced; the question is whether it is one and done.

**Staples lead as Walmart rises 1.5% and Costco 1.3%:** Consumer staples were the best S&P 500 sector on Friday, up 1.4%. The desk's view: The rotation from AI spenders to steady earners that the desk called on October 30 is working: Walmart is up 5.6% since then and Alphabet 4.2%.

**Iran claims a strike in Bahrain and mines hit tankers near Hormuz:** Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it hit a US air-defense radar at Bahrain's Riffa base with six drones early on Sunday, and Bahrain issued its first nationwide alert since September. The desk's view: The surplus forecast and emergency stocks still cap oil, so the desk keeps Brent below $105 into November 20, but at low confidence now: the claims are unconfirmed, yet a named tanker loss or a hit on Gulf oil sites would move Brent $5–10 in a session.

**The stocks:** Broadcom fell 4.8% to $347.01 and Nvidia 2.0% to $162.85 on Friday; Meta rose 2.9% to $641.69 and Walmart 1.5%.

**The desk's views:** Chip stocks stay under pressure into Apple's results: right; Money rotates from AI spenders to steady earners: on track; Brent stays below $105 while Iran talks continue: on track.

**Coming up:** Retail sales and Nvidia results Tuesday; Fed minutes Wednesday.
