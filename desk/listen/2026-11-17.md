*Markets Desk, Tuesday, November 17, 2026.*

## Egypt

**The day:** Foreigners bought a net EGP 29.4bn of Egyptian debt on Monday, about $547m, while the Nasdaq slid into a correction, and the pound firmed to EGP 53.75 by Tuesday morning. The EGX30 held flat but small caps fell 3.76% on profit-taking.

**The close:** EGX30 up 0.02% at 52,199.80; EGX70 EWI down 3.76% at 18,028.40; USD/EGP down 0.20% at 53.75; Gold 24K, per gram down 0.73% at 6,755.

**Foreigners buy $547m of Egyptian debt as Wall Street slides:** Arab and foreign investors bought a net EGP 29.38bn of Egyptian government debt on Monday, about $547m, almost all of it foreign. The desk's view: A global sell-off that sent the Nasdaq down 2.49% did not pull money out of Egypt: foreigners kept buying debt at about 25%, and Treasury yields eased, which widens the carry.

**Small caps fall 3.76% while the EGX30 holds flat:** The EGX70 EWI fell 3.76% to 18,028.40 on Monday and the EGX100 2.88%, while the EGX30 edged up 0.02% to 52,199.80 on gains in CIB, Telecom Egypt and Eastern Company. The desk's view: The small-cap index had run 7.7% in a week to Thursday on speculative buying, so a one-day fall of this size is a reset, not a turn in the market.

**Misr Life Insurance will float 15% on the EGX in December:** Misr Life Insurance plans to sell 750 million existing shares, 15% of its capital, in December, subject to approval, through a private placement and a public offering at one book-built price; the shares belong to Misr Insurance Holding, owned by the Sovereign Fund of Egypt, so the company raises nothing (press release via Fintech Gate; Al Borsa, Hapi Journal). The desk's view: A second state sale in a quarter tests demand at a time foreigners are buying debt more than shares; Banque du Caire closed at EGP 5.58 on Monday, down 1.6%.

**Telecom Egypt and Edita lead on results and index buying:** Telecom Egypt rose 5.2% to EGP 143.00 on Monday, more than double its price at the start of the year, two weeks before MSCI adds it to its standard index at the November 30 close; its nine-month profit rose 44% to EGP 24.4bn. The desk's view: Both moves rest on figures, not tips: earnings growth of 40% or more and, for Telecom Egypt, index buying with a date.

**The stocks:** Edita rose 7.4% to EGP 20.66 on its 41.5% profit rise and Telecom Egypt 5.2% to EGP 143.00 before MSCI's inclusion; MOPCO fell 4.1% and Palm Hills 3.4%.

**The desk's views:** Telecom Egypt holds its ground into MSCI's November 30 inclusion: on track; The pound stays firmer than EGP 56 per dollar into the IMF review: on track; Gas rationing keeps fertilizer stocks under pressure through November: on track; Developers lag while rates stay high: on track.

**Coming up:** Nvidia reports Tuesday night; EFG Holding Wednesday; Misr Life's offering in December.

## United States

**The day:** The Nasdaq fell 2.49% into a correction on Monday as chip stocks slid 5.3% before Nvidia's results tonight and a new US rule on remote access to AI chips. Treasuries rallied and Brent fell 3.5%, so the sell-off was about AI, not rates.

**The close:** S&P 500 down 1.92% at 7,416.59; Nasdaq down 2.49% at 24,509.76; Dow Jones down 1.41% at 51,608.17.

**Nasdaq enters a correction as chip stocks fall 5.3% before Nvidia:** The S&P 500 lost 1.92% to 7,416.59 on Monday, the Nasdaq 2.49% to 24,509.76, more than 10% below its record, and the Dow 1.41% to 51,608.17. The desk's view: The quarter itself is not the question: worries about how AI spending is financed (Fed Governor Barr put the largest AI firms' on-balance-sheet debt above $800bn by year-end) and the new export curbs will outlast one beat.

**Washington shuts the overseas route to US AI chips for Chinese firms:** The Commerce Department issued a rule on Monday to stop Chinese companies using advanced AI chips through overseas data centers: customers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil now need a special license to buy advanced Nvidia and AMD chips, and the rule takes effect in 90 days. The desk's view: It cuts into Southeast Asian sales that had been one of Nvidia's fastest-growing channels, and it lands as the company reports under an interim chief.

**Brent falls 3.5% to $92.44 as a surplus outweighs Hormuz:** Brent fell about 3.5% on Monday to $92.44 a barrel, as the IEA's forecast of a fourth-quarter surplus outweighed Iran's claim that a fourth tanker hit a mine east of the Strait of Hormuz. The desk's view: With Hormuz traffic near zero and oil still falling, the market has priced the closure and is trading the surplus; that is the desk's case for Brent below $105 to November 20, still on track.

**Anthropic prices its IPO Wednesday at $70–78 into a falling market:** Anthropic prices its shares on Wednesday and starts trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT, with a range of $70 to $78 a share. The desk's view: A strong debut would show money still wants AI at the right price and could steady the sell-off; a weak one would deepen it.

**The stocks:** Intel fell 7.0% to $55.47, Oracle 5.0% to $119.09 and Nvidia 4.1% to $156.14 as chip stocks slid before Nvidia's results tonight; Alphabet lost 4.1% and Amazon 3.3%.

**The desk's views:** Money rotates from AI spenders to steady earners: on track; Brent stays below $105 while Iran talks continue: on track.

**Coming up:** Nvidia results Tuesday after the close; Fed minutes and Anthropic's pricing Wednesday; Walmart Thursday.
