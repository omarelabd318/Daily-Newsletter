*Markets Desk, Thursday, November 26, 2026.*

## Egypt

**The day:** Foreigners sold $663m of Egyptian debt in two days and the pound slipped 0.8% to EGP 54.76, even as Egypt reached a staff deal on the IMF's final review. The EGX30 fell 0.68% on EGP 6.2bn, with Egyptian institutions selling a net EGP 162.5m and foreign institutions buying 140.3m.

**The close:** EGX30 down 0.68% at 50,538.70; EGX70 EWI down 1.32% at 16,886.70; USD/EGP up 0.98% at 54.76; Gold 24K, per gram up 1.34% at 6,440.

**Debt outflows push the pound to 54.76 despite an IMF staff deal:** The central bank's dollar rate rose to EGP 54.63 buy and 54.76 sell by Wednesday's close, from 54.17 and 54.31 that morning, as foreigners sold a net $576m of Egyptian government debt on Tuesday and $87m on Wednesday, after buying $842.2m the week before. The desk's view: The outflow is the Fed, not Egypt: third-quarter US growth revised to 4.3% and core PCE at 3.3% made a December 9 hike near certain, and every hike narrows what one-year bills at 26.16% pay a dollar investor.

**Maersk tells Sisi it will bring its full network back through Suez:** Maersk chief executive Vincent Clerc told President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi on Wednesday that its sailings through the canal have resumed and its full network will return to the Red Sea route in the coming months; in October the group expected all its services back by March 2027. The desk's view: A steady dollar stream for the pound over 2027 rather than a jump this month: no date was given, and the Houthis' strikes on Saudi Red Sea ports on the same day are the risk that could send carriers back around Africa.

**Houthi strikes on Saudi ports lift Brent 3.5% to $94.61:** The Houthis claimed strikes on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura refinery and the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan on Wednesday, and Brent settled up $3.21, or 3.5%, at $94.61. The desk's view: Bad for the pound twice over: every $10 on Brent adds to a fuel and LNG import bill already behind a September trade deficit of $5.3bn, and attacks on Red Sea ports are what kept shipping lines away from Suez.

**Orascom Construction falls 2.8% to its lowest since August as the OCI deal closes:** Orascom Construction completed its combination with OCI on Wednesday, issuing 97,201,359 new shares, about 47% of the enlarged group, lifting its share count to 207.4m from 110.2m. The desk's view: Supply, not the business: the backlog of $11.6bn, $4.8bn of it in the US, is unchanged, but holders of OCI who never chose an Egypt-listed builder receive the shares before December 8, and index weights adjust.

**The desk's views:** The pound stays firmer than EGP 56 per dollar into the IMF review: on track; The dollar stays below EGP 56.50 through the central bank's December 17 meeting: open; Telecom Egypt holds its ground into MSCI's November 30 inclusion: on track; Gas rationing keeps fertilizer stocks under pressure through November: on track.

**Coming up:** The EGX trades Thursday while Wall Street is shut for Thanksgiving; the weekly T-bill auction, with one-year bills, sets the yield after the pound's slide.

## United States

**The day:** Wall Street clawed back most of a deep intraday rout on Wednesday: the Nasdaq closed down 0.62% after falling 4.6%, as growth revised up to 4.3% and core PCE at 3.3% made a December Fed hike near certain. Oracle lost 5.1% and Nvidia 3.9%; markets are shut for Thanksgiving.

**The close:** S&P 500 down 0.25% at 7,182.78; Nasdaq down 0.62% at 23,585.80; Dow Jones up 0.33% at 50,182.95.

**AI stocks claw back a 4.6% Nasdaq plunge as credit fears linger:** The Nasdaq Composite fell as much as 4.6% early on Wednesday and closed down 0.62% at 23,585.80; the S&P 500 lost 0.25% to 7,182.78 after being down 3.2%, while the Dow rose 0.33%. The desk's view: The buyers who turned the day are bargain hunters, not a change in the story: the market still doubts that debt-funded AI spending earns its cost, and a Fed hike on December 9 raises that cost.

**Growth revised up to 4.3% and core PCE at 3.3% lock in a December hike:** Third-quarter growth was revised up to an annualized 4.3% from 3.4%, against a 3.5% forecast, and core PCE rose 0.3% in October for an annual 3.3%, with headline PCE at 3.8%. The desk's view: A hike is priced; the risk now is the path.

**Washington proposes Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, China included:** The US Trade Representative proposed tariffs of 15% to 40% under Section 301 on 14 trading partners on Wednesday, after the Supreme Court's February ruling against earlier duties. The desk's view: A slow-moving risk rather than a shock: proposals go through hearings before they apply, which gives importers time.

**Nvidia halts AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries:** Nvidia told manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro to hold pending AI-server orders for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia under US export rules that took effect last week, the Financial Times reported (via Yahoo Malaysia and The National). The desk's view: Deferred revenue rather than lost revenue if licenses come, but it lands while the stock is 20% down this year and investors already question its customers' financing.

**The desk's views:** Money rotates from AI spenders to steady earners: on track.

**Coming up:** US markets are closed Thursday and trade a short session Friday; OPEC+ meets Sunday and the Fed decides December 9.
