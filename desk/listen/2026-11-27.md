*Markets Desk, Friday, November 27, 2026.*

## Egypt

**The day:** One-year T-bill yields rose to 26.35% on Thursday, the highest since March 2024, and the dollar to EGP 54.91, as a near-certain December 9 Fed hike raises what Egypt pays to hold foreign money. Foreigners still bought a net EGP 224.9m of shares while the EGX30 slipped 0.45%.

**The close:** EGX30 down 0.45% at 50,311.78; EGX70 EWI flat at 16,886.20; USD/EGP up 0.27% at 54.91; Gold 24K, per gram up 0.26% at 6,457.

**One-year bill yield hits 26.35%, highest since March 2024, as the dollar rises to 54.91:** The average yield on one-year treasury bills rose to 26.35% at Thursday's auction from 26.16% a week earlier, and the six-month yield to 25.74% from 25.36%; the central bank accepted EGP 87.2bn against a EGP 95bn target. The desk's view: Egypt is paying more to keep foreign money as the Fed tightens, and taking less than it offered shows the central bank resisting a faster climb.

**Brent nears $98 after strikes on Kuwait and Jazan, before OPEC+ meets:** Brent settled at $96.57 on Thursday, its highest since November 6, after drones hit a site in Kuwait and a projectile struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery, and traded at $97.62–97.95 on Friday morning. The desk's view: With OPEC+ likely to stand pat, oil stays in a $90–100 range set by Gulf supply risk, at the dear end for Egypt, which imports fuel and LNG in dollars just as bill yields climb to hold foreign money.

**Suez transits run 31% above last November as container lines return:** In the week to November 22, 318 ships of 30.1m dwt crossed the canal, 31% more than the weekly average of November 2025 though 28% below pre-crisis tonnage, and container capacity reached 984,782 TEU, 24% below pre-crisis levels. The desk's view: A second dollar source is rebuilding beside foreign bill buying, which softens the pound's exposure to a Fed hike.

**Orascom Construction rises 3.5% to EGP 790 as OCI leaves Amsterdam:** Orascom Construction rose 3.5% to EGP 790.00 on Thursday, OCI's last day of trading in Amsterdam before it delists on Friday. The desk's view: About a fifth of the new shares go to holders outside the Sawiris family, some of whom bought OCI for its Dutch listing and may sell Egyptian shares; that supply can cap the stock into mid-December.

**The desk's views:** The pound stays firmer than EGP 56 per dollar into the IMF review: wrong; The dollar stays below EGP 56.50 through the central bank's December 17 meeting: open; Telecom Egypt holds its ground into MSCI's November 30 inclusion: on track; Gas rationing keeps fertilizer stocks under pressure through November: on track.

**Coming up:** The EGX is shut Friday and Saturday; Misr Life's offer price is due Saturday, OPEC+ meets Sunday and MSCI's changes take effect at Monday's close.

## United States

**The day:** Wall Street reopens for a half-day session after Thanksgiving with Nasdaq futures up about 1%, after Wednesday's AI credit scare: Oracle's credit swaps hit a record and S&P Global put Nvidia on CreditWatch negative. A December 9 Fed hike stays priced as near certain.

**The close:** S&P 500 down 0.25% at 7,182.78; Nasdaq down 0.62% at 23,585.80; Dow Jones up 0.33% at 50,182.95.

**AI credit scare: Oracle swaps hit a record, S&P puts Nvidia on watch:** Oracle opened 7.45% lower on Wednesday as its five-year credit-default swap spread hit a record, and closed down 5.1% at $103.88, about 10% below the $116 at which it sold 86.2m shares on November 2. The desk's view: The credit market, not earnings, is now setting the price of AI spending: the companies that borrow to build data centers pay more each week, and Friday's thin rebound does not change that.

**A December hike is priced at about 98%; the guidance is the risk:** CME's FedWatch puts the odds of a quarter-point rise at the December 8–9 meeting at about 98%, from a range of 3.75–4.00%. The desk's view: The hike itself is no longer news; a statement that leaves January open would hit rate-sensitive stocks and help the dollar, while a signal of a pause would lift small caps and housing.

**Brent heads for its first weekly gain in five weeks before OPEC+:** Brent settled at $96.57 on Thursday, its highest since November 6, after strikes on Kuwait and Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery, and traded near $98 on Friday morning; WTI was at about $86. The desk's view: A $90–100 range holds while Gulf attacks continue, which helps Exxon's cash flow and hurts airlines and consumers already paying more for credit.

**HP jumps 11.5% on a revenue beat as memory costs squeeze its margins:** HP rose 11.5% to $31.15 on Wednesday, the S&P 500's biggest gain, after fourth-quarter revenue of $16.0bn beat the $15.3bn consensus; memory and storage now make up 40% of its PC bill of materials, and PC margins fell to 3.0% from 5.8%. The desk's view: The beat came from prices, not margins: hardware makers are passing on memory costs while their own margins shrink, so the rally rests on a quarter that analysts still rate a sell.

**The desk's views:** Money rotates from AI spenders to steady earners: on track.

**Coming up:** US stocks close at 1pm New York (8pm Cairo) on Friday; OPEC+ meets Sunday and the Fed decides on December 9.
