*Markets Desk, Saturday, November 28, 2026.*

## Egypt

**The day:** Misr Life Insurance priced its offer at EGP 12.80, inside its range, after institutions bid 3.42 times the shares on offer: big money still wants Egyptian equity with one-year bills at 26.35%. The EGX30 ended the week 2.2% lower at 50,311.78 as the dollar rose 1.9% to EGP 54.91.

**The close:** EGX30 down 0.45% at 50,311.78; EGX70 EWI flat at 16,886.20; USD/EGP up 0.27% at 54.91; Gold 24K, per gram up 2.99% at 6,650.

**One-year bill yield hits 26.35%, highest since March 2024, as the dollar rises to 54.91:** The average yield on one-year treasury bills rose to 26.35% at Thursday's auction from 26.16% a week earlier, and the six-month yield to 25.74% from 25.36%; the central bank accepted EGP 87.2bn against a EGP 95bn target. The desk's view: Egypt is paying more to keep foreign money as the Fed tightens, and taking less than it offered shows the central bank resisting a faster climb.

**Brent eases to $95.44 before OPEC+ as Saudi-Houthi strikes continue:** Brent settled 1.17% lower at $95.44 on Friday, still up 3.1% on the week. The desk's view: A rollover is priced in, so Sunday is unlikely to lower Egypt's fuel bill; the risk is upward, from attacks on Saudi energy sites such as the Jazan refinery.

**Suez transits run 31% above last November as container lines return:** In the week to November 22, 318 ships of 30.1m dwt crossed the canal, 31% more than the weekly average of November 2025 though 28% below pre-crisis tonnage, and container capacity reached 984,782 TEU, 24% below pre-crisis levels. The desk's view: A second dollar source is rebuilding beside foreign bill buying, which softens the pound's exposure to a Fed hike.

**Orascom Construction rises 3.5% to EGP 790 as OCI leaves Amsterdam:** Orascom Construction rose 3.5% to EGP 790.00 on Thursday, OCI's last day of trading in Amsterdam before it delists on Friday. The desk's view: About a fifth of the new shares go to holders outside the Sawiris family, some of whom bought OCI for its Dutch listing and may sell Egyptian shares; that supply can cap the stock into mid-December.

**The stocks:** Orascom Construction rose 3.5% to EGP 790.00, recovering Wednesday's fall as OCI delists from Amsterdam; Edita rose 2.9% to EGP 21.30 on a $25m IFC loan for Iraq and EFG Holding 2.5% to EGP 26.98, its sixth straight gain.

**The desk's views:** The pound stays firmer than EGP 56 per dollar into the IMF review: wrong; The dollar stays below EGP 56.50 through the central bank's December 17 meeting: open; Telecom Egypt holds its ground into MSCI's November 30 inclusion: on track; Gas rationing keeps fertilizer stocks under pressure through November: on track.

**Coming up:** The EGX reopens Sunday after OPEC+ meets; MSCI's changes and Misr Life's retail close come Monday.

## United States

**The day:** Chipmakers led Wall Street's best week since April 2025: the S&P 500 rose 1.60% to 7,297.49 in Friday's half-day session and 5.6% on the week, as the chip index gained 4.7%. The rebound runs straight into a December 9 hike priced at about 98%.

**The close:** S&P 500 up 1.60% at 7,297.49; Nasdaq up 2.31% at 24,129.50; Dow Jones up 1.18% at 50,774.94.

**Chipmakers lead Wall Street's best week since April 2025:** The S&P 500 rose 1.60% to 7,297.49, the Nasdaq 2.31% to 24,129.50 and the Dow 1.18% to 50,774.94 in Friday's half-day session, as Micron gained 7.6%, Marvell 7.3% and the chip index 4.7%; Nvidia ended only 0.8% higher. The desk's view: This is relief after the worst week since 2020, not a turn: the credit worries behind the selloff are unchanged, with Oracle still below its $116 offer price, volume was thin, and the Fed is set to tighten on December 9.

**A December hike is priced at about 98% as the dollar firms:** Markets price a 98% chance of a quarter-point rise at the December 8–9 meeting, which would take the range to 4.00–4.25%; CME's FedWatch showed 99.4% on Thursday. The desk's view: The hike itself is no longer news; a statement that leaves January open would hit rate-sensitive stocks and help the dollar, while a signal of a pause would lift small caps and housing.

**Tesla rises 5.2% to $400 as SpaceX merger talks advance:** Tesla rose 5.2% to $400.00 on Friday, from $380.14 on Wednesday, after trading as high as $401.18, as special committees of Tesla's and SpaceX's boards negotiate a possible all-stock merger; the FT reported on November 23 that each company named five directors to the talks. The desk's view: Without terms the stock is trading on hope: an all-stock deal would dilute Tesla's holders at a price no one has yet set, and Texas's incoming attorney general has promised scrutiny.

**Vertex falls 5.2% as the FDA delays its kidney drug to February:** The FDA moved its decision on povetacicept for IgA nephropathy from November 30 to as late as February 28, 2027, after classifying a late reply from Vertex as a major amendment. The desk's view: Three months' delay costs Vertex little in value but hands Otsuka and Vera time in the same disease; the fall prices the delay, not a rejection.

**The stocks:** Intel rose 5.4% to $57.40 and Tesla 5.2% to $400.00 as chipmakers and SpaceX merger talk led Friday's half-day session; Amazon gained 4.0% to $254.12, Broadcom 3.8% to $312.34, Oracle 3.1% to $107.13 and Meta 3.1% to $628.96.

**The desk's views:** Money rotates from AI spenders to steady earners: right.

**Coming up:** OPEC+ meets Sunday; Nvidia trades without its dividend on December 2 and the Fed decides on December 9.
