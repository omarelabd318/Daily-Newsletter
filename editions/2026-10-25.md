## Egypt News

**Parliament and Saudi Arabia:** President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said on 18 October that Egypt would provide Saudi Arabia with "all necessary and possible" support against Houthi attacks and asked parliament to authorise deploying Egyptian forces if Riyadh requests help. The vote, delayed once, is now expected on 26 October. Officials describe any role as strictly defensive. The decision matters because it would be one of Egypt's most significant regional military commitments in years, with direct consequences for Red Sea security and Suez Canal traffic.

**Interest rates and inflation:** Urban inflation slowed to 13.9% in September from 14.5% in August, helped largely by a base effect, since prices rose 1.3% month on month. Food inflation edged up to 7.2%, and core inflation stood at 14.1%. The Monetary Policy Committee meets on Thursday 29 October, with the overnight deposit rate at 19% and lending at 20%. A hold would extend a pause that has lasted five straight meetings, and a cut looks unlikely while oil prices and the weaker pound keep price pressure alive.

**Fuel prices and energy costs:** The Automatic Pricing Committee kept domestic fuel and gas cylinder prices unchanged in mid-October, after increases of 14% to 30% in March. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly said the state is absorbing higher energy costs for now, noting that the budget assumed oil at $75 a barrel while prices are near $110. For households this means no new pump-price shock, but the fiscal cost grows with every dollar Brent adds. The Cabinet Media Centre also stressed that remarks about possible power cuts described hypothetical scenarios and not a decision. Electricity subsidies are budgeted up 39% to EGP 104.2 billion in FY2026/27.

**The pound:** The Central Bank of Egypt quoted the dollar at about EGP 52.4 at the start of October, roughly 9% weaker than before the Iran war began, and some market trackers showed further weakness in recent sessions. Reserves are a cushion: net international reserves stood at about $57.3 billion at the end of September. A softer pound feeds into imported food and medicine costs.

**Suez Canal:** Canal revenues rose 23% to $4.67 billion in fiscal 2025/26, according to figures cited by Egyptian officials, still well below the record $10.25 billion of 2023. The authority targets $8 billion in the current fiscal year, but renewed Gulf fighting is making container lines hesitant to return to the route, and that target now looks ambitious.

**IMF programme:** About $7.3 billion has been disbursed under the current IMF loans, and the 48-month programme ends in December 2026. The World Bank has raised its FY2025/26 growth estimate for Egypt to 5.1%, giving policymakers some room as negotiations on what follows take shape.

## Top World Headlines

**Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia:** The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two ballistic missiles fired towards Taif and Yanbu on Saturday and intercepted another aimed at Riyadh. Debris from missiles headed for the Mecca and Medina regions fell in western Saudi Arabia, a claim the Houthis deny targeting. Riyadh's main airport is curtailing flights until further notice, and Aramco has told Dhahran headquarters staff to work remotely. The escalation is the main driver of oil prices.

**US posture in the Gulf:** Washington has ordered non-emergency personnel and all family members to leave Saudi Arabia. Reports also point to US preparations for possible strikes on Iran, with mines and tanker attacks reported near Hormuz. The risk is a wider regional war that closes shipping lanes vital to global energy supplies.

**Iran's threats:** Two senior Iranian security officials told Reuters that Tehran has plans to target undersea internet cables in the Gulf if the US and Israel launch fresh attacks. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi blamed the US for tensions around the Strait of Hormuz during talks with Russia's Sergei Lavrov, while Turkey urged diplomacy. Cable disruption would hit Egypt, a major hub for subsea routes between Europe and Asia.

**Ukraine talks:** President Volodymyr Zelensky said in an interview published Sunday that Donald Trump is trying to force Ukraine to surrender. US envoy Steve Witkoff is expected in Norway this week for another negotiating round. A Russian drone strike in the Kharkiv region killed six people, including three children.

**Gaza:** Israeli strikes killed at least seven Palestinians this week, according to news roundups, keeping pressure on the ceasefire framework in which Egypt remains a central mediator.

**Israel:** A man was detained after allegedly attacking the security detail of Naftali Bennett in Tel Aviv, Times of Israel reported on Saturday, a sign of the tension in the country's political climate.

**Yemen:** The Houthis have nearly encircled Yemen's third-largest city and cut most of its supply routes, deepening the humanitarian crisis that underlies the Saudi front.

## Regional and Gulf

**Saudi Arabia under fire:** The kingdom's air defences have been tested for days, and the disruption to airline traffic and Aramco operations is a direct economic cost. Investors in Gulf equities and sukuk are watching whether the fighting damages energy infrastructure.

**Egypt's role:** Cairo's offer of defensive support reflects both solidarity and self-interest. Saudi Arabia is among Egypt's largest Arab investors and a key source of deposits, financing and remittances, and the Red Sea route is central to Suez income.

**Gulf capital for Egypt:** Gulf funds have been among the largest buyers in Egypt's recent investment wave, but Arab investors were net sellers on the EGX last week, selling about EGP 282 million on net in one weekly tally. Sustained regional risk could slow new inflows and push investors toward safer assets.

**Oil exporters' windfall and risk:** Higher crude lifts Gulf revenues, but physical threats to facilities and shipping offset the gain. Egypt, a net energy importer, sits on the opposite side of that equation.

**Hormuz:** Reports of mines and tanker attacks near the strait keep insurance premiums high and shipping cautious, adding to freight costs across the region.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**EGX30:** The benchmark closed Thursday at 51,052 points, down roughly 3.4% on the day by one count, though weekly tallies differ, with another reporting a 2.3% weekly loss. On Sunday 18 October the index fell 2.6% to 50,896, its lowest close since 1 July, amid heavy selling by foreign investors. The broader EGX70 has also dropped sharply, and market capitalisation lost more than EGP 100 billion in a week. Because sources differ on exact percentages, the direction is clearer than the precise size of the fall.

**Why stocks are falling:** Regional war risk, a softer pound and global rate worries are all weighing. Foreign selling was heavy through mid-October, and Egyptian retail and institutional buyers have absorbed part of it.

**Banque du Caire IPO:** The Financial Regulatory Authority approved the prospectus for a 30% stake worth up to about EGP 24.25 billion ($453 million), which can be upsized to 45% on demand. The EBRD and IFC have each agreed to take up to 15.74% of the enlarged private placement. A confirmed pricing and trading date was not available at publication. It would be the exchange's largest listing in dollar terms in at least five years.

**MNT-Halan:** The fintech listed on 20 October as HALN.CA after selling about 20% at EGP 24.50 a share, raising EGP 7.84 billion with demand of about EGP 126 billion. The strong subscription shows deep local liquidity even as foreign funds step back.

**Central bank:** Thursday's meeting is the main domestic event of the week. A hold at 19% and 20% is the consensus, and the bank's tone on oil, the pound and inflation will matter more than the decision itself.

### Global Markets

**Wall Street:** The S&P 500 slipped 0.5% on Friday to 7,623.68, the Dow fell 1.2% to 52,215.16 and the Nasdaq Composite eased 0.2% to 25,955.34. The S&P 500 lost 0.5% on the week and the Nasdaq 0.7%. Higher oil prices and the coming Fed meeting weighed on sentiment.

**The Fed:** The Federal Reserve meets on 27-28 October. BMO expects a hold now and a quarter-point rise in December, a reminder that markets are debating tightening, not cuts.

**Bonds:** The 10-year Treasury yield has been near 5%, after dropping sharply in one session, which keeps pressure on emerging-market borrowing costs and Egypt's debt-service bill.

**Europe and Asia:** One weekly tally had the Stoxx 600 up about 2.1% for its best week since July and Japan's Nikkei down about 5.4%, its worst since July. The dating of that tally is unclear, so the figures should be read with care.

**Crypto:** Bitcoin recovered to about $80,800 on Sunday after dipping to $79,146 on Saturday, its weakest in more than a month, with the Fed meeting the next catalyst.

**Impact on Egypt:** Higher oil and a hawkish Fed are a double squeeze for emerging markets. They raise Egypt's import bill and cut appetite for local-currency debt and equities.

## Worth Knowing

**Reading an IPO price with the P/E ratio:** The price-to-earnings ratio divides a share's price by the company's earnings per share, showing how many years of current profit an investor pays for.

**Example:** A company earns EGP 2 per share and lists at EGP 24, giving a P/E of 12. If similar listed banks trade at 8, the offer looks expensive unless growth is faster. If peers trade at 15, it looks cheap.

**Why it matters now:** With Banque du Caire and MNT-Halan, investors compare offer prices with peers, growth and dividends. A low P/E can signal a bargain or a business in decline, so the ratio works best next to growth rates, debt levels and sector norms. IPO sizes (EGP 7.84 billion for MNT-Halan, up to EGP 24.25 billion for the bank) matter less than the price paid per pound of earnings.
