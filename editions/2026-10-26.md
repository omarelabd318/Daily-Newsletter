## Egypt News

**Parliament and Saudi Arabia:** The House of Representatives is due to vote today, after a one-week delay, on President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi's request to authorise air defence and logistical support for Saudi Arabia against Houthi missile and drone attacks. The measure needs two-thirds of all 596 seats, roughly 398 votes. Officials stress the mission is strictly defensive and the National Defence Council has reportedly backed it. Two opposition blocs and several individual MPs have voiced objections. The outcome matters for Egypt's Gulf partnerships, Red Sea security and Suez Canal traffic.

**Record-low pound:** The dollar reached EGP 55.89 at the Central Bank of Egypt on Sunday, above the previous peak of EGP 54.73 set on 7 April. The pound has lost about 14% this year and about 5% in October. Foreign and regional investors net sold roughly $1.13 billion of local debt in three sessions last week. A weaker pound raises the cost of imported food, medicine and fuel, which is why Thursday's rate decision is closely watched.

**IMF final review:** An IMF mission arrived on Sunday for the eighth and final review of the $8 billion Extended Fund Facility and the last review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Passing both would release about $2.3 billion. Talks, expected to last about two weeks, cover state asset sales, exchange-rate flexibility, monetary policy and energy subsidies. The Fund wants roughly $1.5 billion in divestment proceeds by programme end, with nearly $1 billion still to raise. Two fuel price increases committed earlier were skipped, and prices are frozen through December. The programme expires on 15 December, and the government is reported to prefer technical assistance over a new loan.

**Economic transformation plan:** Planning Minister Ahmed Rostom presented a draft National Economic Transformation Programme for 2027-2030, now open to two months of public dialogue. Targets include growth of 6.8% by FY2029/30, inflation of 5-7%, public debt near 69.5% of GDP and 200,000 new ICT jobs. It also promises to lift the public-sector minimum wage from EGP 8,000 to EGP 15,000 and to build about one million housing units. Whether the pound and borrowing costs allow those goals is the question for households.

**Wheat and the farm sector:** The winter wheat procurement price rises 6% to EGP 2,650 per ardeb from EGP 2,500, with service costs cut 50% for about one million feddans of small holdings. The state bought over five million tonnes of local wheat this season, about 30% more than in 2025. Higher local procurement reduces import exposure at a time of a weak currency.

**Paperless customs:** From Thursday 29 October, customs will stop accepting paper documents and importers will need an e-seal and e-signature on the Nafeza platform. Average clearance times have fallen to about 5.8 days from about 16 days in mid-2024, against a two-day target. Importers without digital credentials risk delays.

**International school grades:** Education Minister Mohamed Abdel Latif rejected requests to exclude Arabic and history from equivalency scores for international-school Grade 12 students, where each subject carries 10% of the score. Court rulings against the rule are suspended pending appeal, and schools must also cap annual fee rises at 7%. The issue affects families planning university admission.

## Top World Headlines

**Houthi attacks and mediation:** The Houthis say they have fired at Riyadh, Dammam and Najran airports, energy sites and an air base, and Saudi forces continue to intercept missiles. Qatari mediators have travelled to Tehran in coordination with Washington, and Iran's foreign minister has urged Riyadh to pursue diplomacy. Oil eased on the diplomacy but the attacks have not stopped. The IMF's regional director has called the economic impact on the kingdom "limited" so far.

**Hormuz tension:** On Friday Iran claimed four tankers were hit and three mined near the Strait of Hormuz, a claim the Associated Press could not verify and President Donald Trump called a stunt. Aramco evacuated staff from its Dhahran area. Trump is reported to be weighing a deal with Iran against renewed strikes, so the risk of wider conflict persists.

**Oil price:** Brent settled around $117 a barrel on Friday and traded near $114 on Monday morning. ING has said its $80 average forecast for the fourth quarter now looks unattainable. For Egypt, a net energy importer, each sustained dollar on the price adds to the import and subsidy bill.

**Brazil:** Lula won a fourth term with 51.8% of the vote against Flávio Bolsonaro, according to EnterpriseAM's report, a setback for Trump, who had backed his rival. The result keeps Latin America's largest economy under a left-leaning government from January 2027.

**Ukraine:** President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine is ready to join trilateral technical talks with the United States and Russia, and Vladimir Putin signalled interest after meeting US envoy Steve Witkoff. Earlier this weekend Zelensky had accused Washington of pressuring Kyiv to surrender. Any talks would shape sanctions, grain and energy markets.

**Big Tech earnings:** Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon report this week. Investors are watching AI spending, which has pushed several large companies into negative free cash flow.

## Regional and Gulf

**Gulf markets under pressure:** Saudi Arabia's Tadawul fell 2.7% on Sunday to 9,439, Abu Dhabi's index lost 1.2% and Dubai's 1.4%. Saudi equities were down about 4.1% on the week. Egypt's blue-chip index, by contrast, was up 1.5% for the week to Thursday before Sunday's fall.

**EU-Gulf summit postponed:** A joint summit planned for Saudi Arabia next week was postponed because Houthi attacks raised concerns about air travel to the kingdom, according to Zawya.

**Gulf money in Egypt:** Analysts argue that nearly a decade of UAE state-backed investment in Egypt now looks like a hedge against Hormuz disruption. Qatari Diar has planned a $30 billion investment on Egypt's Mediterranean coast, with a first phase launched in August. Gulf funds remain important sources of deposits and equity, though Arab investors were net sellers on the EGX in recent weeks.

**Islamic finance:** An LSEG report showed global Islamic finance assets rising 9% to $6.5 trillion in 2025, with record sukuk issuance of $291.4 billion. Saudi Arabia became the largest sukuk market by outstanding value, which supports the deepening of Gulf debt markets.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**EGX30:** The benchmark fell 3.6% on Sunday to 49,234, its lowest close since early April, though it remains up about 17.7% this year. Turnover was EGP 7.2 billion, 36.4% below the 90-day average, and international investors were the only net sellers. Beltone Holding fell 16.5%, Edita 10.3% and Juhayna 10.1%, while Mopco gained 4.2% and Emaar Misr 2.4%.

**Central bank on Thursday:** The Monetary Policy Committee meets on 29 October with the deposit rate at 19% and the lending rate at 20%. EFG Hermes, CI Capital and HC Securities expect a hold, and only one analyst expects a hike. Urban inflation was 13.9% in September, with monthly inflation up to 1.3% from 0.1%. Deutsche Bank has raised its end-2026 inflation forecast to 15.7% and sees easing resuming in the first half of 2027, with the currency its main risk.

**Banque du Caire IPO:** Price confirmation is due today, with trading expected around 2 November. The indicative range was EGP 4.82-5.30 a share, and Banque Misr has raised the sale to 45% from 30%, which at the top of the range implies about $680 million by EnterpriseAM's estimate. Retail orders in the public tranche are open until Wednesday 28 October. Management points to a return on equity in the high 20s, capital adequacy near 22% and a net interest margin of about 7.5%. It would be the largest EGX listing in dollar terms in years, and a test of foreign appetite.

**Corporate deals:** Grova awarded Hassan Allam Construction an extra $500 million for Phase II of its EastHills development, taking the contractor's combined work on the project past $1 billion. Telecom Egypt's Xceed bought an Indian business-process outsourcing company with more than 500 staff in Hyderabad, its sixth market.

**Index change:** The EGX SME index, Tamayuz, becomes an equal-weighted index from 1 December, ending its 35% single-stock cap, with reviews moving to June and December.

**Impact on Egypt:** Higher oil, a strong dollar and rate-hike talk in Washington combine to squeeze emerging markets, which explains part of the foreign selling of Egyptian bonds and shares.

## Worth Knowing

**Real return: what an interest rate is actually worth.** A headline yield tells how many pounds an investment pays, not how much buying power it adds. Two adjustments matter: tax and inflation, and for foreign investors, the exchange rate.

**Example:** An investor buys a one-year Egyptian treasury bill yielding 25%. With 20% withholding tax on the interest, the net return is 20%. With inflation at about 14%, the real return is (1.20 ÷ 1.14) − 1, or roughly 5.3%. For a dollar-based investor, the pound's 14% fall this year cuts the dollar value of the proceeds: 1.20 × 0.86 is about 1.03, a gain of only about 3%.

**Why it matters now:** Foreign selling of Egyptian debt shows how quickly the currency view overrides a high headline yield. If investors expect the pound to weaken further, a 25% yield can still lose them money in dollars. The central bank watches the gap between yields and inflation for the same reason: it must keep real rates positive enough to hold investors without choking growth.
