## Egypt News

**Saudi defence mandate:** The House of Representatives passed the authorisation by the required two-thirds majority after closed sessions on 18, 19 and 26 October. The text lets President al-Sisi take the "necessary" measures to support Saudi Arabia against Houthi missile and drone attacks, and Egyptian officials describe the role as strictly defensive and limited to protecting Saudi territory. Support applies at Riyadh's request. UN Secretary-General António Guterres urged maximum restraint, and Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said Cairo still favours a diplomatic settlement. Egypt has not sent large ground forces abroad since 1991, and Red Sea security directly affects Suez Canal income, which is why the vote carries weight beyond the Gulf.

**Record-weak pound:** The Central Bank of Egypt quoted the dollar at about EGP 56.21 to buy and 56.35 to sell on Tuesday, up from about EGP 55.9 on Sunday. The currency has lost roughly 14% this year. Importers of food, medicine and fuel absorb the cost first, which is why Thursday's rate decision is watched so closely. BMI Country Risk expects the pound to hold within a 52-57 range into the first quarter of 2027, with reserves near $57 billion.

**One million homes:** A four-year programme budgeted at about EGP 1 trillion would build roughly one million homes across social, middle-income, rental, rent-to-own and worker housing. Separately, President al-Sisi directed the government and the Tahya Misr Fund to build 200,000 rental units, sharing the cost equally. For households priced out of ownership, rental and rent-to-own schemes are the more relevant part of the plan.

**Minimum wage proposal:** Egypt Forum 2026, which closed after six days of sessions in the New Administrative Capital, recommended lifting the public-sector minimum wage from EGP 8,000 to EGP 15,000 by fiscal 2030. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly outlined the recommendations. The forum reported more than 61,000 registered participants and over 16,000 submitted ideas. The proposal covers about 4.5 million state employees, and its cost will depend on growth and inflation holding up.

**Winter hours and paperless customs:** Shops will close at 10 pm on weekdays from 30 October under winter operating hours, and clocks go back one hour at midnight on 29 October. The same day, customs stops accepting paper documents, so importers need an e-seal and e-signature on the Nafeza platform. Businesses without digital credentials risk clearance delays.

**Infrastructure and water:** The public-private partnership committee cleared seven projects, including desalination plants for Suez (about 1 million cubic metres a day) and Port Said (about 900,000), plus four industrial wastewater plants. Sumed is weighing a $400 million expansion at Ain Sokhna, including a fourth berth. Both reflect a push to secure water and energy capacity as demand and regional risk rise.

**Border incident:** Israeli forces said they detained an Egyptian national who crossed the border fence near Nitzana overnight. No further details were available, and the case is likely to be handled through the usual consular channels.

## Top World Headlines

**Israel votes:** About 7.3 million Israelis are eligible to vote for the 26th Knesset. Final polls show a tight race between Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc and the opposition led by Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party, with neither forecast to reach a majority without partners it has ruled out. Shas leader Aryeh Deri says his party will not back a government that fails to protect military-service exemptions for yeshiva students. Coalition arithmetic, not the headline result, will decide who governs, and talks could run for weeks.

**Iran and Hormuz:** Washington and Tehran remain on the brink. Delegations from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey have been in Tehran, Qatar is mediating, and CNN reports that Netanyahu pressed for a strike on Iran before the election but that President Donald Trump refused. Analysts quoted in Israeli media say a resumption of the war looks more likely than not. BMI's oil forecasts assume a preliminary US-Iran agreement to reopen the strait, a premise that has not been tested.

**Oil:** Brent settled at about $115.81 on Monday, down 0.8%, and traded near $114.7 on Tuesday. BMI expects it to stay above $100 in the fourth quarter and above $110 in the first quarter of 2027, if a deal materialises. As a net energy importer, Egypt feels every sustained dollar in its import and subsidy bill, and the government has frozen fuel prices through December.

**Federal Reserve:** The Fed ends its two-day meeting on Wednesday, with the target range at 3.75-4.00%. Most economists expect a hold, though some funds see it as a pause before a possible December hike, with one report putting the odds near 80%. Higher US rates strengthen the dollar and raise borrowing costs for emerging markets such as Egypt.

**Brazil:** Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term, and former president Jair Bolsonaro's party has acknowledged the runoff result. Trump said the outcome "looked rigged", according to news bulletins. The result keeps Latin America's largest economy under a left-leaning government from January 2027.

**Ukraine:** A localised humanitarian ceasefire was reached around Oleshky, and US and Russian diplomats are exploring trilateral talks. Any negotiation would affect sanctions, grain exports and European energy prices, but a comprehensive truce is not close.

**Microsoft buyouts:** Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees, roughly 27% of its 223,000 workforce, according to EnterpriseAM. The move shows how heavily AI spending is reshaping corporate cost structures.

**UK and Europe:** British Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he wants the UK to rejoin the EU in his lifetime but ruled out reversing Brexit in this parliament. Separately, the EU is reportedly preparing import curbs on Chinese goods in sectors such as chemicals, furniture and machinery, with proposals possible from the first quarter of 2027. The EU's trade deficit with China reached $404 billion last year.

## Regional and Gulf

**Houthi attacks:** The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Saudi Arabia's Eastern Region. An earlier strike on Riyadh's airport on 10 October killed 13 people, including an Egyptian flight attendant. The attacks remain the main driver of Gulf risk pricing.

**Saudi, Pakistan, Turkey defence pact:** Egypt is not a party to the Makkah Joint Defense Agreement, which links Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey. Cairo's mandate is therefore a separate, bilateral commitment and has been framed that way by officials.

**Jordan and Syria:** Jordan struck targets in Syria after a terror attack on a church, and Syria's reported death toll rose to eight. The episode adds another front to an already crowded regional security picture.

**Lebanon:** Lebanese sources say Hezbollah is rearming with fresh Iranian funding, rebuilding drone and explosives capabilities, which raises the risk of renewed conflict with Israel.

**Cyprus gas via Egypt:** Cyprus signed a $1.8 billion deal to develop its first gas field, with exports to Europe planned via Egypt, according to news bulletins. The project would reinforce Egypt's role as a regional gas hub.

**Gulf capital in Egypt:** Qatari Diar's planned $30 billion investment on Egypt's Mediterranean coast is one of several Gulf commitments the government is relying on. Four foreign cornerstone funds, the IFC and the EBRD are anchoring the Banque du Caire offering, a sign that institutional appetite for Egyptian assets has not disappeared.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**Banque du Caire IPO:** The offering priced at EGP 5.30, the top of the indicative EGP 4.82-5.30 range. Institutional orders reached EGP 205.8 billion (about $3.72 billion), the largest private tranche in EGX history, equal to about six times coverage, or 19.8 times excluding anchors. Banque Misr is selling 45% of the bank, raising about EGP 36.4 billion (roughly $658 million) at a valuation near EGP 80.8 billion (about $1.46 billion). Retail orders totalled 454 million shares against 343.1 million offered, and subscriptions close on Wednesday 28 October, with execution on 29 October and trading expected on 2 November. Analysts cite fair values of EGP 6.55 (Ostoul base case) to EGP 7.8 (AAIS), about 47% above the offer price. The proceeds go to Banque Misr, not the Treasury, though the IMF wants roughly $1.5 billion in divestment receipts before its programme expires on 15 December. Shares are subject to a lock-up on 51% for at least two years.

**EGX30:** The benchmark closed at 48,417 on Monday, down 1.66% after touching 47,900 intraday. It is the third straight losing session and about 8.4% below Wednesday's close, though the index is still up 15.75% this year. Turnover was EGP 8.9 billion, 21.2% below the 90-day average, and local investors were the only net buyers. Ibnsina Pharma rose 4.6% while Telecom Egypt fell 5.7%.

**Brokers get relief:** The Financial Regulatory Authority temporarily halved the minimum net liquid capital ratio for brokers, from 10% to 5% of total liabilities, through the 29 October session. The measure is designed to ease margin pressure after the sharp sell-off.

**Central bank on Thursday:** The Monetary Policy Committee holds its seventh meeting of 2026 on 29 October, with the deposit rate at 19% and the lending rate at 20%. Urban inflation was 13.9% in September, and Egypt's credit default swap spread is around 357 basis points. Most analysts expect a hold, with the currency the central bank's main constraint.

**Enppi IPO:** The government is aiming to launch the Engineering for the Petroleum and Process Industries offering in the second half of January, priced in dollars, according to EnterpriseAM. The state would sell 10-20%, and a Gulf dual listing, possibly in Riyadh, would follow. Enppi must complete the offering by about 28 December unless the regulator extends its temporary listing.

**Other deals:** Al Ahly Capital agreed to buy El Sewedy's 40% stake in the company behind the New Damietta university project (CIRA), with closing expected by end-2026. Rameda's board approved a share-swap plan to acquire Art Pharma, subject to 90% acceptance. Digital Planet Investments (DPI) closed its Nclude fintech fund at over $112 million, and logistics start-up Turuq raised a seed round from Den VC.

## Worth Knowing

**How an IPO is priced and allocated.** Banque du Caire's offering is a clear example of book-building. Before listing, the sellers publish an indicative price range and invite institutions to bid. If demand far exceeds the shares on offer, the price is set at the top of the range, as happened here at EGP 5.30. Orders are then scaled back, or "allocated", so that investors receive only a fraction of what they asked for.

**Example:** The private tranche was about six times covered. An institution that bid for 60,000 shares at the top of the range would receive roughly 10,000 under simple pro-rata allocation, since only one share in six is available. The retail tranche was about 1.3 times covered (454 million shares requested for 343.1 million offered), so a retail investor would receive roughly three-quarters of the shares requested. The number of shares sold, about 6.86 billion at EGP 5.30, multiplied out gives the EGP 36.4 billion raised.

**Why it matters:** Heavy oversubscription signals demand, but it does not guarantee that the shares will rise once trading begins. Anchor investors and lock-ups, such as the two-year restriction on 51% of the bank, limit early selling. Investors should compare the price with fair-value estimates, which range here from EGP 6.55 to EGP 7.8, and with the bank's returns and capital ratios before deciding whether the premium is justified.
