## Egypt News

**Importers squeezed:** Dollars are available through banks, but with the greenback above EGP 56 the cost of bringing goods in has climbed. Some banks charge sourcing commissions of 0.1% to 0.25% depending on the sector, and port storage and demurrage average about $150 per container per day, according to EnterpriseAM. The Central Bank of Egypt has told banks to prioritise productive sectors and keep strategic-goods importers supplied. Many importers are pricing goods at the rate on the day of entry rather than hedging, in the hope that the Hormuz crisis eases. Goods worth over $65 billion have cleared Egyptian ports since January, with under $5 billion still waiting. For shoppers, the risk is a gradual pass-through into prices rather than a sudden jump.

**Food prices and the government response:** Food manufacturers say foreign-currency sourcing is running normally and prices are being held, but some traders are marking up items such as cheese and edible oil as households stock up. Supply Minister Sherif Farouk said the price-reduction initiative covers 30 basic commodities, with discounts of up to about 25% across 284 outlets in 190 districts. Shoppers comparing prices at those outlets against local shops may find the gap worth checking.

**Wheat:** Local wheat deliveries to the state have risen 17% to 5 million tonnes, according to the Ministry of Supply, which credits higher procurement prices. State buyers have also resumed imports, with cargoes unloading this month after no purchases since May, and Pakistan and Mexico have joined the list of approved origins, now 22. Imports in January to September rose 1.9% to about 9.03 million tonnes, with Russia and Ukraine supplying about 84% in January to August. Silo capacity of 4.4 million tonnes across 85 silos is set to rise to 6 million by 2030.

**Oil storage gap:** Speaking at Egypt Forum 2026, President Sisi said Egypt has built storage for 33 million barrels but lacks the $2.5 billion needed to fill it, enough for six to eight months of needs. Egypt is targeting a strategic reserve of 16 million barrels by year-end, with 10 million from Libya and 6 million from Iraq, at a reported cost of about $1.36 billion before shipping and insurance. The government cleared $6.1 billion in arrears to international oil companies in June and now pays foreign partners about $240 million a month. An expansion at the Al Hamra terminal, costing $457 million, aims to lift crude storage to 5.3 million barrels from 2.5 million by December.

**Qena solar:** Preliminary financing terms worth $650 million were signed on 26 October for a 1 GW solar plant with 200 MWh of battery storage at Nag Hammadi in Qena, in the presence of Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly. Norway's Scatec is the developer, with the European Investment Bank, the EBRD and the African Development Bank as lenders, and the National Bank of Egypt takes part in the project company. Once running, the plant is expected to supply about a third of the electricity Egypt Aluminium needs. No construction or operating dates have been given.

**Paperless customs and a new dry port:** From 29 October customs goes fully paperless on the Nafeza registration platform, so importers without digital credentials risk clearance delays. Separately, the Cabinet approved a state-owned vehicle to build and run a dry port and logistics zone at Sadat City, with no investment figure or timeline disclosed.

**Man crossed into Israel:** The Foreign Ministry said a man who crossed into Israel had been missing since July and is psychologically unstable. The case is expected to be handled through consular channels.

## Top World Headlines

**Fed decision tonight:** The Federal Reserve concludes its two-day meeting, with the target range at 3.75% to 4.00% and a hold widely expected. Markets are pricing in at least two more hikes by mid-2027, which keeps the dollar firm and borrowing costs high for emerging markets such as Egypt. The announcement is due at 2pm New York time, about 9pm in Cairo.

**Big Tech earnings:** Microsoft and Alphabet report results after the US close today, with investors focused on how much cloud growth justifies the roughly $600 billion of planned 2026 AI spending across Amazon, Microsoft and Alphabet, per market previews. Tripartite talks are planned in Abu Dhabi next month. US officials say no deals will proceed while fighting continues, so any easing of sanctions remains distant.

**France student unrest:** The French government offered concessions to end weeks of student protests, according to EnterpriseAM, including replacing the Parcoursup admissions platform by 2028, up to €4 billion in school renovation loans, 15,000 more higher-education places and 500 teaching posts. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has also described the violence at the end of some demonstrations as "urban violence", and about 200 high schools were still mobilised in mid-October. The outcome will test the government's room to spend while holding the line on order.

**UK and the EU:** Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said he hopes Britain rejoins the EU within his lifetime, and named the single market and customs union as options, while framing the idea as a conversation rather than a decision. A UK-EU summit planned later this year is meant to set out the choices. A June Survation poll found 55% of voters would opt to rejoin in a new referendum.

## Regional and Gulf

**Israel election:** With about 84% of votes counted, Netanyahu's right-wing bloc stood at 54 seats, seven short of the 61 needed in the 120-seat Knesset, against 49 for Gadi Eisenkot's alliance and about 17 for Arab parties, according to Al Jazeera. Likud leads as the largest party with around 27 seats, ahead of Eisenkot's Yashar on about 22. Turnout was 77.7%, the highest since 1999. Both leaders claimed the mandate to govern, and because neither bloc reaches a majority without the Arab parties, which both have ruled out, coalition talks look difficult. The final tally is due by Friday and official results on 4 November. President Isaac Herzog will then nominate a candidate, who has up to 42 days to form a government before new elections are called. Israeli bonds and the shekel fell on the result, according to CNBC.

**Houthi strikes:** The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh on 27 October, with shrapnel damaging a building, a construction site and a vehicle and no casualties reported. Air defences also intercepted three missiles aimed at the Eastern region and four drones over the south, and on 28 October a missile launched towards Taif was shot down. The Houthis claimed hits on the Saudi defence ministry headquarters and the Ras Tanura refinery, claims that could not be verified and drew no Saudi comment. Attacks on Red Sea and Gulf energy infrastructure bear directly on Suez Canal revenue and on Egypt's import costs.

**Iran talks:** Iran was reported to be hosting security talks with Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan this week, with Qatar acting as a mediator, though details have not been published. The outcome matters for the Strait of Hormuz, through which a large share of the world's oil passes.

**Saudi commercial law:** A new 271-article Saudi commercial law allows citizens from the age of 15 to do business, and consolidates rules on contracts, accounting and negotiable instruments. It is part of Riyadh's push to widen commercial participation.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**EGX30 rebound:** The EGX30 closed Tuesday at 50,105, up 3.5% on the day and 19.8% year to date. Turnover was EGP 8.2 billion, 27.1% below the 90-day average, and international investors were the only net sellers. Misr Cement (+11.5%), Telecom Egypt (+8.0%) and Cleopatra Hospital (+8.0%) led the gainers. **Rate decision on Thursday:** The Monetary Policy Committee meets on 29 October with the overnight deposit rate at 19.00% and the lending rate at 20.00%. Annual urban inflation eased to 13.9% in September, but prices rose 1.3% month on month, up from 0.1% in August. Most analysts expect a hold, while a minority sees a hike back in play because of the weaker pound and foreign selling of local debt: foreign and regional investors net sold $1.13 billion of Egyptian debt over three sessions last week. The next scheduled meeting is on 17 December.

**Pound near 56:** The central bank quotes the dollar at EGP 56.06 to buy and 56.20 to sell, and CIB at 56.07 and 56.17. The dollar crossed EGP 55 and then 56 as portfolio investors sold local debt. A weaker pound raises import costs, which is why the rate decision is being watched so closely.

**Banque du Caire IPO:** The retail tranche of Banque du Caire's offering closes today. The state raised the stake on offer from 30% to 45%, and earlier reports put retail demand at about 1.98 times the 228.75 million shares allocated to individuals, with the indicative price range at EGP 4.82 to EGP 5.30. The EBRD and the IFC are among the cornerstone investors, and trading is expected in early November. The listing is the main test of appetite for Egyptian equity since the sell-off, and final retail coverage had not been published at the time of writing.

**MNT-Halan injection:** MNT Investments BV subscribed to 163.27 million new shares in MNT Tech Holding at EGP 24.50, about EGP 4 billion, raising issued capital from EGP 160 million to EGP 176.3 million. The parent's stake rises to 80.47% from 78.5%. Because the October IPO was fully secondary, this is the first fresh capital to reach the company. Founder Mounir Nakhla said the rest of the proceeds will fund the Turkish business and an acquisition in an unnamed Arabic-speaking market.

**Tanmiya Capital Ventures:** Fund II closed at $130 million, $10 million above target, with backing from the EIB, the Egyptian-American Enterprise Fund, the EBRD, the IFC and MSMEDA. ## Worth Knowing

**Why a weaker currency raises prices even when dollars are available:** The cost of an imported good is its foreign price multiplied by the exchange rate, plus fees, and each step compounds. Take a shipment of cooking oil invoiced at $100,000. At EGP 52 to the dollar it costs EGP 5.2 million at the border. At EGP 56 it costs EGP 5.6 million, a rise of about 7.7% before anything else changes. Add a bank sourcing commission of 0.25% (EGP 14,000) and demurrage of $150 per container per day, and the importer's cost per unit rises further.

This is why accountants separate the transaction rate from the reporting rate. A company that buys on credit and pays 60 days later records a gain or loss on that gap if the pound moves in between: payables owed in dollars grow in pound terms when the currency weakens. Hedging with forward contracts fixes the rate in advance at a cost, which is why many importers instead price goods at the rate on the day of entry, as importers described this week, and pass any further moves to customers.

For investors the lesson is to ask what share of a company's costs are in dollars and what share of its revenue is. Exporters and businesses with dollar income gain from a weaker pound, while importers and companies with dollar debt are squeezed. Reading the foreign-exchange note in a company's accounts is often more informative than the headline profit figure.
