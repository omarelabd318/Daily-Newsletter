## Egypt News

**Importers, port costs and prices:** Banks are supplying dollars to importers, but with the greenback trading above EGP 56, costs remain heavy. Ahmed El Malwani of the Federation of Egyptian Chambers of Commerce says banks charge sourcing commissions of 0.1% to 0.25%. Mohamed El Arjawy puts storage and demurrage at roughly $150 per container per day and calls for provisional release of containers and for dropping advance cargo information requirements on transit cargo. Customs data cited by Al Borsa show more than $65 billion of goods cleared since January, with under $5 billion still waiting. Food manufacturers say they are holding prices, while traders are reported to be marking up cheese and some edible oils. Supply Minister Sherif Farouk told the Senate that the price-cut initiative covers 30 basic commodities, with reductions of up to about 25% across 284 outlets. The gap between factory-gate and shelf prices is the key variable for household budgets.

**Rate decision on Thursday:** The Monetary Policy Committee meets on 29 October with the deposit rate at 19% and the lending rate at 20%. Urban inflation eased to 13.9% in September from 14.5% in August, and core inflation stands at 14.1%. Monthly prices still rose 1.3%, and food moved faster. The central bank's fourth-quarter target is 7% (plus or minus 2 points). A hold is the consensus: a cut risks weakening a pound already under pressure, while the deposit rate sits about five points above headline inflation, which keeps portfolio inflows attractive. Any easing is more likely in December or early 2027. Net international reserves stood at about $57.3 billion at the end of September, and the IMF's final review of the $8 billion programme is expected in the fourth quarter.

**Parliament and Saudi Arabia:** The House of Representatives approved President Sisi's request in a closed session on 26 October, authorising "necessary defensive measures" in support of Saudi Arabia, including the possible deployment of Egyptian forces. A combat mission abroad requires two-thirds parliamentary approval after consultation with the National Defence Council. Sisi has said the support will not include attacks on Yemen, and the authorisation does not confirm that troops have been sent. The move raises Egypt's profile in Gulf security, and its economic ties with Riyadh, a major investor, are part of the backdrop.

**One million homes:** Madbouly announced the four-year programme at Egypt Forum 2026, covering social, middle-income, rental, rent-to-own and factory-worker housing at an estimated cost of EGP 1 trillion, with private developers expected to take part. Allocations, locations and financing structures have not been announced. Separately, applications for 25,000 units (15,000 through the Social Housing and Mortgage Finance Fund and 10,000 rent-to-own units) close today.

**Oil storage and the import bill:** Speaking at Egypt Forum 2026, Sisi said the state has built storage for 33 million barrels but lacks about $2.5 billion to fill it, and that Egypt spends about $3 billion a month on petroleum purchases. The state cleared $6.1 billion of arrears to oil companies in June. A 16-million-barrel strategic crude target by year-end is reportedly costed at $1.36 billion, and the Al Hamra expansion is set to lift storage to 5.3 million barrels by December. With Brent above $110, the cost of buying fuel is among the largest pressures on the budget and on the pound.

**Wheat, solar and logistics:** State wheat deliveries rose 17% to 5 million tonnes, according to the Ministry of Supply, and total wheat imports reached about 9.03 million tonnes in January to September, up 1.9%, with Russia and Ukraine supplying around 84% in the first eight months. Financing terms of $650 million were signed for a 1 GW solar plant with 200 MWh of battery storage in Nag Hammadi, Qena. The Cabinet also approved a state vehicle to build a dry port in Sadat City, and Egypt's customs system goes fully paperless on the Nafeza platform on 29 October.

## Worth Knowing

**Real interest rates and the carry trade:** A headline rate says little until inflation is subtracted. A deposit paying 19% while inflation runs at 13.9% delivers a real return of about 4.5%, calculated as (1.19 / 1.139) minus 1. That gap is what draws foreign investors into Egyptian treasury bills, a flow known as the carry trade.

*Example:* An investor holds EGP 1,000,000 in a one-year instrument yielding 19%, ending with EGP 1,190,000. If prices rise 13.9%, the same basket costs EGP 1,139,000, so purchasing power grows by about 4.5%. A foreign investor converting dollars to pounds also carries currency risk. At EGP 50 per dollar, $20,000 buys EGP 1,000,000. If the pound weakens to EGP 56 by maturity, the EGP 1,190,000 converts to about $21,250, a 6.3% dollar return instead of 19%. The yield cushions currency weakness only up to a point, which is why central banks hesitate to cut rates when the pound is under pressure.

**What to watch:** Real rates turn negative when inflation outruns the deposit rate, which pushes savers toward gold, property or dollars. A positive real rate, as at present, protects savers and supports the pound.
