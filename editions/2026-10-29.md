## Egypt News

**One million homes:** Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced at the close of Egypt Forum 2026 a roughly four-year national housing programme of about one million units, with estimated investment of EGP 1 trillion, or about EGP 1 million per unit. It is to be delivered with the private sector and would cover social and middle-income housing, rental apartments, rent-to-own homes and accommodation for factory workers. The split between categories, the financing and the timetable have not been published. For households, the numbers matter less than the unit prices, rents and eligibility rules, none of which have been set.

**Golden licences for wind:** The Cabinet granted golden licences to two 900 MW wind projects, 1.8 GW in total, both selling power to the Egyptian Electricity Transmission Company under 25-year purchase agreements. Shadwan, developed by Scatec at Ras Shokeir, is due for completion in March 2028. Shokeir Wind in the Gulf of Suez, owned by Engie (35%), Aeolus and Toyota Tsusho (40%) and Orascom Construction (25%), is due in November 2028. The projects add to a pipeline meant to cut the gas Egypt burns for power.

> **Explained: golden licence.** A single approval that lets an investor in a strategic project skip most of the separate permits a project would normally need, in order to speed up construction.

**Crude imports on credit:** The Oil Ministry plans to import about 15 million barrels of crude in the fourth quarter, 8% more than a year earlier, at an estimated $1.4 billion to $1.5 billion, according to EnterpriseAM. Supplies come from Saudi Arabia, Libya and Greece, with the state oil company paying on short- and medium-term credit. Deliveries are back-loaded: 6 million barrels in October and 4.5 million in each of November and December. Refinery utilisation has risen above 80%, from 66%.

**New credit-check rule:** Under a Financial Regulatory Authority rule taking effect in April 2027, consumer finance companies must check an I-Score credit score before approving financing. The rule widens the role of credit bureau data in lending decisions for households that rely on instalment plans.

**Clocks go back tonight:** Summer time ends overnight. Some local banks may pause internet banking, e-wallets, apps and ATMs around midnight during the changeover, so urgent transfers are better made earlier in the day.

## Top World Headlines

**Fed holds rates:** The Federal Reserve kept its target range at 3.75% to 4% on Wednesday, by a 10-2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favour of a quarter-point hike. The statement upgraded growth from "solid" to "strong", said inflation "remains elevated" and dropped wording that tied policy to a return to 2% inflation. Chair Warsh said conditions are tighter but declined to call them "tight". Traders put the odds of a hike on 9 December at roughly 85%. For Egypt, a firm dollar and costly global borrowing keep pressure on the pound and on foreign appetite for local debt.

**Fed calendar change:** Warsh announced that one 2027 meeting will be devoted to strategy, with no rate vote and no policy statement, a departure from the usual eight-meeting rhythm of decisions.

**Ukraine talks:** Zelenskyy said at a briefing in The Hague that US-Ukraine-Russia talks could be held in Abu Dhabi in early November, with technical teams meeting on 6 to 10 November, and cautioned that the venue could change. Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said Putin agreed "in principle" to resume negotiations after 10 November. Zelenskyy also renewed his call for a meeting with Putin on territorial questions, and expects Washington to give more clarity after next week's midterm elections.

**US midterms:** The US votes on Tuesday 3 November in midterm elections, with the Fed's independence and Washington's bandwidth for the Ukraine talks both in focus. Warsh said politics played "no role" in this week's decision.

## Regional and Gulf

**Israel election:** With about 90% of ballots counted, Netanyahu's bloc held 53 seats, eight short of the 61 needed in the 120-seat Knesset, against 48 for Gadi Eisenkot's bloc and 19 for the Arab parties, according to the Times of Israel. Likud leads as the largest party with 26 seats, ahead of Eisenkot's Yashar on 21. Double-envelope ballots, which include many military votes, were still being counted and could shift seats. The Joint List leader has said his party is ready to use its weight to help remove Netanyahu. With neither bloc at 61, Israel could stay under a caretaker government during coalition talks.

**Houthi claims on Saudi Arabia:** Saudi defences have intercepted a series of Houthi missiles and drones this week, including a missile launched towards Taif. The Houthis claim to have hit the Ras Tanura refinery, a claim that has not been verified by Riyadh. The attacks affect Egypt through Red Sea shipping risk, which bears on Suez Canal revenue, and through fuel import costs.

**Libya reopens its skies:** Libya lifted international flying restrictions after 11 years, according to CairoScene. Easier air links with Libya could help Egyptian airlines and the many Egyptians who work there, though schedules and carriers have not been announced.

**Sudan eases imports:** Sudan lifted import bans on 44 of 46 goods and removed duties on eight food staples, according to EnterpriseAM. Egypt is Sudan's largest supplier, so Egyptian exporters of food and manufactured goods stand to gain if the changes hold.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**EGX30 slips:** The EGX30 closed Wednesday at 49,281, down 1.6% on turnover of EGP 6.6 billion, 40.8% below its 90-day average. It is up 17.8% for the year but 5.0% lower for the month, and local investors were the only net buyers. Telecom Egypt (-3.4%), Palm Hills (-3.0%) and Talaat Moustafa (-3.0%) fell, the last two to their lowest since April, while Elsewedy Electric gained 3.2%. Commercial International Bank closed at its lowest since 31 March.

**Rate decision today:** The Monetary Policy Committee decides today with the overnight deposit rate at 19.00% and the lending rate at 20.00%. Most analysts expect a hold, one forecasts a hike and none expect a cut this year. Foreign and regional investors sold $1.13 billion of local debt last week. The next meeting is on 17 December.

**Pound holds above 56:** The central bank quotes the dollar at EGP 56.06 to buy and 56.20 to sell, and CIB at 56.07 and 56.17. The dollar is within 0.3% of its 52-week high against the pound and about 17.8% higher since the start of the year. A weaker pound raises import costs, which is why the rate decision is watched closely.

**Banque du Caire IPO:** The retail tranche closed about 27 times oversubscribed, with retail investors ordering roughly 9.29 billion shares against 343.1 million offered, according to EnterpriseAM. The price is EGP 5.30 and allocation is pro rata, so applicants receive about 3.7% of their orders. Banque Misr upsized the stake on offer from 30% to 45%, and the IFC and EBRD joined four foreign cornerstone investors who had committed up to $256 million. Retail buyers can sell back at the offer price for 30 days after trading starts, expected on Monday 2 November.

> **Explained: stabilisation mechanism.** A sell-back option that lets small investors return shares at the offer price for a set period, limiting their loss if the price falls after listing.

**Next state listings:** Alamal Alsharif for Plastics, 54.5% owned by state banks, filed to list on the main market, with a notice period through 2 November. The government aims to float or sell stakes in at least 20 state companies before year-end, linked to the IMF programme that concludes in December. Misr Life Insurance, El Nasr Glass and Yayat are also expected to list.

**Investment funds:** Net assets of Egypt's investment funds rose 7.16% in the third quarter to EGP 504.71 billion across 232 funds, according to EnterpriseAM. Retail investors hold about 79.8% of fund certificates. Equity funds returned an average 5.55% in the quarter, against 13.03% for index funds and 2.79% for the EGX30.

**Solar-storage and gas deals:** Nefer Minya, a 1.2 GWp solar plant with 600 MWh of storage, closed a $566 million debt package led by the EBRD, with commercial operation due in September 2027. Eni signed drilling and construction contracts for Cyprus's Cronos field, with first gas targeted in the first half of 2028 and processing through Zohr and liquefaction at Damietta for export to Europe. Apache plans five Western Desert wells in the first quarter of 2027 at about $30 million, adding roughly 70 million cubic feet a day.

**Pharma capital increases:** Holdipharma subsidiary Memphis Pharma will lift its capital to EGP 700 million from EGP 227.5 million, and Nile Pharma will double its capital to EGP 1 billion.

**Oil, gas and bitcoin:** Brent rose 0.5% to $114.40 a barrel, about 3% below its 52-week high, with Hormuz shipping attacks and Houthi strikes on Saudi targets sustaining the risk premium. For Egypt, which imports fuel and wheat, dearer energy and a strong dollar are the main external pressures, and a possible Fed hike in December would add to them.

## Worth Knowing

**Why a 27-times oversubscribed IPO gives small investors only a sliver:** When demand far exceeds the shares on offer, allocation is usually pro rata: each applicant receives the same fraction of what was ordered, and the unused money is refunded.

Take Banque du Caire's retail tranche. About 343.1 million shares were offered and about 9.29 billion ordered, so each applicant received roughly 3.7% of their request. An investor who ordered 100,000 shares at EGP 5.30, a payment of EGP 530,000, would receive about 3,700 shares worth about EGP 19,600, and the rest of the payment would come back.

Two lessons follow. First, oversubscription is a sign of demand, not a promise of a first-day gain, because the price on listing depends on the market on the day. Second, inflating an order to win a larger allocation ties up cash for no benefit if everyone does the same. The 30-day sell-back at the offer price reduces the downside for those who do receive shares, but it protects only the amount allocated.
