## Egypt News

**IMF final review:** An IMF mission has arrived in Cairo to start the eighth and last review of Egypt's $8 billion Extended Fund Facility, together with the final review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility, according to EnterpriseAM. Completing both could release about $2.3 billion, roughly $1.5 billion and $800 million respectively. The review is starting weeks earlier than had been expected, and the programme was due to conclude in December. The outcome matters for the pound and for the state's plan to sell stakes in companies before year-end.

**Old-rent housing and power-bill relief:** The Cabinet extended the deadline for old-rent tenants to apply for alternative housing to 12 January 2027, according to CairoScene. The same meeting approved a 30% cut in electricity consumption charges for coded-meter users for six months and a review of social assistance for residents of properties affected by Ring Road works. Eligibility criteria and the application route were not published in the reports.

**Authorisation for Saudi defence:** Parliament approved on 26 October a request from President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi to authorise defensive measures in support of Saudi Arabia, including the possible deployment of Egyptian forces, according to CairoScene. The vote was held in closed session after attacks on Saudi territory attributed to the Houthis, and the authorisation does not by itself show that troops have been sent.

**Patient safety strategy:** The Health Ministry launched a national quality and patient safety strategy for 2026 to 2030 aimed at cutting medical errors and raising standards, according to CairoScene. Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar called for a confidential nationwide system for reporting errors, focused on learning rather than punishment. A medical liability and patient safety law is already in place, the ministry's first patient safety report for 2025 will serve as a baseline, and a diploma in patient safety run with the World Health Organization has enrolled 70 doctors.

**Desalination alliance:** A national alliance linking researchers, government bodies, financiers and businesses was launched to advance desalination for food production, according to CairoScene. Water scarcity is one of the main limits on expanding farmland in Egypt, so cheaper desalination would matter for food supply.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**Rate hike surprises analysts:** The Monetary Policy Committee raised all key rates by 100 basis points on Thursday, taking the overnight deposit rate to 20.00% and the lending rate to 21.00%, with the main operation and discount rates at 20.50%. It is the first increase since March 2024. Most analysts polled by EnterpriseAM had expected a hold, and the decision came days after the dollar crossed EGP 56 at the central bank. The committee cited challenging external conditions and elevated uncertainty and said the move aims to keep expectations anchored and protect the downward path of inflation.

> **Explained: basis point.** One hundredth of a percentage point, so a 100-basis-point rise lifts a rate by one full percentage point, for example from 19% to 20%.

**EGX30 and the pound:** The EGX30 closed Thursday at 49,596, up about 0.6% from Wednesday's 49,281 and 18.6% higher for the year. The decision came after a week in which the EGX70 fell 11.07% in a single session on Sunday, and the benchmark touched fresh lows. Abu Qir Fertilizers fell 3.5% and Telecom Egypt 1.7%, while Alexandria Containers gained 1.5% and Juhayna 1.3%. EFG Holding closed within 0.3% of its 52-week low. The pound's market mid-rate eased 0.3% to EGP 56.04 per dollar, still within 0.6% of its 52-week high for the dollar, and the previous record before this month was EGP 54.73 on 7 April. The Egyptian Exchange is closed until Sunday, so the market's verdict on the hike comes then.

**Banque du Caire listing:** The state lender's retail tranche closed about 27 times covered, with orders for roughly 9.29 billion shares against 343.1 million offered, so allocations are pro rata and applicants receive about 3.7% of their requests, according to EnterpriseAM. The bank has received the Cabinet's final approval for the sale of a 30% to 45% stake, and shares are expected to start trading on Monday 2 November. Retail investors can sell back at the EGP 5.30 offer price for 30 days after trading starts.

**Enppi IPO:** The government aims to list the state-owned engineering contractor Enppi in the second half of January, priced in dollars, according to EnterpriseAM. The state would sell 10% to 20%, and the Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation, which holds 96.61%, would keep roughly 77% to 87%. **MNT-Halan:** The parent company's EGP 4 billion capital injection into its Egyptian unit is fully paid, with 163.27 million shares bought at EGP 24.50, lifting issued capital to EGP 176.3 million from EGP 160 million, according to EnterpriseAM.

## Worth Knowing

**Nominal versus real interest rates:** A headline interest rate says how fast money grows, not how much more it can buy. The real rate subtracts inflation, and the exact formula is (1 + nominal rate) divided by (1 + inflation) minus 1.

Take an illustrative saver with EGP 100,000 in a one-year deposit at 20%. After a year the balance is EGP 120,000. If prices rose 14% over the same period, the same basket of goods that cost EGP 100,000 now costs EGP 114,000, so the saver's purchasing power rose by about 5.3%, not 20%. Had the deposit paid 19%, the real gain would have been about 4.4%. A 100-basis-point rise in rates therefore adds about 0.9 percentage points of real return when inflation is unchanged.

Two lessons follow. First, the rate that matters for a saver or an investor is the one after inflation, so a hike aimed at lowering inflation can raise real returns twice, once through the higher rate and again if inflation falls. Second, the same arithmetic works for borrowers: a loan at 21% with inflation at 14% costs about 6% a year in real terms, and higher rates make new credit for homes, cars and businesses dearer, which is how a central bank slows demand.
