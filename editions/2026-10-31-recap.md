*Week of 25 to 31 October 2026: a surprise rate hike, a weaker pound, Egypt's Saudi authorisation, an inconclusive Israeli election and a technology rout on Wall Street.*

## The Week in Brief

1. **Rate hike:** The Central Bank of Egypt raised its key rates by 100 basis points on 29 October, a surprise to most analysts, taking the overnight deposit rate to 20% and the lending rate to 21%.
2. **Pound:** The dollar climbed past EGP 56, a record, and ended the week at about EGP 56.12 on the central bank's selling rate.
3. **Parliament's vote:** Parliament approved on 26 October a request to authorise military support for Saudi Arabia against Houthi attacks, with no deployment announced.
4. **IMF:** A mission arrived in Cairo to begin the final review of Egypt's $8 billion programme, weeks ahead of schedule.
5. **EGX30:** The index fell about 2.9% over the week to 49,596.14, and the exchange reopens on Sunday after the rate hike.
6. **Iran and the Gulf:** Tanker strikes in Hormuz and Houthi claims against Saudi sites kept Brent above $113 while mediators shuttled to Tehran.
7. **Israel:** The election ended without a majority for either bloc, leaving weeks of coalition bargaining.
8. **Wall Street:** The Nasdaq lost about 2% on the week and 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025, as doubts over AI spending hit Alphabet and chipmakers.
9. **Ebola:** The outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo passed 5,000 deaths.
10. **Football:** Pyramids led the Egyptian league while Al Ahly, under pressure, scraped a 1-0 win.

## Egypt This Week

**Rate hike:** The Monetary Policy Committee lifted the overnight deposit rate to 20% and the lending rate to 21%, with the main operation and discount rates at 20.5%, according to EnterpriseAM. It was the first increase since March 2024 and went against most analysts polled, who had expected a hold. The bank cited "challenging external conditions and elevated uncertainty" and the aim of anchoring inflation expectations. Headline inflation stood at 13.9% in September, and the one-year Treasury bill yielded 26.08% at the 29 October auction. Analyst Hany Aboul Fotouh told Egypt Independent the move was meant to keep local debt attractive and limit outflows of short-term money. The effect on the currency is yet to be tested, since the exchange reopens on Sunday.

**The pound:** The dollar passed EGP 56 in banks during the week, a record for the pound, and the central bank's selling rate was EGP 56.12 on Saturday, roughly 1.5% weaker than a week earlier (unconfirmed). Importers report sourcing commissions of 0.1% to 0.25% at some banks and port storage costs near $150 per container per day, according to EnterpriseAM. Food prices are the main household exposure, and the Supply Ministry says discounts of up to about 25% cover 30 basic commodities.

**Parliament's vote:** Parliament approved on 26 October, by a two-thirds majority, a request from President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi to authorise measures in support of Saudi Arabia's defence, including the possible deployment of Egyptian forces. No deployment has been announced, officials describe any role as defensive, and Cairo has ruled out strikes on Yemen. A Houthi-run parliament speaker had urged the Egyptian chamber to reject the request.

**IMF final review:** A Fund mission arrived in Cairo to begin the eighth and last review of the $8 billion Extended Fund Facility, together with the final review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility, according to EnterpriseAM. Completion could release about $2.3 billion, a figure reported by Amwal Al Ghad on 1 October that has not been confirmed by the Fund (unconfirmed). Completion is expected by mid-December, when the programme ends.

**Energy and housing:** The Oil Ministry plans to import about 15 million barrels of crude in the fourth quarter on supplier credit, at an estimated $1.4 billion to $1.5 billion, according to EnterpriseAM. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly announced a roughly four-year programme of about one million housing units, with estimated investment of EGP 1 trillion. The Cabinet granted golden licences to two 900 MW wind projects, 1.8 GW in total, and approved a 30% cut in electricity consumption charges for coded-meter users for six months.

**Russia and El-Dabaa:** Sisi met Vladimir Putin in Moscow on 28 October on the sidelines of the Russia-Africa Summit. Sisi reaffirmed Egypt's commitment to the El-Dabaa nuclear plant's timetable and payments and raised new reactors after 2030. Rosatom said construction remains on schedule and that the "sabotage" its chief had mentioned meant misinformation campaigns.

## Region and World

**Houthis and Saudi Arabia:** The Houthis claimed six attacks on Saudi airports and oil sites on 29 and 30 October, including the Ras Tanura and Rabigh refineries, with no independent corroboration. The Saudi-led coalition said it destroyed two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh, and shrapnel wounded one citizen. The attacks followed talks in Tehran among Iran, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry stressed opening negotiating channels. For Egypt, the Red Sea risk bears on Suez Canal revenue and fuel import costs.

**Iran and Hormuz:** Two tankers were hit in the Strait of Hormuz within 24 hours, one by a naval mine and one by explosives attached to its hull. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said four tankers struck mines on an unauthorised route. President Trump said the war, now in its ninth month, would end soon, and Pakistan's army said it was "cautiously optimistic" about mediation as Qatari officials returned to Tehran. No ceasefire has been agreed.

**Israel's election:** With the count complete, Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc held 53 seats and the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot 48, with 19 for Arab parties, against 61 needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Results are due to be certified before coalition talks begin. Eisenkot has ruled out an alliance with Arab-led parties, yet analysts say the opposition cannot govern without them, so weeks of bargaining are likely.

**Gaza, Sudan and Lebanon:** Israeli strikes continued in Gaza despite the ceasefire, and the territory's Health Ministry urged the opening of the Rafah crossing for patients, according to Al Jazeera. In Sudan, senior Rapid Support Forces commander Siddiq Ander announced his defection, accusing the leadership of nepotism. A Lebanese military court charged 13 people linked to Hezbollah over a seized truck carrying 780kg of explosives.

**Brazil:** Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term, defeating Flávio Bolsonaro by more than four million votes, and the real fell about 12% against the dollar in spot trading afterwards, according to Al Jazeera. Bolsonaro left Brazil and landed in Miami on Wednesday after the Supreme Court ordered him to surrender his passport. Trump called the election a "disgrace".

**Ukraine and the Ebola outbreak:** The US approved the possible sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 missiles to Ukraine for about $2.1 billion, though deliveries are not expected until the 2030s, according to Al Jazeera. Zelenskyy said US-Ukraine-Russia technical talks could be held in Abu Dhabi from 6 to 10 November, and Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said Putin agreed in principle to resume negotiations after 10 November. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Ebola deaths passed 5,000 and infections 10,000 since May, with no approved treatment or vaccine for the Bundibugyo strain; Nigeria, Kenya and Uganda have also reported cases.

## Markets This Week

The week belonged to rates and risk aversion. The surprise Egyptian hike came as the pound weakened and foreign money left the Treasury bill market, while the EGX30 slid on Sunday to a fresh low and recovered only part of the loss. On Wall Street, Alphabet's shares fell 12.6% on 29 October, their worst day on record, wiping out about $500 billion of value, according to NBC News, and the selloff in chipmakers deepened on Friday. Gold dropped to a one-year low as higher oil and Fed tightening bets outweighed its safe-haven appeal, according to Kitco.

## Sports This Week

**Al Ahly 1-0 Modern Sport:** Goalkeeper Mostafa Shobeir saved a late penalty to protect the lead in round eight. Coach Hussein Ammouta faces calls from supporters for his dismissal after a 3-3 draw with El Qanah, the club's second straight league game without a win, and management says it backs "technical stability". The CAF tie against Raja Casablanca follows on 18 December and 8 January.

**Egyptian Premier League:** Pyramids led with 21 points from seven wins in seven after a 4-1 win over Tala'ea El Gaish. Zamalek beat Al Mokawloon Al Arab 2-1 on 28 October to reach 19 points, second in the table.

**Liverpool v Chelsea:** Liverpool won 6-5 on penalties after a 1-1 draw at Anfield to reach the Carabao Cup quarter-finals, in Xabi Alonso's first return as Chelsea manager. Everton beat Newcastle 3-1 to reach the last eight.

**PSG 5-1 Le Havre:** Achraf Hakimi scored the first hat-trick of his career and set up two more goals as Paris Saint-Germain came from behind. In Saudi Arabia, Cristiano Ronaldo scored a hat-trick for Al Nassr against Al Khuloud.

**Zverev and Djokovic:** Alexander Zverev becomes world number one on Monday, the second German to do so after Boris Becker, after reaching the Vienna semi-finals. Novak Djokovic lost to Jan-Lennard Struff in Basel, which dents his hopes of a record 19th ATP Finals.

**Africa Cup of Nations:** The Confederation of African Football dropped its plan for a four-year cycle and restored the biennial format for the tournament.

## Culture and Tech

**Binance theft:** Hackers stole at least $6.3 billion from Binance's wallets, which would make it the largest known crypto theft, and security firms TRM Labs and Elliptic attribute it to North Korean hackers, according to TechCrunch. Binance suspended deposits and withdrawals and says user funds are safe, with about $3 billion frozen by industry partners. Bitcoin slid below $77,000.

**AI spending doubts:** Alphabet's weaker search advertising and its plan to spend more than $200 billion this year drove the week's selloff in technology shares, and investors are questioning how long AI investment will take to pay back.

**Shakira at the Pyramids:** Shakira's concert at the Giza Pyramids, planned for 28 November, has been postponed to 2 April 2027, citing "the current circumstances in the region".

**Egyptian start-ups:** BY Venture Partners closed a $60 million third fund for AI start-ups, and Egypt's Raya Ventures made its first investment, in UK AI firm Pidima. SubwayTakes creator Kareem Rahma won an Instagram Rings Award.

## How It Played Out

**Federal Reserve decision:** A hold was widely expected, and the Fed kept its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% on 28 October by a 10-2 vote, with two members dissenting in favour of a quarter-point rise, according to Kitco. Markets now price a possible December increase after third-quarter growth of 3.4% and September core PCE inflation of 3.1%.

**Banque du Caire retail offering:** The retail book closed about 27 times covered, with orders for roughly 9.29 billion shares against 343.1 million offered, so applicants receive about 3.7% of their orders. The offer price is EGP 5.30 and trading is expected to begin on Monday 2 November.

**Alphabet and Big Tech results:** Investors were watching how far cloud growth justifies planned AI spending. Alphabet's revenue and advertising sales missed expectations and its shares suffered their worst one-day fall on record.

**Chelsea's early lead:** Morgan Rogers put Chelsea ahead from the penalty spot before Cody Gakpo equalised, as the sides' meeting at Anfield was settled by a shoot-out.

**Clocks going back:** Egypt's summer time ended overnight on 29 October, and some banks paused online services around midnight during the changeover.
