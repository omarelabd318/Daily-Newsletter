## Egypt News

**First locally assembled EV:** El Nasr Automotive began producing the MAGE EV, the electric version of China's Dongfeng Mage, at its Helwan plant, with local content put at 45%, per a Cabinet statement. The car is built by SN Automotive, a joint venture between the state-owned El Nasr and Al Safy Group, Dongfeng's local agent, formed in 2024 with $40 million of initial investment. Industry Minister Khaled Hashem said the next stage is to raise the local share and make EV components, batteries and motors in Egypt. The government is also studying customs exemptions on production inputs for EV factories. Batteries now carry 5% duty and motors 2%, while fully imported EVs enter at 0% customs, a gap that favours importers over local assemblers. The measure may go to parliament in November in a customs-law package.

**Fuel monitoring pilot:** The Petroleum Ministry is launching an electronic system to track fuel distribution, starting with a one-month pilot in Port Said. It draws on electronic stickers issued by Interior Ministry traffic departments to follow fuel from distribution to end user and flag abnormal consumption, in a bid to curb diversion and smuggling. The ministry says prices and quantities dispensed will not change. Vehicle owners without stickers get the trial month to comply, and coverage will extend to all stations in the governorate.

**Industrial permits simplified:** From 1 November, the Industrial Development Authority approves architectural drawings and building permits first, and the Decennial Insurance Pool reviews structural designs only afterwards, a sequence meant to shorten permit times. A 90% cut in the estimated construction cost used for insurance requirements exempts most small and medium industrial establishments from submitting policies.

**Skilled workers for Germany:** Egypt ranks sixth worldwide for applications to have professional qualifications recognised in Germany, with applications up nearly 90% in 2025, a German embassy official told the Labour Mobility Forum in Cairo. Egypt's foreign ministry signed a memorandum with Germany's Federal Employment Agency on organised mobility of skilled workers, and a letter of intent with Siemens Healthineers to train radiology technicians. The remittances and skills that returning workers bring are a growing part of the case for legal migration routes.

**Oil find in the Eastern Desert:** Petronefertiti, a venture under state-owned Ganope backed by Dana Petroleum and KNOC, struck oil and gas at the Lorcan-6 exploratory well in North Zeit Bay, producing 1,500 barrels a day of oil and 8 million cubic feet a day of associated gas in testing, according to an Oil Ministry statement. Test flows are an early indicator rather than output: the oil rate is about 0.3% of the roughly 527,000 barrels a day Egypt produces. The well is one of 101 exploration wells planned for 2026 under a $1.3 billion programme.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**Rate hike aftermath:** Banks begin reviewing savings and lending rates today after the Monetary Policy Committee raised the overnight deposit rate to 20.00% and the lending rate to 21.00% on Thursday, its first increase since March 2024. Variable-rate certificates, including the National Bank of Egypt's Platinum and Banque Misr's Al Qimma, rose by the same point. The central bank cited monthly inflation: headline prices rose 1.3% in September after 0.1% in August, even as annual headline inflation eased to 13.9% and core to 14.1%, and it pushed its 7% (±2 points) target date back to the fourth quarter of 2027. The statement did not name the pound, which crossed 55 to the dollar on 22 October and sold at EGP 56.12 at the central bank on Thursday, within 0.4% of its 52-week high. Interest payments absorb about 60% of central government revenue, and foreign investors net sold EGP 115.1 billion of Treasury bills and bonds in the week of 18 to 22 October, according to the EGX's weekly report.

> **Explained: preemptive hike.** Raising rates before a feared price rise shows up in the data, to anchor expectations rather than react to inflation that has already happened.

**EGX30 and the Treasury bill test:** The EGX30 closed Thursday at 49,596.14, up 0.6% on turnover of EGP 7.5 billion, 32.8% below its 90-day average, with local investors the only net sellers. The index is up 18.6% for the year and 4.4% lower in October. Beltone Holding gained 5.8%, Alexandria Mineral Oils 4.9% and Orascom Development 3.7%, while Abu Qir Fertilizers fell 3.5% and MOPCO 2.5%. EFG Holding sits within 0.3% of its 52-week low. The 29 October auction cleared the one-year bill at 26.08% and the six-month bill at 25.46%, with bids about twice the amount sold, so the Finance Ministry accepted only part of the demand. Today's EGP 110 billion of 91- and 273-day bills and Monday's EGP 27 billion of bonds show whether foreign money returns after the hike.

**Giza hotel loan:** ADD Properties, the hospitality arm of Sami Saad Holding, lined up a $265 million syndicated loan for a Grand Hyatt overlooking the Grand Egyptian Museum. The EBRD takes the largest share at $127.5 million, alongside the IFC at $60 million, Emirates NBD Egypt at $40 million and the National Bank of Egypt at $37.5 million, with EFG Hermes pricing the deal.

**Qalaa, Grova and Maridive:** Qalaa Holdings shareholders approved an EGP 3.87 billion capital increase that funds a move to 27.1% of the Egyptian Refining Company from 13%, according to an EGX filing. Hassan Allam's Grova SPAC opens an EGP 90 million private placement running to 5 November, raising its capital to EGP 100 million, the Financial Regulatory Authority said. **Qatar's Al Mana:** Investment Minister Mohamed Farid met the chairperson of Qatar's Al Mana Holding Group in Doha to discuss expanding its existing Egyptian projects, one of several Gulf investment conversations that help foreign currency supply.

## Worth Knowing

**Real interest rates:** A nominal interest rate says how much money a saver earns. The real rate subtracts inflation and says how much purchasing power the saver gains, which is what matters for deciding whether to save, borrow or invest.

Take an illustrative saver who puts EGP 100,000 into a deposit paying 20% a year while prices rise 13.9%. After a year the account holds EGP 120,000, but goods that cost EGP 100,000 now cost EGP 113,900. The saver can buy about 5.4% more than before, close to the 6.1-point gap between the two rates, which is the approximate real rate. If inflation instead reached 25%, the same deposit would lose purchasing power.

This is why central banks watch the real rate. The deposit rate sits 6.1 points above September's headline inflation after the hike, up from 5.1 points, which makes holding pounds more attractive and borrowing dearer. For a government, a high real rate also raises the cost of financing debt, and investors judge it by comparing it with where they expect inflation to be, not where it has been.
