## Egypt News

**Banque du Caire debut:** The state-owned lender made a quiet start on the EGX on Monday, closing 0.19% higher at EGP 5.31 against an offer price of EGP 5.30, after touching EGP 5.68 in the opening minutes and dipping as low as EGP 5.25. A senior government official said the Finance Ministry will take up to half of the EGP 36.37 billion (about $658 million) raised, as much as EGP 18.2 billion, to ease public debt pressures, and that the Cabinet has approved the same split for state offerings more broadly. Banque Misr sold 45% of the bank, and demand was strong, with institutional orders of about EGP 205.8 billion and a retail tranche covered 27 times. Prime Minister Moustafa Madbouly attended the opening bell. The listing matters because it is the state's first privatisation by IPO in two years and arrives as the IMF programme heads into its final review before it expires on 15 December; state asset sales are among its requirements. Pricing at fair value left little room for a first-day pop, one asset manager said.

**Inflation forecast raised:** JPMorgan lifted its Egypt year-end inflation forecast to 14.3% from 13.4%, as the pound's slide feeds into prices, according to a research note cited by Asharq Business. The bank expects October's reading at 13.8%, against 13.9% in September, held down by a high base from last year, with the climb showing in the November and December prints. It reads the central bank's 100 basis point hike on 29 October, which lifted the overnight deposit rate to 20% and the lending rate to 21%, as an attempt to buy time, and expects no cuts before the second half of 2027. For households, higher inflation with steeper borrowing costs means pricier loans and little relief on shop-shelf prices before next year.

**Tax sukuk pilot:** The Finance Ministry will launch Egypt's first tax-backed sukuk this month as a pilot, Mae Adel, the finance minister's deputy for public debt management, said. Taxpayers will subscribe through bank branches for up to 20-25% of the previous year's tax bill, earning a tax-free floating return set at the central bank's average deposit rate minus one percentage point, about 19% against the 20% deposit rate. The sukuk runs for six months to match the April filing season and will not trade on the EGX until the second half of 2027, once the ownership-transfer system is tested. Only taxpayers with three clean years of filings and no evasion record qualify. The pilot is far smaller than the EGP 50-100 billion floated earlier, which signals caution about appetite.

> **Explained: tax-backed sukuk.** A certificate that taxpayers buy and later use to settle their tax bills, with the return itself exempt from tax, so the state borrows from the people who will pay it back in taxes.

**Carbon border levy:** The government is again negotiating with Brussels for a phased timetable under the EU's carbon border mechanism as it nears full application, two senior officials told EnterpriseAM. One scenario would have exporters absorb part of the cost in importing markets, but that needs a domestic carbon price and verified plant-level emissions data that officials say do not yet exist. Environment Ministry studies put the annual cost to local manufacturers at about $317 million, 74% of it falling on steel. The World Bank estimates real income could shrink by about 0.6% without adaptation. Any home-market carbon cost risks feeding into prices at a time when inflation is rising again.

**Spending cuts and stockpiles:** Prime Minister Madbouly told the Central Crisis Management Committee that Egypt will expand strategic reserves and tighten government spending because of regional tensions. The Finance and Planning ministries are finalising a framework that would limit state fuel and electricity use and cut advertising, while protecting funding for strategic commodities, petroleum, electricity and basic services. Supply Minister Sherif Farouk said strategic commodity reserves average about six months of supply. Stock quantities were not disclosed.

**World Bank financing:** Egypt signed a $1 billion financing agreement with the World Bank under the second phase of its reform programme, covering private-sector growth, fiscal reforms and the green transition. The money lands as the government leans on external partners while it borrows heavily at home.

**Shams El-Senaa solar drive:** A new initiative targets 1,000 MW of solar capacity to power 7,000 factories, aiming to cut industrial energy costs. Details on financing and timing were not given in the report.

## Worth Knowing

**Why a "fair-value" IPO barely moves:** Banque du Caire's shares closed 0.19% above their offer price, and that is no failure. When a seller prices a company at its independently assessed fair value, new buyers get no discount for taking the risk, so the stock tends to hover near the offer price until earnings change the picture. A cheaper issue leaves a cushion that can produce a first-day jump, but the seller, here the state, raises less.

Take a simple example. A bank is valued at EGP 80.83 billion at an offer price of EGP 5.30 a share. At about 5 times earnings and 1.2 times book value, it trades close to the sector's multiples. Priced at a 10% discount, the offer would be EGP 4.77, a buyer would hold an instant paper gain if the market valued it at fair value, and the seller would have left roughly EGP 3.6 billion on the table on a EGP 36.37 billion offering. Price-to-earnings divides the share price by profit per share, and price-to-book divides it by net assets per share, so both show what investors pay for each pound of earnings or equity.

The lesson for investors is to judge an IPO by what comes next, not by day one: earnings growth, dividends and trading volume decide whether a stock rises from here. As one asset manager put it, investors will need earnings growth to make money from here.
