## Egypt News

**EgyptAlum stake sale:** The government is looking to sell what a senior official called a large stake in Egypt Aluminum on the EGX before the end of the year, with the size pending a fair-value study, EnterpriseAM reported. The state already controls almost all of the company, and the real public float is closer to 3% than the 8% filed, by the outlet's calculation. As recently as July the state wanted the valuation to rise threefold or fourfold first; stronger earnings, a share rally and foreign appetite for metals changed the plan. The sale also serves the year-end target of $1.5 billion from the offerings programme under IMF commitments, of which the Banque du Caire listing has already delivered about $658 million.

**Gas rationing:** Gas allocations to fertiliser and methanol plants have been cut by 20% since 1 November, and Kima told the EGX its supplies fell 20% from noon on Sunday while its urea plant keeps running. The squeeze reflects a market that the International Energy Agency says will stay tight into 2027, with about 150 billion cubic metres of LNG supply lost to the regional war through 2030. Egypt has asked suppliers to deliver about 10 cargoes in November, down from 12 in October, and deferred five of the 15 contracted into 2027. The Idku plant is due to ship its first export cargo to Europe in months by the end of November. Officials expect fertiliser allocations to be restored to protect export earnings and avoid higher food costs.

**Banking agents:** The central bank approved a framework letting banks hire third-party agents outside their branch networks for a closed list of tasks, including customer identification and e-KYC checks, forwarding loan and account applications and delivering unactivated cards. Each contract needs written central bank approval and runs for up to three years, and the bank stays fully responsible. The aim is to lift financial inclusion to 80% of adults by FY 2029/30 from 76.3% last fiscal year, according to EnterpriseAM.

**Distressed factories:** The Industry Ministry is in talks with investors to take over about 30 distressed factories and is assessing 51 for restructuring, Industry Minister Khaled Hashem told the House Industry Committee. Buyers would get working capital at a reduced rate after closing. About 90 troubled plants have registered on the unified digital platform. Restarting idle capacity adds local supply and protects jobs without the cost of new construction.

**Property sales:** Contractual real estate sales reached about EGP 1.048 trillion for 64,500 units in the first nine months of 2026, according to a KW Egypt Property Intelligence report cited by CairoScene. East Cairo and the North Coast took 74.4% of sales, and Talaat Moustafa led developers with EGP 301 billion, ahead of Palm Hills at EGP 184 billion. Developers still offer down payments of 0-10% and instalments of 8-12 years, which keeps demand sensitive to borrowing costs after the latest rate hike.

**Electric vehicle plant:** Mansour Group aims to finish its electric vehicle plant in 6th of October City in the first quarter of 2027, after construction began in late 2025, Prime Minister Moustafa Madbouly was told during a progress review. The Industry Ministry calls expanding EV, battery and component manufacturing a priority.

**Martyrs fund law:** Parliament gave final approval on Monday to amendments to the law governing the Fund for Honoring Martyrs and Victims of Military and Security Operations, removing financial burdens tied to some medical services and documents and clarifying disability compensation claims.

> **Explained: floating-rate certificate.** A bank certificate whose return is set as a margin over the central bank's rate, so it rises and falls when the central bank changes rates.

## Worth Knowing

**Why savings funds can grow faster in pounds than equity funds:** Egypt's investment fund industry reached EGP 504.7 billion in September, and money market funds alone held about EGP 303 billion. Equity funds are growing faster in percentage terms, yet the bigger gains in pounds still go to money market funds. The reason is the base effect: a small percentage on a large base can outweigh a large percentage on a small one.

Take an illustrative example. A money market fund with EGP 300 billion that grows 9% adds EGP 27 billion. An equity fund category with EGP 32 billion that grows 36% adds about EGP 11.5 billion. The equity line looks four times as fast, but the money market line adds more than twice as much new money. A reader comparing growth rates should therefore also ask how large the starting base was.

Money market funds also behave like savings accounts. Rules cap their holdings at 13 months' maturity, so they favour Treasury bills, which currently yield around 26% a year. Equity funds, by contrast, hold shares for longer and carry price risk. That is why a rate rise does not drain them, and why the stock market has not yet felt the fund boom. For investors, the lesson is to compare funds by return after inflation and by risk, not by headline growth.
