## Egypt News

**Spending and reserves:** Prime Minister Moustafa Madbouly told the Central Crisis Management Committee on Monday that the Finance and Planning ministries are finalising a plan to rationalise public spending while keeping basic goods and petroleum products funded. Strategic commodity reserves cover an average of six months, with new contracts signed to add to stocks, and the state will cut its own fuel and energy use. Thirty basic commodities are sold at 15% to 25% below market prices through an expanded outlet network. The package is a response to regional tension that has raised energy and shipping costs.

**Phone fee waiver:** The government will stop charging the 5% development fee on handsets that factories in the economic and private investment zones sell into the domestic market, trading part of the fee for new investment and capacity, three officials told EnterpriseAM. The budget targets about EGP 1.9 billion from the fee this fiscal year. Fifteen brands now assemble phones in Egypt, output has reached about 10.5 million handsets against a government target of 14 to 15 million this year, and a telecoms source says local sales are down about 20% on higher prices. Imports of phone production inputs rose 37.1% to about $500 million in the first half, so whether the waiver pays off depends on how much of each phone is made locally.

**Electric vehicle target:** Egypt aims to produce 100,000 electric vehicles a year by 2032. A major foreign automaker plans to start building three models in 2027 and to lift local content from 10% to 27% by 2032, while Dongfeng has begun making EVs with SN Automotive on El Nasr Automotive's lines. The government is also working on local batteries, motors and charging.

**Arab Organization for Industrialization:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi asked that the organisation become an "industrial integration platform" joining state manufacturing, private expertise and foreign investment, with more localised technology and exports and a role in development projects in several African countries.

**Sadat City dry port:** A dry port in Sadat City, Menoufia, is due to break ground in the first half of 2027, with a first phase costing about EGP 3 billion, two officials said. It joins a Transport Ministry plan for 33 dry ports and logistics zones meant to cut freight costs for exporters by linking factories to the Mediterranean and Red Sea ports by rail and road.

**Nile water accord:** Egypt and Sudan rejected unilateral action in the Eastern Nile Basin after a third round of "2+2" talks, reaffirmed their rights under the 1959 agreement and signed a memorandum for water projects in Sudan. Water Minister Hany Sewilam argued Ethiopia could meet its energy needs from solar and wind rather than large dams.

**Esna illness:** The Health Ministry said reports of a "mysterious virus" in Esna, Luxor, reflect a seasonal viral illness with fever and body aches. None of the cases needs hospital care and those admitted have recovered, while the Luxor health directorate stepped up monitoring and insect and rodent control.

> **Explained: PMI.** The purchasing managers' index surveys firms on output, orders and hiring; a reading below 50 signals that activity is shrinking.

**Convertible pound:** Central bank Governor Hassan Abdalla said Egypt wants the pound to be "legally convertible" abroad, exchangeable and holdable in foreign bank accounts, within five years. ## Worth Knowing

**Why a falling market on thin trading is not always a crash:** October's EGX30 loss of 4.43% came as stock turnover fell 20.9% from September. Analysts read that as a sign the selling lacked conviction, because a genuine sell-off normally comes with heavy volume as many holders rush out together.

Take an illustrative example. A share falls 5% over a week while only 10 million shares change hands, against a usual 15 million. Few holders gave up; the price slipped because buyers stepped back, not because sellers stampeded. Had it traded 30 million shares on the way down, the move would signal broad selling and a higher chance of further falls.

The same test works in reverse. A rally on falling volume, where few buyers lift prices, is the one analysts trust least, because it tends not to hold. Volume therefore works as a measure of conviction: a price move says what happened, while volume says how many people agreed. Investors comparing sessions should read both, and treat a sharp price move on unusually low turnover with caution.
