## Egypt News

**US framework agreement:** Egypt and the United States signed their first Strategic Objectives Framework Agreement in Cairo on Wednesday, committing $125 million in US funding to expand opportunities for American companies in Egypt and support private-sector growth. ## Top World Headlines

**Paraguay impeachment:** Paraguay's lower house voted 78-0 to impeach President Santiago Peña on Thursday, hours after Congress declined to accept his offer to resign. The Senate opened proceedings and summoned him for Friday. The crisis stems from the collapse of Ueno, a digital lender with about 3 million customers in a country of 6.5 million, whose capital fell below half the legal minimum and which handled close to $850 million in public funds. Peña had held a stake in Ueno's parent group, which he says he sold in 2025, and the attorney general has opened three criminal investigations. Vice President Pedro Alliana would take over if he is removed.

**Najib house arrest:** A Malaysian court allowed former prime minister Najib Razak to serve the rest of his sentence at home after a royal pardon. Najib was jailed in 2022 over the 1MDB scandal, in which investigators say about $4.5 billion was stolen, and his party reportedly raised the roughly $12 million fine through crowdfunding. The decision may stir politics, since Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim has voiced concern about the pardon.

**Indonesia wiretapping case:** Indonesian police named a fourth suspect in the alleged wiretapping of President Prabowo Subianto, a former monitoring chief at the Criminal Investigation Agency who is said to have approved the interception. The case exposes a rift between Prabowo and his predecessor Joko Widodo, while investor confidence in the $1.5 trillion economy weakens.

## Worth Knowing

**Why the dollar index can rise while the pound strengthens:** On Thursday the dollar index rose 0.1%, yet the Egyptian pound gained 1.4% against the dollar. A currency's move against the dollar mixes two things: how the dollar is doing against everything, and what is happening to that specific currency.

Take an illustrative example. Suppose the dollar gains 2% against a basket of major currencies and a pound importer sees the pound slip only 0.5%. The pound has outperformed the basket even though it fell. Reversed, a pound that rises 1.4% on a day when the dollar is flat has gained for its own reasons, such as heavy inflows into Treasury bills.

This is why analysts compare a local rate with a broad index before drawing conclusions. A pound that rises while the index is flat or rising suggests local demand, from carry-trade investors, remittances or foreign deals. A pound that rises only when the index falls suggests a global dollar move that could reverse. Readers following the exchange rate for import costs, travel or savings abroad should therefore watch both.
