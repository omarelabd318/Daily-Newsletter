*Week of November 1 to 7, 2026: inflation slows and the pound recovers, gas rationing hits fertilizer plants, Democrats take Congress and an AI sell-off drags Wall Street to its worst week since June.*

## The Week in Brief

1. **Inflation:** Annual inflation nationwide eased to 12.0% in October from 12.8% in September, widening the gap below the central bank's 20% deposit rate.
2. **Pound:** The central bank's dollar selling rate fell for six sessions in a row to EGP 54.01, a 3.8% recovery for the pound over the week.
3. **EGX30:** The index slid to its lowest close since April on Monday, then rallied three days running to finish the week up 3.1% at 51,152.73.
4. **Gas rationing:** Gas supplies to fertilizer and methanol plants were cut by 20% and five of November's 15 LNG cargoes were deferred as each shipment topped $100 million.
5. **Gold-mining crackdown:** Two army officers were killed in a desert campaign against illegal gold mining that has led to 691 arrests.
6. **Iran:** The United States struck Iranian air defenses on Qeshm island twice and sent a third aircraft carrier toward the region, while Qatar pressed a draft deal.
7. **Israel:** Gadi Eisenkot won the backing of 67 lawmakers to form a government, and President Isaac Herzog names his choice on Sunday.
8. **US midterms:** Democrats won both chambers of Congress, and Stephen Miller replaced Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff.
9. **Wall Street:** Chip stocks had their worst day since 2020 on Thursday, and the S&P 500 lost 2.7% on the week despite a strong jobs report.
10. **Paraguay:** The Senate removed President Santiago Peña over the collapse of digital lender Ueno, and he was placed under house arrest.

## Egypt This Week

**Inflation eases:** Annual headline inflation across Egypt slowed to 12.0% in October from 12.8% in September, the statistics agency CAPMAS said on Thursday, with prices up 0.6% on the month against 1.5% in September. Urban inflation, the measure the central bank targets, slowed to 12.7% from 13.9%, according to the Rio Times (unconfirmed; the central bank has not yet published it). The reading came in below JPMorgan's forecast of 13.8% for October, though the bank still expects prices to pick up in November and December as the pound's October slide passes through. With the overnight deposit rate at 20% after the October 29 hike, savers now earn a real return of about eight points.

**Pound and savings rates:** The pound regained ground every session after the hike took effect on November 1. The central bank's dollar selling rate fell from EGP 56.12 to EGP 54.01 by Thursday's close, its strongest level since about October 20, as foreign institutions returned to buy Treasury bills on the secondary market, according to the Middle East Observer. Net international reserves edged up to $57.5 billion in October from $57.35 billion. Banks passed the hike straight through to floating-rate certificates, with National Bank of Egypt and CIB at 20.50% on their monthly products, and National Bank of Egypt and Banque Misr raise their main fixed-rate certificates by a point to 18.75% from Sunday, Youm7 reported.

**Gas rationing:** Gas allocations to fertilizer and methanol plants have been cut by 20% since the start of November, and Kima told the EGX its supply had fallen by that amount while its urea plant kept running. The government also asked suppliers to defer five of the month's 15 LNG cargoes, each now costing more than $100 million, roughly double September's price, according to Asharq Business, cited by EnterpriseAM. The International Energy Agency expects the regional war to keep the LNG market tight into 2027. Granular urea was fetching about $535 a ton, but fertilizer shares fell through the week.

**Gold-mining crackdown:** The military spokesperson said on Thursday that Colonel Ahmed Samir and Lieutenant Colonel Ahmed Gouda were killed in exchanges of fire during operations against illegal gold mining in the Eastern Desert near the Sudanese border. Three phases since June 14 have led to 691 arrests, 540 of them foreign nationals, and the seizure of 34 weapons and 317 vehicles; a fourth phase begins today. Rights groups have raised concerns about the campaign, The New Arab reported.

**State finances:** The government is preparing for the IMF's eighth and final review, which the Fund aims to take to its board before the program ends in mid-December, with about $2.3 billion at stake. The Finance Ministry will take up to half of the EGP 36.37 billion raised in the Banque du Caire offering to ease debt pressure, and officials are weighing a large stake sale in Egypt Aluminum on the EGX before the year-end, EnterpriseAM reported. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly said the state will cut its own spending and energy use, while strategic commodity reserves cover about six months.

**Subsidies, phones and Washington:** The Supply Ministry will test cash payments in place of in-kind food subsidies for ration card holders in Beheira from January 2027, with no amounts announced. The government dropped the 5% development fee on phones that zone-based factories sell at home in return for new investment. Egypt and the United States signed their first Strategic Objectives Framework Agreement on Wednesday, with $125 million in US funding for private-sector growth, water, health and AI, which Egyptian officials describe as the first of five annual tranches.

## Region and World

**Iran and Hormuz:** Talks to end the nine-month war made little headway. Iran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz over two days if the United States met seven demands, including lifting sanctions and its naval blockade, then called Washington's reply "not convincing". President Donald Trump confirmed "minor" US strikes on air defenses on Qeshm island on Tuesday and again on Friday, and said a decision on resuming the war would come "soon". Qatar is pushing a draft under which the blockade would be lifted in return for Iran inviting nuclear inspectors back, and the USS Theodore Roosevelt is due in the Arabian Sea late next week, giving the US three carrier groups in the region, The National reported. For Egypt, the risk keeps insurers wary of the Red Sea route and Suez Canal revenue under pressure.

**Israel's coalition race:** Herzog's consultations ended with 67 of 120 lawmakers recommending Eisenkot, the former military chief, and 46 backing Benjamin Netanyahu, after the ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism declined to back the prime minister. Arab-led parties recommended Eisenkot, but he and Netanyahu both rule out governing with them, and Herzog warned of "mutual vetoes" within and between the blocs. The nominee will have 28 days, extendable by 14, before another election becomes likely.

**Yemen's war:** A month into the government's offensive, fighting has settled into attrition, with Houthi gains in Taiz and Lahij and government claims of ground near Bab al-Mandeb and in al-Jawf. A strike on a market in Taiz province killed dozens on Friday, and each side blamed the other, Asharq Al-Awsat reported, while the Houthis kept up missile and drone fire into Saudi Arabia.

**Democrats take Congress:** Democrats won the Senate and the House in Tuesday's midterms, with gains in North Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Maine and Iowa, and Michigan's Abdul El-Sayed, whose parents emigrated from Egypt, became the first Muslim senator-elect. Vice President JD Vance blamed the Iran war for the losses. Trump then named Stephen Miller, the architect of his deportation policy, as chief of staff from November 9 after Susie Wiles resigned, a sign of a harder line rather than compromise with the new Congress.

**Paraguay's president removed:** The Senate voted on Friday to remove Santiago Peña, a day after the lower house impeached him 78-0, and prosecutors placed him under house arrest on suspicion of breach of trust. Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in to serve until August 2028. The crisis followed the collapse of Ueno, a digital lender with about three million customers that lawmakers say received hundreds of millions of dollars in public funds; Peña denies wrongdoing.

**New Zealand, Ukraine and Ebola:** New Zealand's election on Saturday produced no clear winner, with Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's coalition projected at a one-seat majority and final results due on November 27, according to the NZ Herald. Russia fired ballistic missiles at Kyiv early Saturday, days before US-mediated talks with Ukraine and Russia resume in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, the first since February. Congo's Ebola outbreak has passed 10,500 confirmed cases and 5,100 deaths, and two vaccine candidates are joining a trial, according to UN News.

## Markets This Week

The week turned on two things: a recovery in Egyptian assets after the central bank's hike, and an AI-driven rout on Wall Street. The EGX30 hit its lowest close since April 8 on Monday as Banque du Caire's debut drew money from other banks, then rose three sessions running as foreign institutions bought, led by Telecom Egypt and CIB. Brent fell 13% on hopes of an end to the Iran war, which lowers Egypt's fuel import bill. In New York, Thursday's sell-off took the Nasdaq into a correction, more than 10% below its October 6 record, as the Philadelphia semiconductor index lost about 12% and Nvidia about 9%, after voters rejected data-center projects and investors questioned the return on AI spending. Friday's strong jobs report lifted chip stocks but revived bets on a Federal Reserve rate rise in December, and San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said further tightening is likely.

## Culture and Tech

**AI money cools:** OpenAI has raised $11 billion of a round that could reach about $20 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below earlier targets, TechCrunch reported, and its president Greg Brockman said the company could be ready to list in 2027. Canva cut 1,271 jobs, 22% of its staff, as chatbots eat into its design business.

## How It Played Out

**Treasury bills after the hike:** The first auctions were expected to test demand at higher rates. The 273-day bill cleared at 26.36% on November 1, its highest since September 2025, but by Thursday the one-year yield had eased to 26.03% from 26.08% as foreign demand returned.

**Banque du Caire debut:** After a public offering 27 times covered, a strong first day was expected; the shares closed Monday just 0.19% above the EGP 5.30 offer price at EGP 5.31, and edged up to about EGP 5.58 by Thursday, according to Investing.com data (unconfirmed).

**Bank of England:** A quarter-point rise was expected, and the Bank delivered it, lifting Bank Rate to 4% by an 8-1 vote, its first increase since August 2023, with Catherine Mann voting for half a point.

**US payrolls:** Forecasts were for about 60,000 jobs; employers added 165,000 in October and unemployment eased to 4.1%, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
