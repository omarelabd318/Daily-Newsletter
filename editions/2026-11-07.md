## Egypt News

**October inflation:** Annual headline inflation across Egypt eased to 12.0% in October from 12.8% in September, statistics agency CAPMAS said on Thursday, while prices rose 0.6% on the month. Food and drink rose 0.8%, led by meat and poultry, up 3.2%, and dairy and eggs, up 2.5%, while fruit and vegetables fell. Urban inflation, the measure the central bank targets, slowed to 12.7% from 13.9%, according to the Rio Times (unconfirmed; the central bank has not yet published it). Housing and utilities costs were still up 24.1% on a year earlier and transport 22.3%. Slower inflation eases pressure on household budgets after the pound's sharp fall in October, and it widens the gap between prices and the central bank's 20% overnight deposit rate.

> **Explained: real interest rate.** The interest rate minus inflation. When it is positive and wide, savers and foreign investors earn more than prices rise, which supports demand for the local currency.

**Illegal gold mining crackdown:** Two army officers, Colonel Ahmed Samir and Lieutenant Colonel Ahmed Gouda, were killed in exchanges of fire during an operation against illegal gold mining in the Eastern Desert, the military spokesperson said on Thursday. Three phases since June 14 led to 691 arrests, 540 of them foreign nationals, while 6,614 people surrendered before the operations began. Forces seized 34 weapons, 317 vehicles and 916 pieces of mining equipment and removed 4,488 tents, and a fourth phase begins today. Officials link the armed groups behind the mining to arms and drug smuggling and illegal migration across the Sudanese border, and Egypt is coordinating with Sudanese authorities.

**Childhood cancer screening:** The Health Ministry will roll out a national program for the early detection of childhood cancers in every governorate from January 2027, at an estimated cost of EGP 1 billion, according to CairoScene, citing ministry spokesperson Hossam Abdel Ghaffar. It covers children and teenagers up to 18 and starts with six cancers, including acute lymphoblastic leukemia, Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma and retinoblastoma. Screening begins at primary care units and school clinics, with suspected cases referred to pediatric oncology centers, and the aim is to cut diagnosis times by at least 20%. A pilot runs to the end of this year.

**Nuweiba flood defenses:** The Irrigation Ministry denied viral posts claiming that flood dams in Nuweiba, South Sinai, had collapsed, saying all primary dams remain intact. The photos showed erosion around a bridge support on the Sharm el-Sheikh to Taba highway, outside the ministry's remit, while some secondary barriers need substantial repair after the October 27 storm. The Wadi Watir defenses captured about 13 million cubic meters of floodwater, and an EGP 10 billion expansion with nine new dams is planned.

**World Golf Awards:** Madinaty Golf Club hosts the 13th World Golf Awards tonight, the first time the ceremony has been held in Africa. The course, developed by Talaat Moustafa Group, has been named Egypt's best five times. The event is part of a push to market Egypt as a winter golf destination alongside its beach resorts.

## Top World Headlines

**Paraguay's new president:** Paraguay's Senate voted 44 to 0, with one abstention, on Friday to remove President Santiago Peña, and prosecutors ordered him placed under house arrest on suspicion of breach of trust. Vice President Pedro Alliana, 52, was sworn in as president the same day. The crisis followed the collapse of digital lender Ueno, which served about 3 million people, nearly two-thirds of the country's adults, and held about $355 million in state pension money; Peña had owned shares in its parent group. At least 27 people face charges, and a warrant was issued for the former central bank chief.

**New Zealand's close vote:** New Zealand's ruling National Party and the opposition Labour Party were each just under 30% in early counting of Saturday's election, Al Jazeera reported, with projections giving Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's coalition a one-seat majority. ## Worth Knowing

**Fixed or floating: how to choose a savings certificate when rates move:** A floating certificate pays a rate tied to the central bank's overnight deposit rate, rising and falling with it. A fixed one locks in a rate for the whole term, typically three years. Choosing between them is a bet on where rates go next.

Take EGP 100,000. A fixed certificate at 18.75% with monthly payouts pays about EGP 1,563 a month, or EGP 18,750 a year, for three years whatever the central bank does. A floating certificate starting near 20.5% pays more today, about EGP 20,500 a year, but if inflation keeps falling, from 12.8% in September to 12% in October, the central bank may start cutting next year. If rates fell by four points by 2028, the floating holder's income would drop to about EGP 16,500 a year, while the fixed holder would still collect EGP 18,750.

The rule of thumb: floating protects savers while rates rise, fixed when cuts are coming, which is why banks raise fixed rates more slowly. Early-redemption penalties, which can wipe out months of interest, are worth checking before choosing either.
