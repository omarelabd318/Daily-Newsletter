## Egypt News

**Spending squeeze:** The Cabinet approved a draft decree on Thursday ordering three months of spending cuts across every state budget body and public economic authority, extendable if needed. Government entities must cut electricity and lighting use by 20% against the same period last fiscal year and fuel for official vehicles by at least 30%; at least 20% of remaining goods-and-services allocations are frozen, fuel-hungry national projects are slowed or postponed, and no new projects may start this fiscal year. Advertising, receptions, conferences and most travel abroad are cut, and government car purchases are off until June 30, 2027. Any spending with a foreign-currency component now needs Finance Ministry approval, coordinated with the central bank. Health, pensions, the Takaful and Karama cash-support programs, food subsidies and fuel supply are exempt. It is the second such squeeze this year, after a March decree, and the Cabinet tied it to "events in the Middle East." For households, the point is protection: the state is trimming its own bills and its dollar needs to keep subsidies and services funded while the Iran war drags on.

**Export rebates rise:** A revamped export rebate program, approved by the Cabinet on Thursday, raises support by an average of 35% from this month's shipments, according to a Cabinet statement reported by EnterpriseAM. The state will also cover half of sea and land freight to Africa, Latin America, Australia, Southeast Asia and Russia, a quarter of air freight to Europe and half to the UK for farm exports, and a quarter of domestic transport and cargo insurance. Textiles, food, engineering, pharma, furniture, chemicals and building materials are covered; free-zone exporters are not. The budget sets aside EGP 48 billion for export support this fiscal year, up from EGP 28 billion paid out last year. Non-oil exports grew 7.8% to $37.3 billion last fiscal year, while non-oil imports jumped 16.9%, so a broader export base is one of the few ways to earn dollars that do not leave at the first shock.

**Strategic stockpile:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi ordered officials to study building a strategic reserve of goods for emergencies and told every state body to speed up sending data to the National Authority for Crisis and Emergency Management, Daily News Egypt reported. The aim is one national database to guide decisions in a crisis, with the authority's head tasked with setting how much of each strategic good the country should hold. With the Iran war disrupting shipping and fuel markets, a larger buffer of essentials is meant to shield prices and supplies at home.

**US aid template:** The Foreign Ministry and the US Embassy signed the first bilateral Strategic Objective Agreement for Advancing Mutual Prosperity, with Washington committing $125 million for projects in food security, farming, education, health, water and workforce skills. The money is tied to private-sector growth and market access for American companies, a new model after USAID ended 44 Egyptian programs worth about $950 million last year. Egypt's roughly $1.3 billion a year in US military aid is unaffected.

**Fishermen freed:** Nine Egyptian fishermen whose boat, the Mohamed Abu Al-Aza'im, was held in Saudi Arabia's Tabuk region since October 16 are on their way home after Cairo secured their release, Egyptian Streets reported, citing the Foreign Ministry. The boat had entered Saudi waters without authorization, and the ministry urged crews to get permits before fishing in neighbors' waters.

**Abdelatty in Tripoli:** Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty flew to Libya on Saturday for talks with senior Libyan officials and the 22nd session of the executive council of the Community of Sahel-Saharan States. Stability in Libya matters directly to Egypt's western border, to the many Egyptians who work there and to Egyptian builders bidding for reconstruction work.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**Goldman on the pound:** Goldman Sachs says the pound is trading more than 30% below fair value after October's slide past 56 to the dollar, making it one of the most undervalued emerging-market currencies, according to a research note reported by Asharq Bloomberg and EnterpriseAM. The bank now sees the dollar at EGP 53 in three months, 52 in six and 50 in a year, and keeps its buy call. It estimates foreign holdings of local debt fell to about $20 billion from $30 billion at the end of September, and warns more could leave, but argues that high rates pay investors to wait.

> **Explained: real effective exchange rate.** A currency's value against a basket of trading partners' currencies, adjusted for differences in inflation. When the market rate sits well below it, the currency is cheaper than the economy's fundamentals suggest.

## Worth Knowing

**Share buybacks: what happens when a company buys its own stock:** A buyback is a company using its cash to purchase its own shares on the market. The shares stop counting toward earnings, so each remaining share owns a slightly bigger slice of the profit. Take a company that earns EGP 100 million and has 100 million shares: earnings per share are EGP 1. If it buys back 5 million shares, the same profit is spread over 95 million shares, lifting earnings per share to about EGP 1.05, a 5% gain without any new business. At the same price-to-earnings multiple, the share price should rise in step.

Boards usually buy back when they think the market has priced the company too low, which is why buybacks cluster after selloffs such as October's on the EGX. They are also a cheaper way to return cash than dividends in Egypt, where dividends carry a 10% withholding tax.

There are catches. A buyback uses cash that could have funded growth or cut debt. In Egypt, shares held in treasury cannot exceed 10% of the company and must be canceled or resold within a year: canceled shares shrink the count for good, but resold shares come back as supply. And when free float is already thin, removing more shares can make a stock jumpier.
