## Egypt News

**Al-Ayyat train crash:** Seven people were killed and 125 injured on Sunday afternoon when a Tahya Misr train running from Giza to Fayoum ran into the back of a Cairo-Aswan service between Kafr Ammar and El Reqqa stations in Al-Ayyat, south of Cairo, the Health Ministry said. Several carriages left the tracks. Witnesses said the Aswan-bound train had stopped when it was hit, at about 1:30pm. Seventy-two ambulances took casualties to Al-Wasta, Al-Ayyat and Atfih hospitals; by late Sunday, 71 had gone home and 43 remained under observation. The railway authority has not given a cause, President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi ordered an urgent investigation to hold anyone negligent to account, and the prosecution has taken charge of the case. The Financial Regulatory Authority raised compensation for each death and total disability from EGP 30,000 to EGP 50,000 as an exception. Al-Ayyat was the scene of Egypt's worst rail disaster, a 2002 fire that killed more than 360 people, and the crash revives questions about the slow modernization of the passenger network that millions use daily.

**Gas cuts deepen:** The government has raised gas curtailments for fertilizer and methanol plants to 33%, from 20% imposed on November 1, Mopco told the bourse on Sunday, and has notified Ezz Steel of a 30% cut with no end date, Asharq Business reported. The reason is cost: Asian spot LNG prices rose about 36% in October to more than $34 per million Btu, a single cargo now costs close to $120 million, and five of the 15 cargoes contracted for November have been deferred. The plants being squeezed are among the country's best dollar earners: fertilizer exports brought in $2.53 billion in the first nine months of the year, and urea fetches about $535 a ton. Steelmakers say they can shift partly to scrap, but only by importing more and spending more foreign currency.

**Drug prices test:** Drugmakers are pushing for price rises of about 20% after the pound's October slide, Al Borsa reported, but the Egyptian Drug Authority says any change must go through its new pricing formula, backed by documents proving higher costs. That formula, in force since September 24, gives the exchange rate 60% of the weight, urban inflation 30% and the central bank's deposit rate 10%, and allows a rise only when the result moves at least 10% for 45 days. The dollar never stayed above the roughly EGP 55 trigger that long, said Ali Ouf, who heads the pharma division of the Federation of Egyptian Chambers of Commerce, and even approved increases would not reach pharmacy shelves before late in the second quarter of 2027. For patients, prices are likely to stay put for months; the risk is that loss-making medicines are moved to supplement registrations outside the system.

**Back in Tripoli:** Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty reopened Egypt's embassy in Tripoli after a 12-year closure, in the first bilateral visit to the Libyan capital by an Egyptian foreign minister since 2011. The mission was evacuated in January 2014 after diplomatic staff were kidnapped, and the empty building was later looted and hit by a car bomb. Abdelatty also met Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibah. A working embassy in the west matters for the many Egyptians who work in Libya and for Egyptian firms chasing reconstruction work.

**Tourism target:** Egypt received about 16.2 million tourists by the end of October and aims for 20 million by year-end and 22 million in 2027, Tourism Minister Sherif Fathy said on the El Hekaya talk show. He called Egypt "no longer a cheap destination" and said spending per visitor is rising; tourism earned $18.4 billion in the last fiscal year, up 10.3%, according to central bank figures. Earlier ministry figures put first-half arrivals at about nine million.

**Bo Island handover:** The Tahya Misr development fund's holding company is taking over Maxim's Bo Island project in Sidi Abdel Rahman on the North Coast after the New Urban Communities Authority approved the transfer of its two land plots, the Housing Ministry said. The ministry told buyers to ignore rumors and wait for official updates, but has not said what the handover means for their contracts or delivery dates.

## Worth Knowing

**Weighted formulas: how an index decides a price:** Many regulated prices, from medicines to electricity tariffs and some rents, are set by a weighted formula: several cost drivers, each given a share of the total, combined into one number. Egypt's new drug pricing rule is a clear case. It weights the exchange rate at 60%, urban inflation at 30% and the central bank's deposit rate at 10%.

Take an example. Suppose that since the last repricing the dollar has risen 10%, inflation has run at 12% and the deposit rate has risen 5% in relative terms (say from 19% to about 20%). The formula result is 0.6 × 10% + 0.3 × 12% + 0.1 × 5% = 6% + 3.6% + 0.5% = 10.1%. That just clears a 10% trigger. If the dollar had risen only 8%, the result would be 4.8% + 3.6% + 0.5% = 8.9%, and no change would be allowed.

Two points follow. First, weights decide which shock matters most: here a currency move counts six times as much as a rate move. Second, triggers and waiting periods, such as the 45 days Egypt requires, smooth out short spikes, so a brief jump in the dollar does not reach the pharmacy. The trade-off is lag: when costs rise for good, producers absorb the gap for months.
