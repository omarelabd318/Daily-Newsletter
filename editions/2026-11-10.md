## Egypt News

**Inflation eases:** Annual urban inflation slowed for a third month to 12.7% in October from 13.9% in September, its lowest since January; the nationwide rate fell to 12.0% from 12.8%, Amwal Al Ghad reported. Monthly price growth halved to 0.7%. Vegetables fell 2% after September's spike, but poultry and cooking oils each jumped 7.4% and meat 3.1%, so the weekly shop did not get cheaper. Transport, at 25.8% a year, is now the fastest-rising category, while housing and utilities cooled to 24.5% from 35.2% as last year's tariff increases dropped out of the comparison. Analysts had braced for worse after the pound's October slide: JPMorgan had forecast 13.8%. The warning sign is core inflation, which eased to 13.1% a year but rose 1.2% in the month, up from 0.8%, and JPMorgan now sees headline inflation back at 14.3% by December as the favorable base runs out.

> **Explained: core inflation.** A measure of price growth that strips out volatile items such as fruit, vegetables and regulated fuel prices, so it shows the underlying trend central banks try to steer.

**Al-Ayyat crash detentions:** Prosecutors ordered the detention of a station controller and the stationmaster at El-Raqqa station, and placed the driver's assistant in custody pending questioning, after preliminary findings of negligence in Sunday's collision south of Cairo. According to the prosecution, the driver of train No. 134 had disabled the automatic train control system, the two officials admitted failing to watch the control panel for a signal tower, and the controller is suspected of tampering with the signals logbook. A committee of Cairo University engineering professors will review signaling, maintenance and safety procedures, along with earlier accidents, to find recurring causes. The crash killed seven people; reported injury totals range from 112 to 125.

**Evergreen back in Suez:** Taiwan's Evergreen sent its first ship through the Suez Canal in nearly three years on Sunday, when the 16,556-container *Ever Eon* joined the northbound convoy from the Netherlands to Singapore. Evergreen was the last member of its Ocean Alliance to return and plans to route more sailings back through the canal. The recovery is visible in the data: October toll receipts rose 94% from a year earlier to $724 million, the highest monthly figure since the Red Sea crisis began, according to EnterpriseAM, and container ships averaged 13.6 transits a day in the first 18 days of October, against 9.9 a year earlier, according to [IMF PortWatch](https://portwatch.imf.org/).

**Health spending pledge:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi said delivering quality healthcare would need a budget at least double or triple today's, and that his "greatest dream" is for Egypt to have 10,000 doctors of surgeon Magdi Yacoub's caliber, speaking at an Armed Forces College of Medicine conference in the New Capital. He said Kayan University will open in February 2027 with six faculties and room for about 25,000 students, and ordered the Tahya Misr Fund to pay gifted students' fees.

**Building violations relief:** Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly told ministers to finish amendments that would make it easier and cheaper to legalize buildings put up in breach of planning rules, a scheme that touches a large share of households. Proposals include completing settlement within 12 months of the law's amendment, fees that reflect local market values, cheaper terms for low-income family homes than for commercial buildings, longer installment plans and fewer documents. The government wants to agree the wording with lawmakers who have filed their own amendments.

**Waste-to-power stalled:** Green Tech Egypt has put its Abu Rawash plant, planned as the country's first to turn municipal waste into electricity, on hold because lenders will not finance a tariff of 7 US cents plus EGP 0.35 per kilowatt-hour, split across two currencies. The company wants 7.7 cents fully linked to the dollar for the $140 million, 35-megawatt plant, which would burn 1,500 tons of Giza's waste a day. The waste regulator says the rate stands and prefers turning rubbish into fuel for cement kilns.

**Shelter for stray dogs:** Cairo opened its first stray-dog shelter in El Tebbin, south of the capital, with room for about 700 dogs, two operating theaters and a program of sterilization and rabies vaccination before adoption. The governorate plans more if it works.

## Worth Knowing

**Real returns: what a 20% rate is really worth:** A savings rate tells you how many more pounds you will have; the real return tells you how much more you can buy. With prices rising, the two can be far apart.

The quick estimate subtracts inflation from the interest rate. With the central bank's deposit rate at 20% and urban inflation at 12.7%, that gives 7.3 points. The exact method divides instead: (1 + 0.20) ÷ (1 + 0.127) − 1 = 6.5%. The gap between the two widens as rates rise, which is why the shortcut overstates real returns in high-inflation economies.

Take a saver with EGP 100,000 in a three-year certificate paying 18.75% a year. After one year the bank pays EGP 18,750. If prices rise 12.7%, the basket that cost EGP 100,000 now costs EGP 112,700, so the saver's EGP 118,750 buys about 5.4% more than a year earlier. If inflation climbed back to 14.3%, as JPMorgan expects by December, the real gain would shrink to about 3.9%.

Two points follow. First, a fixed rate locks in the nominal return, not the real one: the saver carries the inflation risk. Second, the same arithmetic drives foreign money. Investors compare Egypt's real yield with what they can earn elsewhere, and the wider the cushion, the more they are paid to hold pounds.
