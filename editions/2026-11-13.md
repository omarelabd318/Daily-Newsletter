## Egypt News

**Power price for data centers:** Egypt has set a dollar-denominated electricity price for artificial-intelligence data centers of 7.5 US cents per kilowatt-hour in Upper Egypt and 8 cents in the northern governorates, Electricity Minister Mahmoud Esmat said, with a formal announcement expected within days, Youm7 reported. The country is planning five to seven large centers: the north, near the Mediterranean's undersea cable landings, for international connectivity and cloud services, and Upper Egypt, with cheaper land and nearby solar power at Benban, for green and backup facilities. Charging in dollars shields the state from currency swings and signals that Egypt wants a share of the global computing build-out, a scarce and power-hungry industry. The grid, Esmat said, can meet both household and project demand.

**Tax cut on medical devices:** A set of amendments to the value-added tax law, published as Law 149 of 2026, cuts VAT on machinery, equipment and medical devices to 5% from the standard 14%. Kidney dialysis equipment is exempt, and the period during which tax on imported machinery can be deferred until it is installed in production rises to four years from two. The changes also shorten VAT refund periods and bring commercial rents into the tax at the general rate. Cheaper medical equipment is meant to ease costs for hospitals and, in time, for patients.

**School bus drivers tested for drugs:** Of 4,510 school-bus drivers screened in the first two months of the academic year, 31 tested positive for drugs, 28 for hashish and three for the painkiller tramadol, the Fund for Drug Control and Treatment of Addiction said. All were referred to the public prosecution and face dismissal. The fund said the positive rate has fallen sharply over the years, from 12% in 2017, and that unannounced testing will continue and widen to more governorates.

**6th October Bridge partly closed:** Cairo's 6th October Bridge, one of the capital's busiest arteries, is partly closed in both directions while crews repair metal expansion joints, part of a wider road-maintenance drive. Traffic is still moving around the work zones, with signs and extra officers deployed; no end date was given.

**Power of attorney goes online:** Egyptians can now request a power of attorney through the Digital Egypt platform and receive the signed document by post at home, part of a push to move routine paperwork off government counters. The service covers several common types of authorization.

**Millions of pupils screened:** The Health Ministry has examined about 3.4 million students for anemia, obesity and stunting under a national school-health drive, with free treatment for confirmed cases and some 2,000 medical teams deployed. The program aims to catch nutrition and growth problems early, when they are cheapest to treat.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**Wind farms reach financing:** Two renewable projects on the Gulf of Suez locked in financing within a day of each other, Egypt's largest clean-energy commitments yet. A consortium of Japan's Aeolus (40%), France's ENGIE (35%) and Orascom Construction (25%) reached financial close on the 900 MW Gulf of Suez III wind farm, to be built with 86 turbines and sold to the grid under a 25-year power agreement, with lenders including the Japan Bank for International Cooperation, Société Générale and KfW IPEX-Bank, and commercial operation due in September 2028. Separately, Alcazar Energy Partners closed about $436 million of debt for its 500 MW Niat and Rasgha wind farms near Ras Ghareb, a roughly $626 million complex financed in equal thirds by the EBRD, the European Investment Bank and Germany's DEG, with Siemens Gamesa supplying the turbines, the EBRD said. Together the projects add about 1,400 MW and draw in long-term foreign capital at a time the state is courting investment.

**EGX30 slips from its highs:** The EGX30 fell 0.9% on Thursday to 52,023.37, pulling back from midweek highs though still up 24.4% so far this year, near the top of its range. Juhayna dropped 2.3%, Talaat Moustafa Group 2.0% and Cleopatra Hospital 1.6%, while Alexandria Containers rose 1.7% and the government fintech firm e-finance (EFIH) edged up 0.7% after announcing a bonus-share distribution. **One-year bills steady:** The central bank's November 12 auction left the one-year treasury-bill yield at 26.07%, barely changed, with the six-month tenor at 25.02%, keeping the real return that underpins foreign investors' carry trade broadly intact before the bank's December 17 rate meeting.

> **Explained: the carry trade.** Borrowing in a low-interest currency to invest in a higher-yielding one, such as Egyptian treasury bills, pocketing the gap as long as the exchange rate holds steady.

## Worth Knowing

**Project finance: how a wind farm pays for itself:** Big infrastructure, like the Gulf of Suez wind farms that reached financing this week, is rarely paid for out of a company's own pocket. Instead it uses project finance: lenders are repaid not from the sponsors' other assets but from the cash the project itself will generate once it is running.

The structure starts with a special-purpose vehicle, a standalone company created to own just that one project. Into it goes equity from the sponsors, typically 20-30% of the cost, with the rest as long-term debt. The revenue backbone is a power-purchase agreement, here a 25-year contract to sell electricity to the grid at a set price, which gives lenders a predictable income to lend against. "Financial close" is the moment all the contracts are signed and money can start flowing to build.

Take a simplified case: a $600 million farm funded with $180 million of equity and $420 million of debt. If it earns $70 million a year selling power, that covers interest and gradually repays the loans, while the sponsors keep what is left once the debt is cleared. Because the debt is "non-recourse," lenders scrutinize the contract and the wind resource closely, since the project's own earnings are their only security.
