## Since Friday

**Salah's record night:** Egypt beat Malawi 5-0 at Cairo International Stadium on Friday in Ali Maher's first game as coach, and Mohamed Salah's two late goals, a header in the 84th minute and a stoppage-time penalty, took him to 70 for his country, one more than Hossam Hassan's long-standing record. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed overloaded switches and routers, saying "we use 1980s little copper lines", and officials ruled out a cyberattack. FlightAware counted more than 6,600 cancellations and 25,100 delays.

**Trump, Xi and Putin to meet:** The White House said on Friday that Donald Trump will end a visit to China next week with the first three-way meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18 and 19. Nuclear arms reduction and Iran are on the agenda, Ukraine is not, and US officials expect no agreement.

**Mines in Hormuz:** Iran's Revolutionary Guard said two oil tankers caught fire overnight after hitting mines while trying to cross the Strait of Hormuz by an "unauthorized" route with their tracking switched off, and Iran's navy reported a third tanker ablaze, AFP reported. None of the ships was named and the claims have not been independently confirmed; the UN says 25 seafarers have died in and around the strait since February.

## What Tied the Week Together

The week's thread was the cost of energy in a war that neither ends nor escalates. With the Strait of Hormuz still disrupted and spot LNG cargoes near $120 million each, the government cut gas to fertilizer plants by a third and to steel mills by 30%, and ordered state bodies to use a fifth less electricity and nearly a third less fuel for three months. Abu Qir Fertilizers fell 4.3% on the week. These are measures to save dollars, and for now the dollars are arriving: remittances reached a record $38.2 billion in nine months, Suez Canal revenue nearly doubled from a year earlier in October, and the central bank's dollar rate eased about 0.9% to EGP 53.50, while the EGX30 added 1.7%.

Abroad, the pressure ran the other way. A third hot US inflation reading pushed the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, the dollar index to a 19-month high and expectations toward Fed hikes in December and, by Bank of America's reckoning, January. A stronger dollar and higher US yields usually make emerging-market debt less attractive, though Egypt's one-year bill still pays 26.07%, and the same dollar strength took gold in Cairo down 3.9% on the week, softening the price of a household's main hedge.

Unresolved going into next week: whether Egypt's gas cuts ease before December as an EGAS source suggested, the IMF's final review before the program ends on December 15, and whether the Trump-Xi-Putin meeting in Shenzhen moves the Iran war, which drives both the canal's traffic and the price of Egypt's LNG.

## Egypt This Week

**Al-Ayyat train crash:** Seven people, including a driver, were killed and 125 injured when two passenger trains collided at Al-Ayyat in Giza on Sunday, November 8. Prosecutors found that the automatic train control system on one of the trains had been switched off and detained the El-Raqqa signal controller and stationmaster. Transport Minister Kamel El-Wazir spent about six hours before parliament's transport committee on Tuesday, saying "we acknowledge that there are mistakes" and that 21 officials had signed off the train's safety checks, The National reported. Switching off the control system is now punishable by dismissal. Some lawmakers demanded his resignation; he stays, the Cairo–High Dam line reopened on Tuesday and a Cairo University engineering panel is reviewing safety.

**Gas rationing and the spending squeeze:** Mopco told the bourse on November 8 that its gas supply had been cut by 33%, up from 20% at the start of the month, and Ezz Steel, Suez Steel and Beshay were notified of 30% cuts. The Petroleum Ministry budgeted about $10.1 billion for LNG imports this fiscal year, and prices are running above that. An unnamed EGAS source called the cuts temporary, but no official end date has been given. The same strain is behind a three-month squeeze on state spending that the Cabinet approved on November 5: government bodies must use 20% less electricity and at least 30% less fuel, and no new projects may start this fiscal year, with health, pensions, cash support and food subsidies exempt.

**Prices, remittances and the pound:** Urban inflation slowed to 12.7% in October from 13.9% in September, its lowest since January, though core prices rose 1.2% on the month, faster than September's 0.8%. Egyptians abroad sent home $13 billion in July-September, up 20.4% from a year earlier, taking nine-month remittances to a record $38.2 billion, according to the central bank. The central bank's dollar selling rate eased to EGP 53.50 on Thursday from 54.01 a week earlier. Goldman Sachs called the pound more than 30% undervalued and sees it at 50 to the dollar within a year. The one-year Treasury bill yield held at 26.07% at Thursday's auction, still far above the 20% deposit rate.

**Troops in Saudi Arabia:** Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty told France 24 on November 10 that Egyptian forces have been in Saudi Arabia "for several months" to help defend sensitive sites, including the holy cities, and will not enter Yemen. Sources put the force in the hundreds, and The National says it includes armored vehicles and air-defense batteries. Saudi Arabia has not commented.

**Debt, land and pay:** A presidential decree lets the Finance Ministry use 26,690 feddans of Red Sea land to cut public debt rather than only to back sukuk, as the government aims to bring debt to about 69.5% of GDP by 2030. The Cabinet approved a pay rise for all public university faculty, with the amount due on Monday; a Cabinet spokesman said it takes effect on July 1, 2027. The IMF still aims to complete the eighth and final review, worth about $2.3 billion, before the program ends on December 15.

**Wind, ships and the EU:** The 900 MW Gulf of Suez III wind farm, led by ENGIE with Orascom, reached financing, and the EBRD agreed up to $145 million for two Alcazar wind farms totaling 500 MW. Evergreen sent its first ship through the Suez Canal in nearly three years on November 8, after canal revenue rose 94% from a year earlier to $724 million in October. Egypt also became the first country outside Europe to align its electronic signatures with the European Union's framework, which eases trusted cross-border documents.

## Region and World

**Iran:** After meeting Qatar's prime minister on November 9, Trump said "Iran militarily is effectively over" and kept the naval blockade and a new task force to cut Iran's trade by land. On Thursday he wrote that "we don't need a deal with Iran", moved Vice President JD Vance off the talks, and later told an interviewer he could resume strikes "right now". Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Friday it destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, while US Central Command said it shot down two drones with no damage. Israel's security cabinet ordered the army to prepare for a possible attack on Iran, according to the public broadcaster KAN, cited by Anadolu. The blockade stays, with no talks scheduled, keeping insurers wary of the Red Sea route that feeds the Suez Canal.

**Houthis and the Gulf:** Houthi missiles shattered windows on several floors of Aramco's tower in Riyadh's financial district on November 8, with no casualties, and the group later claimed strikes on an East-West pipeline pumping station. Turkey and Qatar signed a mutual defense pact in Istanbul on Friday pledging a joint response to any attack.

**Israel's coalition:** Gadi Eisenkot received the mandate to form a government on November 8 and has 28 days, extendable by 14. He is courting the ultra-Orthodox parties while Avigdor Liberman rules out a minority government relying on Arab-led parties, The Jerusalem Post reported. Benjamin Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister.

**Gaza and Sudan:** An Israeli strike on the surgery building of Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis killed at least six people on November 12, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old boy, and a baby died of cold in a tent the same day. More than 50 countries and organizations urged Israel to let aid in freely; the UN says 150,000 tents are stuck at the borders. In Sudan, the army said it downed a second drone from Ethiopia, which Ethiopia denies.

**Ukraine:** Russian, Ukrainian and US negotiators met in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, their first direct talks in nine months, agreeing a large prisoner exchange but no wider deal. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner then spent about two hours with Putin, who called European troops in Ukraine "absolutely unacceptable". The EU named Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland to speak for Europe at the next round, which has no date, Reuters and RTÉ reported.

**Elsewhere:** The gunman who killed six people in Krakow's Main Market Square on November 7 died in hospital on November 9; police found his parents and sister shot dead at home, bringing the toll to nine. Spain's snap-election campaign opened after a general strike over housing, with polls pointing to a right-wing majority on November 29. Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases and 5,400 deaths, and Médecins Sans Frontières called it "out of control".
