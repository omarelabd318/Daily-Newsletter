## Egypt News

**Minimum wage rises:** The National Wages Council raised the private-sector minimum wage to EGP 8,500 a month from January 1, 2027, up 21% from EGP 7,000, at an extraordinary meeting chaired by Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly on Sunday. Part-time and temporary workers get a minimum net hourly wage of EGP 34, and from 2027 employers must grant an annual raise of at least 3% of the social insurance wage, and no less than EGP 350. The floor applies to about 26.5 million private-sector workers, according to EnterpriseAM, and sits above the public sector's EGP 8,000. With annual inflation at 12.7% in October, the rise restores some of the buying power lost since the last increase.

**Price on request banned:** The Consumer Protection Agency said that hiding prices behind "price in private message" replies, a common habit of shops selling on Instagram and Facebook, breaks consumer law, with fines of up to EGP 500,000 or the value of the goods, whichever is greater. The warning, carried by Masrawy and Al-Shorouk, applies to physical stores and online sellers alike, and gives shoppers grounds to complain when a seller will not post a price.

**Suez Canal discounts:** The Suez Canal Authority extended its transit-fee discounts for 11 vessel types, among them container ships, tankers, LNG and LPG carriers, bulk carriers and car carriers, to June 30, 2027. The rebates range from 10% to 75% of normal fees depending on ship type and route. The move aims to win back shipping lines still sailing around the Cape of Good Hope; Japan's ONE has begun returning ships to the canal, according to Arab Finance.

**Growth plan reviewed:** President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi reviewed the 2026/27 economic plan with the prime minister and Planning Minister Ahmed Rostom on Sunday. The plan targets growth of up to 5.4% and total investment of EGP 3.7 trillion this fiscal year, which began in July. Faster growth is the government's main lever to bring down debt as its IMF program nears its end.

**Trade push with India:** Egypt and India aim to almost double bilateral trade from $6.5 billion in 2025/26 to $12 billion by 2030, Investment Minister Mohamed Farid Saleh said at a business forum with India's commerce minister, Piyush Goyal, in Cairo on Sunday. The two sides also agreed to speed up the first Indian industrial zone in the Suez Canal Economic Zone, a bid for factory jobs and exports.

**New trains on Metro Line 1:** The first two of 55 new air-conditioned Alstom trains entered service on Cairo Metro Line 1, the city's oldest and busiest, at a launch attended by Transport Minister Kamel al-Wazir and the French ambassador. Each train has nine carriages, and the full fleet should ease crowding for millions of daily riders.

**Kuwait agreements:** Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty and his Kuwaiti counterpart, Sheikh Jarrah Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah, closed the 14th session of their joint committee in Kuwait on Sunday with deals on civil aviation, economic zones, trade, education and culture. Kuwait is home to one of the largest Egyptian communities abroad.

## Top World Headlines

**Kerch bridge strike:** Ukraine hit the Kerch bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea early on Monday, halting traffic and stranding about 2,000 vehicles. Russia's defense ministry said it downed 1,336 drones over 16 regions overnight, the largest Ukrainian attack of the war, beating 1,254 on October 29. Refineries were also targeted. EU foreign ministers accused Moscow of stalling peace talks while bombing Ukraine's power plants as winter sets in.

**Trump heads to Asia:** President Trump flies to Manila for a US-ASEAN summit on Tuesday, then to Shenzhen for the APEC summit, where he is due to hold talks with Xi Jinping and a first three-way meeting with Xi and Vladimir Putin. He arrives weakened by the November 3 midterms, which handed Democrats both chambers of Congress, and by the Nvidia spy scandal. The talks matter for oil, trade and diplomacy on Ukraine and Iran.

**Estonia shuts its Russian border:** Estonia will close all three of its border crossings with Russia from December 1 after police detained eight Latvian citizens suspected of trying to set fire to Threod Systems, a military drone maker near Tallinn, on November 14. Officials blamed Russia's GRU military intelligence. The crossings carry more than 60% of Russians' overland trips to Europe.

**Japan's economy shrinks:** Japan's economy contracted at an annualized 0.1% in July-September, its first contraction in four quarters, as high prices and bad weather held back consumer spending, according to preliminary Cabinet Office data. Economists had expected growth. The yen is near 160 to the dollar, complicating the Bank of Japan's next rate decision.

**South Africa shootings:** At least 17 people were killed in two mass shootings on Sunday night: nine in a shack near Pretoria and eight in a minibus in KwaZulu-Natal, police said. South Africa records about 60 murders a day, and 28 people died in two shootings in September.

**US flights recover:** US air travel was returning to normal on Monday after a nationwide ground stop on Friday caused a weekend of disruption. About 8,365 flights were delayed and 533 canceled by early Monday, against 26,392 delays and 2,858 cancellations on Sunday, according to FlightAware data cited by NBC News.

## Regional and Gulf

**Houthis near Aden:** Yemeni government forces backed by Saudi Arabia pulled out of Dhubab and positions near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Sunday to defend Aden, the government's interim capital, leaving the Houthis in control of ground overlooking the strait. AFP put the rebels about 60 kilometers from Aden. The UN says nearly 330,000 people have been displaced since fighting escalated in August. For Egypt, Houthi control of the Red Sea's southern gate is a direct risk to the Suez Canal's recovery.

**Iran shortens its Hormuz timeline:** Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi told state TV that Iran had cut the time its plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would take from seven days to two, after talks with mediators including Qatar and Pakistan, and said Washington must now take "concrete steps." He also ruled out UN nuclear inspections until the war ends. Meanwhile the USS Theodore Roosevelt became the third US aircraft carrier in the region, and the UK maritime agency UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unknown projectile near Hormuz on Monday, with no casualties.

**Bahrain jails clerics:** A Bahraini court sentenced 64 people to life in prison and five to 15 years for colluding with Iran's Revolutionary Guard, in a case rights groups say mostly involved Shi'ite clerics. The ruling comes as Iran claims drone attacks on US targets in Bahrain.

**Iraq moves on a militia stronghold:** Iraq ordered its army into Jurf al-Sakhar, south of Baghdad, a stronghold of the Iran-backed Kataib Hezbollah, after an inquiry confirmed oil smuggling there. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi had dismissed nine security officials over the case on Saturday, setting up a rare confrontation with the militia.

**Jordan bans Telegram:** Jordan's Public Security Directorate blocked Telegram, citing extremism, cybercrime and extortion, amid a crackdown after nine people were killed in an attack on a Greek Orthodox church last month. Iraq banned the app in April.

## Markets

### Egypt Markets

**Small caps slump:** The EGX30 edged up 0.02% to 52,199.8 on Monday, but the equal-weighted EGX70 fell 3.76% to 18,028.4 and the EGX100 lost 2.88%, as 195 stocks fell and only 18 rose. Selling built through the day in small and mid caps, led by speculative names: Cairo Poultry dropped nearly 20%. Telecom Egypt rose 5.2% and Edita 7.4% to hold up the main index. Turnover was EGP 11.15 billion, and market value fell about EGP 52 billion to EGP 4.15 trillion. Arab institutions were the only net sellers, at EGP 161.6 million; no outlet named a single trigger. Despite the day, the EGX30 is still up about 25% this year.

**Edita's record quarter:** Edita Food Industries posted record third-quarter revenue of EGP 7.3 billion, up 32.9% from a year earlier, though its gross margin narrowed to 32.9% from 35.0%. Net income reached EGP 938.1 million, up 41.5%, according to EnterpriseAM; Zawya put the rise in profit attributable to shareholders at 44%, so the exact growth rate is unconfirmed until the filing is checked.

**Bank and broker results:** Suez Canal Bank's nine-month net profit rose 12.9% to a record EGP 5.7 billion, as net interest income grew 42.9% to EGP 8.8 billion. Beltone Holding's consolidated revenue jumped 144% to EGP 21.6 billion, but its profit after minorities fell 32% to EGP 1.01 billion from EGP 1.49 billion, a reminder that revenue growth and profit can part ways.

**Gas from Mina West:** Shell began production at the Mina West gas field off the North Alexandria coast, three years after its discovery, with capacity of up to 160 million cubic feet a day. Shell holds 60% and a KUFPEC unit 40%. Every new field helps cut the costly LNG imports behind the gas rationing at fertilizer and steel plants.

**Holcim restarts a kiln line:** Holcim Egypt restarted the fifth production line at its Ain Sokhna cement plant after seven years idle, an investment of more than EGP 1.3 billion that creates 560 direct and indirect jobs.

**Pound and the IMF:** The central bank's dollar rate eased to EGP 53.75 on Monday from 53.86 on Sunday. Deutsche Bank Research expects the pound to trade between 48 and 60 to the dollar depending on the course of the Iran war, and sees the IMF approving Egypt's last tranches of about $2.3 billion before the program expires on December 15, EnterpriseAM reported. Fitch Solutions, by contrast, does not rule out a new IMF program next year.

**What it means for Egypt:** Higher US yields and a strong dollar narrow the gap foreign investors earn on Egyptian treasury bills, at about 26% for one year, and tend to pull money from emerging markets. Cheaper oil and gold cut Egypt's import bill and local gold prices.

> **Explained: carry trade.** Borrowing in a low-interest currency to invest in a higher-yielding one, such as Egyptian treasury bills, profiting from the gap as long as the exchange rate holds.

## Worth Knowing

**Equal-weight versus market-cap indexes: why one can fall 3.8% while the other stands still:** Most headline indexes, including the EGX30, weight each company by its market value, adjusted for the shares that trade freely. A giant such as Commercial International Bank therefore moves the index far more than a small food producer. An equal-weighted index, such as the EGX70 EWI, gives every member the same share, so a small company counts exactly as much as a large one.

Take a simple index of three stocks: one worth EGP 800 billion and two worth EGP 100 billion each. If the big one rises 1% and the two small ones fall 10%, a value-weighted index changes by (0.8 × 1%) + (0.1 × −10%) + (0.1 × −10%), a loss of 1.2%. The equal-weighted version averages the three moves: (1% − 10% − 10%) ÷ 3, a loss of 6.3%.

That is what Monday's session looked like: a few heavyweights such as Telecom Egypt held the EGX30 flat while broad selling hit small and mid caps. For investors, the gap between the two kinds of index is a quick gauge of market breadth: when the equal-weighted one lags badly, most stocks are falling even if the headline number looks calm.
