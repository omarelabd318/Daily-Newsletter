## Egypt News

**Tariff slabs under review:** The electricity ministry is studying three or four new models for household tariff slabs, Minister Mahmoud Esmat told the House energy committee on Monday, though no decision or date has been set. The options are to split the slabs differently, add new ones, or keep the system with new cut-off points. The current design means a home using 651 kWh a month instead of 649 can pay about EGP 400 more. An earlier proposal, charging each kWh at its own slab's rate, would have saved the ministry about EGP 17 billion without raising prices but would have lifted bills for homes using 300-400 kWh. Separately, Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly discussed incentives to cut power use and a target of 1,000 MW of solar plants serving about 7,000 factories. Any change to the slabs would reach almost every household bill in the country.

**University pay rise:** Faculty and staff at public universities and research centers will get raises of 55% to 65% on current income, depending on grade, phased over 18 months from November 2026 to July 2028, Finance Minister Ahmed Kouchouk said at a joint news conference with the higher education minister on Monday. The annual cost will exceed EGP 25 billion once the increases are fully in place, according to youlyou. Low academic pay has long driven staff abroad or into private universities, and the raise follows the private-sector minimum wage increase to EGP 8,500 from January.

**Trump on the Brotherhood:** President Donald Trump said Egypt "has clamped down on the Muslim Brotherhood at my request" and that he "might" widen the US terrorist designation of the group, in remarks to The Center Square on Monday before leaving for Asia. Asked about the Council on American-Islamic Relations, he said, "I'm not going to say that yet, but it could happen." Egypt designated the Brotherhood a terrorist organization permanently last week, and in January Washington listed its Egyptian, Jordanian and Lebanese branches as foreign terrorist organizations.

**Trade talks with India:** India and Egypt hope to start negotiating a preferential or free trade agreement within 90 days, Indian Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal said in the New Capital on Monday, at the first ministerial session of the countries' joint trade committee. The two sides also discussed India's first dedicated industrial zone in the Suez Canal Economic Zone, aimed at renewable energy, green hydrogen, pharmaceuticals and data centers, and an investment platform between GAFI and Invest India. Both aim to lift bilateral trade to $12 billion by 2030 from $6.5 billion.

**Saudi deployment:** Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said Egyptian troops have been in Saudi Arabia for months on a defensive mission to protect the kingdom and key sites, and that they "will not enter Yemen," CairoScene reported from the sidelines of a G7 meeting in Paris. He rejected Houthi claims that the deployment amounts to a declaration of war. On Monday he discussed de-escalation with his Saudi counterpart, Prince Faisal bin Farhan, by phone.

**Shipping returns to Suez:** Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd will route three more services of their Gemini alliance through the Suez Canal, with the first sailings from Asia on December 4, December 20 and January 14, according to a Maersk statement. The canal's revenue rose 94% from a year earlier to $724 million in October as transits climbed 32%, and every returning service adds hard currency for the state.

## Worth Knowing

**Beat, miss and guidance: why a stock can fall on record results:** When a big company reports, the market does not judge the numbers against last year but against what analysts expected, the "consensus" estimate built from dozens of forecasts. A result above it is a beat; below it, a miss. Because share prices already reflect that consensus, a company can post record revenue and still see its shares drop if the figure merely matches what was priced in.

Investors also weigh guidance: what management expects for the next quarter. Often it matters more than the quarter just ended, since a share price is a bet on future profits.

Take a hypothetical chipmaker expected to report revenue of $100 billion and guide to $110 billion for the next quarter. If it reports $103 billion, a beat of 3%, but guides to $108 billion, below the $110 billion investors hoped for, the stock can fall, because the outlook for future profits just got smaller. If it reports exactly $100 billion but guides to $120 billion, it may jump.

Traders also talk about a "whisper number," an unofficial and higher bar set by the most bullish investors. When expectations run that high, as they do for the AI leaders, even a clean beat can disappoint.
