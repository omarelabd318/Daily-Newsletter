## Egypt News

**IMF program nearly done:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi said Egypt's IMF program is nearing completion, citing 5.1% growth last fiscal year. He told the World Economic Forum's first country dialogue on Egypt, held in Cairo on November 16-17 with about 80 companies and lenders, that foreign reserves stood at about $57.5 billion at the end of October. He also set a target of 45% of electricity from renewables by 2028. No staff-level agreement on the eighth and final review, worth about $2.3 billion, has been announced, and the program expires on December 15. The International Monetary Fund wants about $1.5 billion booked from state asset sales by then, EnterpriseAM reported. Why it matters: the last tranche, and what follows the program, shape the pound and the state's borrowing costs into 2027.

**Price in DM banned:** Shops that hide prices online or in store behind "price in DM" or "price on request" are breaking the law and face fines of up to EGP 500,000, the Consumer Protection Agency warned in a statement shared by the Cabinet. The agency says it is monitoring stores and social media platforms. The rule hits the many small businesses that sell through Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp.

**Electricity law amended:** The House of Representatives gave final approval on Monday to amendments to the 2015 Electricity Law that add energy-efficiency duties and widen the powers of the Electricity Regulatory Agency, including a new monitoring unit. Producers and importers that fail to put efficiency labels on appliances face fines of EGP 200,000 to EGP 1 million and confiscation of the goods. An MP on the energy committee said the penalties target licensed companies, not households, and the changes do not touch tariffs.

**Antiquities haul seized:** Police arrested two men in Sharqia and Dakahlia with 3,391 Pharaonic, Greek and Roman artifacts, including rare coins, that they had dug up illegally and planned to sell, the Interior Ministry said.

**New Metro trains:** The first of 55 new air-conditioned Alstom trains entered passenger service on Cairo Metro Line 1 on Monday, under a €876 million contract that includes eight years of maintenance, CairoScene reported. The 44-kilometer line, the city's oldest, carries about 2.5 million riders a day.

**Tourism ranking:** Egypt ranked 63rd of 110 countries in the World Economic Forum's 2026 Travel and Tourism Development Index, down two places, but first on price competitiveness. The country received 16.2 million tourists through October against a target of 20 million for the year.

## Worth Knowing

**Ex-dividend day: why a share "falls" without losing value:** On Tuesday, Egypt Aluminium closed 5.2% lower, one of the EGX30's worst performers on paper. Almost none of that was a real loss. It was the stock's ex-dividend day, the first session in which a buyer no longer receives the declared payout.

A dividend is cash leaving the company for its shareholders. The moment the right to it detaches from the share, each share represents a company that is poorer by exactly that amount, so the exchange marks the reference price down by the dividend.

The arithmetic: Egypt Aluminium closed at EGP 326.99 on Monday and pays EGP 16 a share. The adjusted starting point for Tuesday was therefore 326.99 − 16 = EGP 310.99. It closed at 310.07, a real move of about 0.3%, not 5.2%. A shareholder who held through the date has EGP 310.07 in stock plus EGP 16 in cash due, about where they started.

Three practical points follow. Buying just before the ex-date to "collect" a dividend gains nothing, since the price drops by the payout and the cash may be taxed. Price charts that do not adjust for dividends exaggerate falls, which is why total-return figures add payouts back. And a stock with a big dividend can look weak on its ex-date while its owners are no worse off.
