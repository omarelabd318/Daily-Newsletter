## Egypt News

**IMF deal reached:** The IMF and Egypt reached a staff-level agreement on the final review of the country's loan program, which could release up to $2.3 billion. The deal covers the eighth and last review of the Extended Fund Facility, worth about $1.5 billion, and the third review of the Resilience and Sustainability Facility, worth up to about $800 million, both subject to approval by the IMF's executive board, which has not set a date. Payouts under the two would then reach about $9.6 billion. Both arrangements expire on December 15. Mission chief Amine Mati said exchange-rate flexibility "should remain the first line of defense" against external shocks, with intervention limited to excessive volatility, according to The National. Some outlets reported only the $1.5 billion loan tranche. Why it matters: the money closes a program that has shaped the pound, fuel prices and subsidies since 2022, and the board's approval would ease Egypt's borrowing costs going into 2027.

> **Explained: staff-level agreement.** A deal between IMF staff and a government on a program review; the money moves only after the IMF's executive board approves it, usually a formality weeks later.

**Growth slows to 4.9%:** Egypt's economy grew 4.9% year on year in July to September, the first quarter of fiscal 2026/27, down from 5.3% a year earlier but up from 4.7% in the previous quarter, the Planning Ministry told the Cabinet on Wednesday. Suez Canal activity rose 47.5% and oil refining 19.6%. Unemployment edged up to 6.0% from 5.8%, according to CAPMAS figures. Moody's expects 5.1% growth for the full fiscal year. Why it matters: growth near 5% supports jobs and tax revenue, but rising unemployment shows it is not yet reaching every household.

**Private-sector minimum wage:** The National Wages Council set the private-sector minimum wage at EGP 8,500 a month from January 1, 2027, up 21.4% from EGP 7,000, with a minimum annual raise of 3% (at least EGP 350) and an hourly floor of EGP 34. Council member Alaa El-Sakkaty said the figure includes in-kind benefits, putting the average cash wage nearer EGP 6,500, Al-Masry Al-Youm reported. The labor ministry has announced inspection campaigns to enforce it. Why it matters: the raise outpaces October's 12.7% inflation, though economists warn some of the cost may feed back into prices.

**Flights to Syria:** Egypt and Syria signed a civil aviation cooperation protocol on the sidelines of International Civil Aviation Organization meetings in Montreal, clearing the way for regular direct flights, halted since 2011. No airlines or start date have been announced. Why it matters: direct routes would cut travel times and costs for families and traders on both sides.

**Sadat honored in Washington:** The late President Anwar Sadat was posthumously awarded the US Congressional Gold Medal at a ceremony in the Capitol on Wednesday, an honor Congress approved in 2018. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty attended on behalf of President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi, who expressed his deep appreciation to Congress. Why it matters: Cairo used the occasion to press for a lasting peace in Gaza.

**Trade with Africa:** Egypt's trade with African Union countries rose by $1.1 billion to $8.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, from $7.1 billion a year earlier, official figures showed. Why it matters: Africa is a growing outlet for Egyptian exporters as the government courts new markets.

## Top World Headlines

**Shenzhen three-way talks:** Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin were due to hold their first three-way meeting on Thursday, on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Nuclear arms control, the Iran war and Ukraine top the agenda, according to AP. Why it matters: any understanding on Iran or Ukraine would ripple through oil prices and Gulf security.

**Kerch Bridge hit again:** Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea for the third time this week, and traffic was "temporarily suspended," Crimea's Moscow-appointed governor Sergei Aksyonov said on Thursday. A section collapsed in a strike on November 17. Why it matters: the bridge is Russia's main supply route to its forces in southern Ukraine.

**EU's €45 billion for Ukraine:** The European Commission proposed lending Ukraine a further €45 billion for 2027, up to €25 billion of it for defense, to be repaid only once Russia pays reparations. It would cover about two-thirds of Kyiv's estimated €70 billion funding gap and comes on top of an existing €90 billion loan. Why it matters: it keeps Ukraine financed as US support grows less certain.

**US-Venezuela military pact:** Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced a deal on intelligence sharing and joint training with Venezuela's interim government during an unannounced visit to Caracas on Wednesday, more than 10 months after Nicolás Maduro's capture. Interim President Delcy Rodríguez called it a "resumption of the technical-military relationship." Why it matters: it cements Washington's influence over a country with the world's largest proven oil reserves.

**Bukele strikes in Haiti:** El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele confirmed his forces bombed the mansion of Haitian gang leader Jeff Larose north of Port-au-Prince, calling it "my gift to the Haitian people." About 2,500 Salvadoran troops have been in Haiti since October; it is unclear whether Larose was inside. Why it matters: a foreign army is now waging open war on Haiti's gangs.

**Alaska flips:** Democrat Mary Peltola won Alaska's Senate race with 50.1% of first-choice votes, the ninth Republican seat Democrats took in the November 3 midterms, their biggest gain since 1958. Democrats will hold 56 of 100 seats from January. Why it matters: the margin gives Democrats firm control over confirmations and treaties.

**Siberia plane crash:** A Russian Antonov An-12 military transport crashed in the Kemerovo region of western Siberia on Tuesday, killing all 19 people on board, the Defense Ministry said, citing preliminary signs of equipment failure. Why it matters: it is the latest in a string of losses of aging Soviet-era aircraft.

## Worth Knowing

**IPO price stabilization: the safety net under a new listing:** When a company lists, its banks usually appoint a stabilization manager, armed with extra shares and cash from the offering, to support the price for the first few weeks. If the share falls below the offer price, the manager buys in the market, or, under Egypt's rules, stands ready to buy shares back from retail investors at the offer price until the window closes.

MNT-Halan shows how this works. Its shares were sold at EGP 24.50 but traded at EGP 21.70 by Wednesday. Retail investors who took the buyback offered 20.65 million shares, receiving about EGP 506 million, instead of selling at the market price and losing EGP 2.80 a share, or 11.4%.

The mechanism protects small investors against an immediate slump, but it is time-limited and capped. Once the window closes, the share trades on its own merits. A large take-up, as here, also signals that many early holders lacked confidence in the price, which can weigh on the stock afterward. For anyone buying into an IPO, the length of the stabilization window, and what happens when it ends, are worth checking in the prospectus.
