*Week of November 15 to 21, 2026: an IMF deal that closes Egypt's loan program, a higher minimum wage, a widening trade gap, and the worst week on Wall Street since 2020.*

## Since Friday

**Wall Street's rout deepens:** US stocks had their worst week since 2020 on Friday, when the S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq 5.28% to 22,617.73 and the Dow 1.93% to 48,147.33. The S&P 500 and the Dow closed in correction territory, the S&P's seventh straight fall was its longest losing run since February 2020, and the Nasdaq erased its gain for the year, CNN reported. Nvidia lost 14% to $125.53 after a Reuters report that enforcement staff at the Securities and Exchange Commission are examining its accounting, taking its loss on the week to about 23%.

**Missiles over Riyadh:** The Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed three ballistic missiles fired by the Houthis toward Riyadh early on Saturday, with no casualties reported. It was the second attack on the Saudi capital in three days, after a Houthi missile damaged the PIF Tower, home of the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund, on November 19.

**Russian missiles in Poland:** Two Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in Poland during an overnight Russian attack on Ukraine on November 20, Poland's defense minister said, though some reports count one missile. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the incursion did not appear accidental, and NATO's Mark Rutte called it a "grave violation" of Polish airspace. Poland asked allies for more air defenses but did not invoke NATO's Article 4.

**Aswan Forum opens:** President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi opened the sixth Aswan Forum for Sustainable Peace and Development on Saturday, a two-day meeting on Africa's place in a shifting world order, saying the continent must be a full partner in drafting the rules of the international system, Youm7 reported.

**Weather:** Cairo is cloudy today with a high of 29°C and light winds, turning cooler tomorrow, with highs of 24-27°C and no rain expected.

**What to watch today:** Tottenham, with Omar Marmoush, host Ipswich at 5pm Cairo time and Newcastle meet Arsenal at 7:30pm in the Premier League; Real Madrid play Celta Vigo at 10pm; the ATP Finals semifinals in Turin, including Carlos Alcaraz against Ben Shelton; and the Aswan Forum's opening day.

## The Week in Brief

1. **IMF deal:** Egypt and the IMF reached a staff-level agreement on the final reviews of its loan program, worth about $2.3 billion, with board approval still to come.
2. **Minimum wage:** The private-sector minimum wage will rise 21% to EGP 8,500 a month from January 2027.
3. **Growth and trade:** Growth slowed to 4.9% in the first fiscal quarter, while the September trade deficit widened 60.6% as imports rose nearly 30%.
4. **Suez Canal:** October revenue jumped 94% to $724 million, and transit-fee discounts of up to 75% were extended to mid-2027.
5. **Gas for fertilizer:** The price of gas for fertilizer plants rose 50% to $12.75 per mmBtu until the end of December.
6. **Libya and Turkey:** Egypt signed gas, fisheries and labor agreements with Libya and called the Turkey-Libya maritime memorandum null.
7. **Houthis and Riyadh:** The Houthis pushed toward Aden and struck Riyadh's PIF Tower, as Saudi Arabia replaced its Yemen commander.
8. **Shenzhen summit:** Trump, Xi and Putin signed a four-point declaration in their first three-way talks, with no deal on Ukraine or Iran.
9. **AI selloff:** Nvidia beat forecasts but fell 23% on the week, Anthropic raised a record $81.3 billion in its listing, and the Nasdaq lost 10%.
10. **Gold slumps:** Gold fell about 9.5% to a 14-month low as Fed rate-hike bets firmed, taking 21-karat gold in Egypt down 8.5%.

## What Tied the Week Together

The week split Egypt's story from the world's. At home, the pieces of the end of the IMF program fell into place: a staff-level deal worth about $2.3 billion, a president declaring the program nearly done, and decisions that pass costs and gains along, from a 21% higher minimum wage to a 50% rise in the price of gas for fertilizer plants. Yet the figures behind them showed the strain the program leaves unresolved. Growth slowed to 4.9%, imports rose nearly 30% in September, and external debt service reached $38.2 billion last fiscal year, so the canal's 94% revenue rebound and the new gas from Mina West matter as sources of dollars as much as of growth.

Abroad, the AI trade broke. Nvidia beat forecasts and still lost 23% on the week, Anthropic's record listing could not lift the market, and the Nasdaq fell 10%. Fed minutes pointing to a December rate rise strengthened the dollar, which pulled gold to a 14-month low and took 21-karat gold in Egypt down 8.5%, the most direct effect on households. The EGX30 lost only 1.1%, but smaller companies fell about 5.5% and the pound weakened slightly as foreign investors sold.

Going into next week, the IMF board date is still unset with the program expiring on December 15, the Houthis are pressing on Aden and the Red Sea route, and markets wait to see whether US inflation data and Thanksgiving trading slow the selloff.

## Egypt This Week

**The IMF program's last step:** IMF staff and Egypt reached a staff-level agreement on November 18 on the eighth and final review of the Extended Fund Facility, worth about $1.5 billion, and the third review of the Resilience and Sustainability Facility, worth up to about $800 million. The fund's executive board must still approve the deal, and no date has been set; both arrangements expire on December 15. A day earlier, President Sisi said the program was "approaching completion" and put growth for the 2025/26 fiscal year at 5.1%. Growth then slowed to 4.9% year on year in the July-September quarter, from 5.3% a year earlier, EnterpriseAM reported.

**Wages up, bills under review:** The National Wages Council raised the private-sector minimum wage to EGP 8,500 a month from EGP 7,000, effective January 1, 2027, though a small-business representative said the figure counts in-kind benefits. University staff pay is to rise 55% to 65% between November 2026 and July 2028, the finance minister said. The electricity ministry is studying three or four new models for household tariff slabs, with no decision yet; under the current system, a home moving from 649 to 651 kilowatt-hours a month can pay about EGP 400 more.

**A wider trade gap and heavy debt service:** The trade deficit widened 60.6% to $5.3 billion in September as imports rose 29.7% to $10.6 billion and exports only 7.4%, according to CAPMAS figures reported by Arab Finance. The central bank's bulletin showed Egypt paid $38.2 billion in external debt service in the year to June 2026, $30.4 billion of it principal and $7.8 billion interest.

> **Explained: debt service.** The money a country pays each year on what it owes abroad: repayments of the loans themselves (principal) plus the interest on them.

**Energy, gas and the canal:** Gas for fertilizer plants now costs $12.75 per mmBtu, up 50% from $8.50, from November 16 to December 31, in return for the right to export full output, Abu Qir Fertilizers disclosed. The Mina West gas field began producing two months early at 160 million cubic feet a day, and AMEA Power signed $850 million in deals with Chinese partners for a 500-megawatt wind farm at Ras Shukeir and Egypt's first wind-turbine factory. Suez Canal revenue rose 94% to $724 million in October from $373 million a year earlier, and the canal extended discounts of 10% to 75% for 11 vessel types to June 30, 2027.

**Libya, India and Saudi Arabia:** Egypt and Libya signed agreements on gas infrastructure, fisheries and labor during Libyan Prime Minister Abdulhamid Dbeibah's visit on November 19, a day after Cairo called the Turkey-Libya maritime memorandum "null" and not binding on Egypt. India's commerce minister said the two countries hope to open talks on a trade agreement within 90 days, aiming for $12 billion a year in trade by 2030 from about $6.5 billion. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty confirmed that Egyptian forces have been in Saudi Arabia for months on a defensive mission and "will not enter Yemen", CairoScene reported.

**Prices and listings:** The Consumer Protection Agency warned that sellers who hide prices behind "DM for price" posts face fines of up to EGP 500,000 or the value of the goods, whichever is higher, under existing law. Misr Life Insurance plans to sell 750 million existing shares, 15% of its capital, on the EGX in December, pending approvals, with no price or date set.

## Region and World

**Houthis close in on Aden:** Houthi forces were about 80 kilometers north and 110 kilometers west of Aden by November 20, according to Reuters, after government forces left positions on the Bab el-Mandeb strait; Saudi Arabia replaced its Yemen coalition commander on November 16. The coalition struck missile sites in Hodeidah, and the Houthis threatened Saudi ports. For Egypt, a Houthi-held Bab el-Mandeb would threaten the Red Sea route that feeds the Suez Canal's recovery.

**The Iran war, without talks:** Iran claimed drone strikes on US radar sites in Bahrain and a US base in Kuwait during the week; US Central Command said the November 17 attack was intercepted with no damage. The US Senate rejected a resolution to end US involvement in the war 51-48 on November 19, and Trump said Tehran wanted a deal, though not one he would accept. Tankers were hit off Khasab, Oman, near the Strait of Hormuz on November 20 and 21, gCaptain and Iran International reported.

**Gaza's truce strained:** Israeli tanks crossed the "yellow line" near Nuseirat and Bureij in central Gaza on November 18, the first announced ground operation past the boundary since the October 2025 ceasefire, targeting a tunnel, and Israel said the operation ended the next day. The US-backed Board of Peace had warned the UN Security Council on November 17 that the truce "may be on the brink of collapse".

**Three leaders in Shenzhen:** Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held their first three-way talks on November 19 at the APEC summit and signed a four-point declaration on UN reform, oil supply, nuclear risk reduction and the International Criminal Court that does not mention Ukraine or Iran. Days earlier, Ukraine's drones and missiles brought down a road span of the Kerch bridge to Crimea; engineers cited by The New York Times estimated two years to restore road traffic.

**Britain edges back toward Europe:** At a summit in Brussels on November 20, Prime Minister Andy Burnham and EU leaders agreed to scrap most checks on British food exports from 2028 in return for the UK following some EU rules, link emissions trading and open a youth scheme for 100,000 under-30s each way. Britain's Treasury estimates the deals could add up to £17 billion a year to the economy, and Burnham said closer ties would be explored, the BBC reported.

**America's new Senate:** Democrat Mary Peltola won Alaska's Senate race on November 17 with 50.1% of first-choice votes, the ninth Republican seat Democrats took in the November 3 midterms, their biggest gain since 1958, leaving them with 56 seats from January.

## Markets This Week

Egypt's market held up far better than the world's. The EGX30 slipped 1.1% between the Thursday closes to 51,458.04, but smaller companies fell hard: the equal-weighted EGX70 lost about 5.5%, and foreign investors were net sellers on Thursday, Zawya reported. The central bank's dollar rate weakened about 0.7% to EGP 53.88, and the one-year treasury bill yield rose to 26.16% at the November 19 auction from 26.07%. Abroad, the AI trade cracked: Nvidia beat forecasts with quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, then fell 23% on the week, and Taiwan's market had its worst week since 1991. Minutes of the Federal Reserve's October meeting showed nearly all officials expecting a rate rise at the December 8-9 meeting, which futures now price at about 95%, lifting the dollar and pushing gold to its lowest in 14 months. Brent fell for a third week as exports through Hormuz recovered.
