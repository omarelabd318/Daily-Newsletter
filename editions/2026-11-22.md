## Egypt News

**Idle wells back online:** The Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation signed a framework agreement with US oilfield services firm Weatherford on Friday to bring about 100 shut-in wells, onshore and offshore, back into production within 18 months. The deal uses AI-based analysis to choose repairs that need no drilling rig; no output target or contract value was given. That pace, about five or six wells a month, compares with 591 wells brought onstream in the two years to June. More local oil means fewer costly fuel imports, which weigh on the dollar bill.

**Aswan Forum:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi called for "serious reform of the international financial system" to give developing countries long-term affordable finance and lighter debt, in a recorded address to the sixth Aswan Forum for Sustainable Peace and Development, which runs to Sunday. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty signed cooperation agreements with Sudan and the Democratic Republic of Congo on the sidelines and warned that a growing foreign military presence in the Red Sea could fuel regional tensions. Talks with Sudan and Eritrea ended with a joint line that Red Sea security belongs to the countries on its shores.

**Experts stay in Libya:** State Information Minister Diaa Rashwan said on Sunday that reports of Egypt withdrawing its military experts from Benghazi over differences with Khalifa Haftar's forces were "completely baseless." The denial follows the signing of gas and labor deals with Libya's Tripoli-based government on November 19 and Cairo's rejection of the Turkey-Libya maritime pact.

**Haier expands:** Prime Minister Moustafa Madbouly opened the second phase of Haier's industrial complex in 10th of Ramadan City, where the Chinese appliance maker has invested $70 million, according to Egypt Independent, which said the line makes refrigerators and freezers for export to more than 30 countries. The Egyptian Gazette described the $70 million line as air-conditioning equipment, so the product mix is unconfirmed. Plants like this earn dollars through exports and replace imported appliances.

**EgyptAir returns to Syria:** EgyptAir will resume direct flights to Damascus from December 4 and to Aleppo from December 6, restoring direct links between the two capitals.

**Renewables target:** Electricity Minister Mahmoud Esmat said Egypt aims to raise renewables to 42% of its generating capacity, which will require upgrades to the grid and to storage, Egypt Independent reported; no target year was given. Each gigawatt of solar or wind frees natural gas that would otherwise be burned in power plants, gas Egypt now partly imports.

## Worth Knowing

**Dividend yield vs a bank certificate:** Misr Life's chief executive framed his listing against a 28% certificate of deposit, and the comparison shows how investors weigh a share against a safe deposit.

A share's dividend yield is the yearly dividend divided by the share price. Take a hypothetical insurer earning EGP 5 billion a year that pays out 70%, or EGP 3.5 billion. If the whole company is valued at EGP 35 billion, the dividend yield is 10%: an investor putting EGP 100,000 into the shares would receive about EGP 10,000 a year, against EGP 28,000 from a 28% certificate.

On that arithmetic alone the certificate wins. A share buyer is betting on two other things: that profits and dividends grow, and that the share price rises as interest rates fall. If certificate rates drop to 18%, a 10% yield that is growing looks far better, and the share price usually rises to reflect it.

The certificate's return is fixed and the capital is safe; the share's is neither. That is why companies listing in a high-rate market stress payout ratios and rate cuts, and why investors compare the dividend yield plus expected growth, not the yield alone, with the deposit rate.
