## Egypt News

**LNG cargoes turn away:** Three liquefied natural gas tankers bound for Egyptian terminals changed course in recent days, two of them heading to Europe, where buyers are paying more, EnterpriseAM reported, citing Bloomberg ship-tracking data compiled with Kpler. The Qassar turned west after reaching Port Said, the Amaryllis Knutsen swung around near the coast and the Greenergy Wind turned south in the mid-Atlantic toward Asia. A single cargo now costs about $100 million; the government asked suppliers for about 10 cargoes in November, down from 12 in October, and has deferred five of the 15 it contracted into 2027. Gas supply to fertilizer plants is already cut by 20% to 40%. The diversions matter because imported gas keeps power stations running and factories supplied as local output lags demand.

**Phone prices:** Prime Minister Moustafa Madbouly ordered a government working group on Sunday to prepare a full study of the mobile phone market after what officials described as excessive price rises, with mechanisms to control prices and to tie incentives for manufacturers to the share of locally made components. Cabinet spokesman Mohamed Adel said the government is working on two tracks at once: supporting local industry and keeping devices affordable. No price targets or deadlines were announced. Phones have become one of the most visible markers of the cost of living as the pound weakens and import costs rise.

**Iraqi prime minister in Cairo:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi receives Iraqi Prime Minister Ali Falih al-Zaidi on Monday, on his first official visit to Egypt since taking office, with joint committee meetings to follow. Iraq's ambassador, Qahtan Taha Khalaf, said the agenda covers energy, electricity, transport, trade and investment, and that bilateral agreements are due to be signed. Iraqi crude supplies and the long-planned electricity link make Baghdad one of Cairo's more important economic partners in the region.

**Renewables target:** Egypt aims to reach 31,905 megawatts of installed renewable capacity by 2030, backed by 14,420 megawatt-hours of battery storage, according to a review of projects by Madbouly and Electricity Minister Mahmoud Esmat on Sunday. The plan is central to cutting the fuel import bill that the LNG diversions above make more expensive.

**EU pesticide rules:** Egyptian food exporters have until January 29, 2027 to meet lower European Union limits on pesticide residues, EnterpriseAM reported. Pesticide residues already account for 81% of the 91 EU alerts raised on Egyptian shipments from January to August, with nine more in September. Citrus, strawberries and vegetables to Europe are among Egypt's largest farm exports, so tighter rules put a premium on testing and traceability on farms.

**Consumer finance contracts:** The Financial Regulatory Authority issued two standard contracts for consumer finance customers, capping late-payment charges at the unpaid principal, and gave companies three months after publication in the Official Gazette to comply. The rules cover the installment plans that millions of households now use to buy appliances, phones and cars, and aim to make terms clearer and comparable between lenders.

## Top World Headlines

**COP31 collapses:** The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended on Sunday without an agreed outcome after two days of overtime, the longest such summit on record. The European Union and Pacific island states rejected a final draft that dropped any mention of fossil fuels, and a $120 billion adaptation finance target fell away. The failure leaves climate finance for developing countries, Egypt among them, without new commitments.

**Burnham's EU deal:** British Prime Minister Andy Burnham tells MPs on Monday that the package agreed with the European Union in Brussels on Friday is "the single biggest growth measure to be delivered in this parliament." Treasury analysis says it will add up to 0.5% to GDP; GB News reported a £17 billion annual gain by 2040, a figure that rests on that one outlet.

**Strike on Zelensky's hometown:** A Russian missile and drone attack on Kryvyi Rih on Saturday killed a seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old emergency worker and wounded about 60 people, including six children. Drones struck again as rescuers arrived, a pattern Ukrainian officials call a "triple tap."

**Baltic cargo ship:** A sanctioned Russian cargo ship hit by drones in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday was towed out of Finnish waters on Sunday after its 16 crew were evacuated. Finland's border guard blamed Ukraine, and President Alexander Stubb said the burning ship posed a "serious danger."

**Genocide ruling:** Rohingya refugees welcomed the International Court of Justice's ruling of November 19 that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, in the case brought by Gambia. Human Rights Watch said it is the first time the court has found a state responsible for genocide; the judgment is final and binding.

**Indonesia's vice presidency:** President Prabowo Subianto submits two candidates for vice president to parliament on Monday, after the resignation of Gibran Rakabuming Raka. Legal questions over how a vacancy is filled have clouded the choice in the world's third-largest democracy.

**Delta delays:** More than 1,600 Delta Air Lines flights were delayed on Sunday after one of its weight-and-balance systems failed, prompting a ground stop in Atlanta at the start of the Thanksgiving travel week. Delta said the outage was not a hack.

## Worth Knowing

**Market breadth: reading beneath the index.** An index such as the EGX30 tracks a fixed basket of large companies, weighted by size, so a handful of heavyweights can carry it up or down. Market breadth looks at the whole market instead: how many stocks rose against how many fell, and how much was traded doing it.

The simplest measure is the advance-decline ratio. Suppose 120 stocks rise and 80 fall on a day the index climbs 1%: breadth confirms the move, since most stocks are taking part. Now suppose the index rises 1% but only 40 stocks gain while 160 fall: a few large names are masking broad weakness, and analysts often treat such rallies as fragile.

Sunday's session on the EGX showed the opposite extreme. The EGX30 fell 2.85%, but the equal-weighted EGX70, where every small and mid-sized company counts the same, fell 5.55%, and only 4 stocks rose against 205 that fell. That pattern points to indiscriminate selling, often by individual investors cutting positions or meeting margin calls, rather than a judgment on any one company. Breadth that stays this weak for several sessions is a warning sign; a sharp swing back to broad gains, by contrast, often marks the point where panic selling has run its course.
