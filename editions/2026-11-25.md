## Egypt News

**Suez Canal revenue doubles:** Suez Canal revenue reached $593 million from the start of November, against $295 million in the same period of 2025, a 101% rise, the Suez Canal Authority said. Transits rose to 1,194 vessels from 886 and net tonnage nearly doubled to 72 million tons. Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd plan to bring three joint Gemini services back through the canal gradually from December to mid-January, and authority chairman Osama Rabie met German Ambassador Jürgen Schulz on Tuesday to discuss cooperation. The canal was among Egypt's largest sources of hard currency before Red Sea attacks diverted ships around Africa, so a sustained return of the big container lines would ease pressure on the pound.

**Phone prices:** Mobile phone prices in Egypt have risen about 60% since January, while sales have fallen 25% to 30%, according to Mohamed Talaat, head of the mobile division at the Federation of Chambers of Commerce. "The market does not suffer a shortage of supply, but a recession," he told Annahar. Traders blame global memory-chip shortages driven by AI demand, shipping costs and taxes, including retroactive tax claims on imported handsets. Prime Minister Moustafa Madbouly met ministers and customs officials on Sunday and ordered a study of the market and more local production. The 60% is a trade-body estimate, not an official price index.

**Sudan's prime minister in Cairo:** President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi received Sudanese Prime Minister Kamil Idris on Tuesday, who delivered a message from army chief Abdel Fattah al-Burhan. Sisi directed officials to study "positively" allowing Sudanese prisoners in Egypt to serve the rest of their sentences in Sudan, and Idris said Khartoum is counting on Egyptian companies to rebuild its infrastructure, energy and transport networks. Egypt hosts the largest community of Sudanese who fled the war, and reconstruction contracts could become a sizable opening for Egyptian contractors.

**Migrant boat sinks off Benghazi:** About 27 Egyptians, most from Qalyubia governorate, were aboard a migrant boat that sank off Benghazi in eastern Libya on November 5, the Foreign Ministry said on Tuesday. Seven were rescued and three bodies have been identified; the ministry did not give a final toll and said it is working with Libyan authorities to repatriate the dead. Eastern Libya remains a main departure point for Egyptians attempting the crossing to Europe.

**Competition authority's new powers:** The Egyptian Competition Authority has referred three cartel cases to prosecutors since September, covering suppliers to Alexandria University tenders, contractors on National Media Authority tenders and board members of a Red Sea shipping cooperative accused of fixing freight rates, EnterpriseAM reported. It also refused a settlement offered by a food company. An amended competition law, passed by the House in April and awaiting ratification, would let the authority's board impose fines itself and raise the cap for cartels to 15% of product revenue, from 2% to 12%.

**Green hydrogen pipeline narrows:** Twenty companies have withdrawn from or let lapse their green hydrogen agreements with Egypt, including seven of the nine framework deals signed around COP27, leaving a handful of projects, led by Egypt Green's 100-megawatt plant, still moving. The retreat follows a global pullback from green hydrogen as costs stayed high and buyers proved scarce, and it dims hopes that the Suez Canal Economic Zone would become an export hub for the fuel.

**3 million jobs target:** Planning Minister Ahmed Rostom outlined a National Economic Transformation Programme that targets 3 million new jobs through five sectors: manufacturing, agriculture, communications and IT, tourism and energy. These sectors account for about 40% of jobs and of GDP, and the program is due to be finalized by the end of December. No timeframe was given for the jobs target.

## Top World Headlines

**Russia's war dead:** At least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died in the war in Ukraine since 2022, according to leaked records from Russia's statistics agency, Rosstat, analyzed by the BBC and the independent outlet Mediazona. The death certificates run to the end of August 2026 and exclude the missing; Mediazona's own estimate including them is about 700,000. Moscow has never published an official toll, and the figure is far above anything it has acknowledged. The strikes go on: a Russian attack on Odesa killed two people, including a 17-year-old, Ukrainian officials said.

**China's purge reaches the politburo:** Chen Jining, the 62-year-old Communist Party chief of Shanghai and a politburo member, is under investigation for "suspected severe violations of discipline and laws," the state news agency Xinhua said on Tuesday. He is the fourth politburo member to be investigated since 2025, extending President Xi Jinping's anti-corruption drive to the top tier of the party.

**Pakistan's first astronaut:** China's Shenzhou-24 lifted off on Wednesday with three astronauts, including Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, Pakistan's first astronaut and the first foreigner on a Chinese crewed mission. The flight marks China's opening of its Tiangong space station to partner countries, a role long dominated by the US-led International Space Station.

**Cartagena hostages:** Masked gunmen took about 25 people hostage in a supermarket in Cartagena, one of Colombia's busiest tourist cities, on Tuesday after a failed robbery of an armored truck. Several hostages, including elderly women, were freed during negotiations. President Abelardo de la Espriella called the attackers "common criminals."

**Trump's hush-money case:** A federal appeals court on Monday gave President Donald Trump a new route to overturn his 2024 hush-money conviction, sending the case back toward federal court, where he can seek dismissal on presidential immunity grounds. A district judge had refused to take the case three times.

**North Korea expels Swiss diplomat:** North Korea ordered the head of Switzerland's diplomatic office in Pyongyang to leave within 48 hours, over Swiss sanctions on a North Korean children's camp, weeks after the office reopened. Switzerland is one of the few Western countries that keep a channel to Pyongyang.

## Worth Knowing

**How an IPO book fills:** In a share offering, "coverage" compares the shares investors ask for with the shares on offer. A tranche of 37.5 million shares that draws orders for 7.5 million is covered 0.2 times; orders for 75 million would make it twice covered, or oversubscribed.

Coverage matters because it sets the price and the first days of trading. In a book-built offering, banks collect institutional bids at different prices within the range. Suppose bids for 1.5 billion shares arrive for a 750 million-share offer: the book is twice covered, and if most bids sit at the top of the range, the seller can price there. If demand is thin, the price lands at the bottom, or the offer is cut or pulled.

Retail investors often wait until the last days, since orders can usually be placed until the close and money tied up early earns nothing. A light first day says little; the final count matters more. Heavy oversubscription often signals a first-day rise, because unfilled investors buy on the market, but it is no guarantee. For a buyer, the key questions are the price against the company's fair value, the dividend policy and what the seller does with the shares it keeps.
