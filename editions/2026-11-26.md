## Egypt News

**Ring Road fire:** A truck carrying petroleum products overturned and caught fire on Cairo's Ring Road near Al-Warraq in Giza on Wednesday evening, killing 31 people and injuring 21, the Health Ministry said. The load exploded after the driver lost control and hit a concrete barrier, a security source said, and prosecutors said about 27 cars and motorcycles burned. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly ordered an urgent investigation and compensation for the families, without naming sums, and the Transport Ministry denied claims that a power cable caused the blaze. Traffic reopened overnight after 15 fire engines put out the fire. It is one of the deadliest road disasters in Egypt in years, and it puts fresh attention on how hazardous loads are moved through the capital's busiest roads.

**IMF final review:** Egypt and the International Monetary Fund have reached a staff-level agreement on the eighth and final review of the country's loan program, Madbouly said on Wednesday. Once the IMF board approves it in December, the deal would release about $2.3 billion: about $1.5 billion from the Extended Fund Facility and up to $800 million from the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. The program expires on December 15; no successor arrangement has been announced. The money adds to reserves of $57.5 billion and keeps a seal of approval that foreign investors in Egyptian debt watch closely.

> **Explained: staff-level agreement.** A deal between IMF staff and a government on the terms of a review. It is not final until the IMF's executive board approves it, which is when the money is released.

**Maersk returns to Suez:** Maersk chief executive Vincent Clerc told President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi on Wednesday that the shipping group has resumed sailings through the Suez Canal and plans to bring its full network back to the Red Sea route "in the coming months." No date was given. At the same meeting, Norway's Scatec set out plans to invest about $5 billion in Egypt over two years. A full return by the world's second-largest container line would add to a recovery in canal revenue, a major source of foreign currency, though fresh Houthi attacks in the Red Sea remain a risk.

**Industrial finance widened:** The Cabinet opened its EGP 30 billion subsidized industrial finance facility to working capital, splitting it into EGP 15 billion for raw materials and inputs and EGP 15 billion for machinery, at a 15% rate for five years, EnterpriseAM reported. The cap for strategic industries doubles to EGP 200 million from EGP 100 million. With bank lending rates above 20%, the scheme gives factories a cheaper way to fund daily operations, not just new equipment.

**Dutch Embassy closes:** The Dutch Embassy in Cairo closed to the public on November 24 until further notice. Egyptian Streets said the closure followed fresh protests outside the mission over the detention in the Netherlands of activist Ahmed Nasser, organized by the Egyptian Youth Union Abroad.

**Egypt and Sudan on the Nile:** Egypt and Sudan said in a joint statement that Ethiopia's Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, run without a binding agreement, remains a threat to downstream countries, and called on Addis Ababa to change its unilateral policy, at the end of Sudanese Prime Minister Kamil Idris's visit to Cairo. Egypt also said it was ready to take every step allowed under international law and the two countries' 1976 joint defense agreement to protect Sudan, and both rejected naval access to the Red Sea for countries that do not border it.

## Top World Headlines

**New US tariffs:** The US Trade Representative proposed tariffs under Section 301 of US trade law on 14 trading partners on Wednesday, after the Supreme Court's February ruling against earlier duties. Proposed rates run to 20% for China, with a 40% alternative, 15% for the EU, Japan and South Korea and 35% for Vietnam, according to trade law firm Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg and the BBC; hearings are set for January 25, so no duties take effect before then. Markets read the plan as milder than feared.

**Huawei convicted:** A federal jury in Brooklyn found China's Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiring to steal trade secrets from US companies and bank fraud tied to hiding its business in Iran; it was acquitted of racketeering. Sentencing is set for September 2027. The verdict lands as Washington and Beijing spar over tariffs and chip exports.

**COP31 collapses:** The UN climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended about 49 hours late without an agreement. Australian minister Chris Bowen, who led the negotiations, blamed "unbridgeable red lines," and a $120 billion climate finance goal was dropped. Pope Leo XIV called the collapse "worrying." For poorer countries such as Egypt, it means no new commitment of money to adapt to heat and water stress.

**UK-EU treaties:** The British government is due to publish the texts of two new treaties with the EU on Thursday afternoon, including a food and farm trade deal. The food deal has a 10-year termination clause, and ending it would mean paying to rebuild border checks. It is the biggest step in rebuilding trade ties since Brexit.

**Russia's war dead:** More than 720,000 Russian soldiers may have been killed since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, roughly one every four minutes, according to a projection by the independent outlet Mediazona based on leaked official data, CNN and NBC News reported. Moscow does not publish its losses. The figure is an estimate, well above the deaths counted directly in the leaked records.

**Hong Kong fire, a year on:** Hong Kong marked a year since the Wang Fuk Court blaze, which killed 168 people when scaffolding and netting on a housing estate under renovation caught fire. Human Rights Watch called for accountability and an end to the suppression of criticism, while the Hong Kong Free Press questioned whether the government's overhaul of building works goes far enough.

## Worth Knowing

**How an IPO price range works:** When a company lists on the stock exchange, it rarely names a single price at the start. It sets a range and runs a book-building: institutional investors, such as funds and banks, say how many shares they want and at what price, while retail investors usually subscribe at whatever final price is set.

Take Misr Life Insurance's offering of 750 million shares at EGP 12.00 to 13.60 each. At the bottom of the range the sale raises EGP 9.0 billion; at the top, EGP 10.2 billion. Because the shares are 15% of the company, the range also values the whole insurer at EGP 60 billion to EGP 68 billion.

Where the price lands depends on demand. If funds put in orders for several times the shares on offer, a measure called oversubscription, the price tends to be set at the top of the range or higher. Weak demand pushes it to the bottom, or the sale is cut or delayed.

For a buyer, the range is a first signal of value: compare the implied valuation with the company's profits and with listed peers. A price set near the top shows strong demand, but it also leaves less room for gains once trading begins.
