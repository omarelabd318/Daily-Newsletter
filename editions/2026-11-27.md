## Egypt News

**Ring Road fire suspect:** Prosecutors detained the driver of a private car accused of causing Wednesday's Ring Road fire, which killed 31 people. The man was ordered held for four days pending investigation. According to the prosecution, he admitted driving at about 110 km/h, weaving between lanes and hitting the fuel tanker from behind on the Ring Road near Al-Warraq in Giza, setting off a blaze that engulfed nearby vehicles. The Health Ministry put the final toll at 31 dead and 26 injured, up from 21 injured in earlier counts. Prosecutors also ordered a drug test, and forensic teams have taken DNA samples from families, since most of the bodies were too badly burned to identify. One outlet, Al Manassa, carried a different account in which the tanker crossed the median. Opposition politicians have demanded accountability and tighter rules on hazardous cargo. The crash is among the deadliest road disasters in Egypt in years and has put truck safety on the Ring Road, one of Greater Cairo's busiest arteries, back on the agenda.

**Warraq relief fund:** Donations for the victims' families reached about EGP 200 million within six to seven hours of an appeal, cabinet spokesman Mohamed Adel said on Thursday. The central bank directed banks to contribute EGP 60 million, and businesses, aviation companies and charities added the rest. Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly asked Social Solidarity Minister Maya Morsy to arrange exceptional compensation, including investment certificates and replacement cars, though no amount per family has been set. A dedicated bank account is open for public donations.

**Qatari emir in Cairo:** Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani met President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi in Cairo on Thursday, with talks covering the Gaza ceasefire, Gulf security and economic ties. Sisi stressed raising bilateral trade, widening investment and closer cooperation in energy, while the emir wrote on X about "the importance of continuing consultations and coordinating joint efforts to address current challenges." No new investment figure was announced. Qatar is one of Egypt's largest Gulf investors and a key LNG supplier as Cairo competes with Europe for cargoes this winter.

**Antiquities fees to rise:** Entry tickets at key archaeological sites will rise from April 1, 2027, after the Supreme Council of Antiquities' board approved new prices on November 9, Egypt Independent reported. Tiers seen so far run to EGP 300 and EGP 500 for foreign visitors, with half price for foreign students, and EGP 40 for Egyptians. Separately, Aswan's local fee for non-Egyptian visitors to Abu Simbel rises to EGP 70 from EGP 2. The increases aim to raise revenue for site upkeep as tourist numbers grow.

**Reviving shut-in wells:** The state's Egyptian General Petroleum Corporation signed a framework with US oilfield services firm Weatherford to bring about 100 shut-in oil and gas wells back into production within 18 months. Higher local output would trim Egypt's costly fuel and LNG imports at a time when cargoes are being outbid by European buyers.

**Consumer finance rules:** New standard consumer finance contracts cap late-payment fees and require that monthly installments stay below 50% of a borrower's income, EnterpriseAM reported. The rules matter for the growing number of households buying appliances, phones and cars on installment as prices rise.

## Worth Knowing

**What a 26% treasury bill really pays:** A headline yield is the starting point, not the return an investor keeps. Two things eat into it: tax and inflation, and for a foreign investor, the exchange rate too.

**An example:** An Egyptian saver puts EGP 100,000 into a one-year bill at 26.35%. Before tax, that earns about EGP 26,350. Egypt withholds 20% tax on treasury bill returns, leaving about EGP 21,080, or 21.1%. With annual inflation at 12.7%, part of that gain only keeps pace with prices: the real return is roughly 1.211 ÷ 1.127 − 1, or about 7.5%. That real yield, not the headline 26%, is what grows purchasing power.

**The foreign investor's sum:** A fund buying the same bill with dollars must also convert back. If the pound weakens 10% over the year, its dollar return falls to roughly 1.2635 ÷ 1.10 − 1, or about 14.9%; a 20% slide would cut it to about 5%. That is why foreign appetite for Egyptian debt tracks expectations for the pound as closely as the yield itself, and why yields rise when those expectations wobble.

**The takeaway:** Compare yields after tax and inflation, and for any currency other than your own, after the expected exchange-rate move.
