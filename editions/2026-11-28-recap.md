*Week of November 22 to 28, 2026: a deadly fire on Cairo's Ring Road, an IMF deal that closes Egypt's loan program, a weaker pound, war spreading across the Gulf, and Wall Street's best week in more than a year.*

## Since Friday

**Saudi Arabia and the Houthis trade strikes on capitals:** Strikes on Yemen's capital, Sanaa, wounded at least 15 people on Saturday, according to the Houthi-run health ministry, while Saudi Arabia said it shot down a Houthi cruise missile aimed at Riyadh, the Associated Press reported. The exchange follows a week of Houthi attacks on Saudi soil, including a drone strike on Riyadh's PIF Tower on November 24 that injured 11 people, four of them Egyptian, and a strike on the 400,000-barrel-a-day Jazan refinery on November 26 that killed a Nepali worker; the coalition says it has destroyed 50 Houthi targets. Ministers of the Mecca defense pact, which binds Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey, called the attacks "unacceptable" in Islamabad on November 27. The Saturday casualty figure comes from the Houthi side only.

**US disables another blockade runner:** US forces disabled the merchant ship Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman on November 26, saying it tried to breach the naval blockade on Iranian ports, US Central Command said; the crew was unharmed. It is at least the 15th ship disabled under the blockade, with 158 more turned back, and Iran's navy chief promised operations to "break the naval blockade" and drive US forces from the region's waters.

**Doctors Without Borders staff killed in Gaza:** An Israeli strike late on Friday killed two watchmen working for Doctors Without Borders (MSF) at one of its clinics in Gaza City, bringing the group's staff deaths in Gaza to 17. MSF said it was "devastated and outraged"; Israel said on Saturday, without giving evidence, that the two were Hamas militants. The AP described the strike as near the clinic rather than on it.

**Misr Life Insurance prices its IPO:** The state insurer set its offer price at EGP 12.80 a share, within its EGP 12.00-13.60 range, after institutional bids reached EGP 31.2 billion, 3.42 times the shares on offer, Asharq Business reported, citing sources. The sale of 750 million shares, 15% of the company, raises about EGP 9.6 billion and values it at about EGP 64 billion. Retail subscription closes on November 30, and trading is expected to start around December 3.

**Weather:** Cairo has a mix of sun and cloud today with a high of 23-24°C and a low of 15-17°C, with morning fog possible; highs stay at 22-24°C through Tuesday, with a chance of showers.

**What to watch today:** Zamalek vs Club Africain and Pyramids vs Maghreb Fes open the CAF Champions League group stage at 6pm Cairo time; qualifying for the Formula 1 Qatar Grand Prix at Lusail; the second day of talks among the Saudi-Pakistani-Turkish Mecca defense pact's ministers in Islamabad; Misr Life Insurance's retail subscription stays open.

## The Week in Brief

1. **Ring Road fire:** A collision with a fuel tanker on Cairo's Ring Road near Warraq killed 31 people and injured 26, and pledges for the victims passed EGP 360 million.
2. **IMF deal:** Egypt reached a staff-level agreement on the eighth and final review of its IMF program, which would release about $2.3 billion once the board approves it in December.
3. **The pound:** The central bank's dollar rate weakened by about one pound in the week to EGP 54.91, and the one-year treasury bill yield rose to 26.35%, its highest since March 2024.
4. **Suez Canal:** Canal revenue in November doubled from a year earlier to $593 million, and Maersk told President Sisi it would bring its full network back to the Suez route.
5. **Misr Life listing:** The state insurer's EGP 9.6 billion IPO was priced at EGP 12.80 a share after strong institutional demand.
6. **Gulf war widens:** Drones killed two Italian technicians in Kuwait; Kuwait and Italy blamed Iran, which denied it, as US and Iranian forces faced off over the blockade of Iranian ports.
7. **Houthis and Saudi Arabia:** Houthi strikes hit Riyadh's PIF Tower, injuring 11, four of them Egyptian, and the Jazan refinery, and Saudi Arabia struck back in Yemen.

9. **Pyramids top Al Ahly:** Pyramids won 2-0 to stay perfect in the league, and Al Ahly sacked coach Hussein Ammouta the next day.

## What Tied the Week Together

The war in the Gulf moved closer to the economy every day. Houthi missiles and drones struck a Saudi refinery and Riyadh's business district, drones killed two European civilians in Kuwait, and attacks on shipping around Hormuz in November reached their highest since the war began. Brent rose about 3% on the week to settle at $95.44 on Friday. At the same time, strong US data, with third-quarter growth revised up to 4.3% and core inflation at 3.3%, left futures pricing a near-certain Federal Reserve rate rise on December 9, and the dollar index stood near its highest in a year.

For Egypt, that mix of dearer oil and a firmer dollar showed up in the pound and the bill market. The central bank's selling rate weakened from EGP 53.88 to 54.91, foreign and Arab investors sold a net $696 million of treasury bills on the secondary market during the week, according to the EGX's weekly report as carried by Masrawy, and the one-year yield climbed to 26.35%. The EGX30 lost 2.2%, and smaller companies fell further. Gold in Egyptian shops, priced off both the world price and the pound, rose 6.6%.

Against that, the week brought the dollar news that the economy has waited for: an IMF deal worth about $2.3 billion, canal revenue running at double last year's pace and Maersk's pledge to return. Going into next week, three things are unresolved: what OPEC+ decides on Sunday, whether the Fed raises rates on December 9, and what replaces the IMF program when it ends on December 15, ahead of the central bank's rate meeting on December 17.

## Egypt This Week

**Ring Road fire:** A private car hit a fuel tanker on the Ring Road near Warraq on November 25, setting off a fire that killed 31 people and injured 26, according to the Cabinet. Prosecutors ordered the car's driver held for four days; he told investigators he was driving at 110 km/h and hit the tanker from behind, though some accounts say the tanker swerved first. Pledges for the victims passed EGP 360 million by Saturday, banks pledged EGP 60 million at the central bank's direction, and aid payments to families start on November 30.

**IMF final review:** Prime Minister Mostafa Madbouly said Egypt and the IMF reached a staff-level agreement on the eighth and final review of the $8 billion program, worth about $2.3 billion: about $1.5 billion from the Extended Fund Facility and up to $800 million from the Resilience and Sustainability Facility. The IMF board is due to consider it in December, before the program expires on December 15. No successor program has been announced.

**Suez Canal revival:** Canal revenue reached $593 million from November 1 to about November 24, up 101% from $295 million a year earlier, Business Today Egypt reported. Maersk's chief executive, Vincent Clerc, told President Sisi that the company's full network would return to the Suez route "in the coming months," without giving a date.

**Regional visitors:** Iraq's prime minister, Ali al-Zaidi, agreed with President Sisi on November 23 to lift entry visas between the two countries, with no start date yet, as Baghdad studies supplying Egypt with about 10 million barrels of crude. Sudan's prime minister, Kamil Idris, visited on November 24, and the two countries said Ethiopia's Nile dam, run without a binding agreement, remains a threat to downstream states. Qatar's emir, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, discussed Gaza, Gulf security, trade and energy with President Sisi on November 26; no investment figure was announced.

**Energy squeeze:** Three LNG cargoes bound for Egypt sailed elsewhere in November as European buyers outbid Cairo, EnterpriseAM reported, and gas supply to fertilizer plants stays cut by 20% to 40%, at $12.75 per mmBtu through December 31. On the supply side, the state oil company hired Weatherford to bring about 100 idle wells back into production within 18 months, and Valmore Holding expects the next phase of its Offshore North Sinai gas project to start in early 2027.

**Phone prices:** Mobile phone prices have risen about 60% since January and sales have fallen 25% to 30%, according to Mohamed Talaat of the Federation of Chambers of Commerce, a trade estimate rather than an official index. Madbouly ordered a plan to rein in prices on November 22; no measures have followed yet.

## Region and World

**Iran and Hormuz:** Drones hit an air-defense site in Kuwait on November 26, killing two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall, the first European civilians killed in the war, the BBC reported. Italy's foreign minister said the drones were Iranian; Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, called the charge "a precooked recipe for war" and offered a joint inquiry, while saying Qatar and Pakistan are still relaying proposals to Washington. President Trump said that if the US acts again, it would "definitely hit Pickaxe Mountain," a fortified site near Natanz.

**Hamas ultimatum and UN peacekeepers:** Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said on November 26 that Hamas must "disarm voluntarily" or face "another evacuation of one million Gaza residents southward." Israel killed Hamas security chief Naim Abu Nahl on November 24. In Lebanon, Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said France is drafting a Security Council resolution to keep UN peacekeepers working after their mandate ends on December 31.

**Warship sunk in Libya:** Ukraine said seven of its naval drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi on November 22, about 2,400 km from the front line, and two Libyan officers later died of their wounds. Libya said it was "not a party," and Russia's ambassador said the attack "will not go unpunished."

**Russia and NATO:** NATO's council met on November 23 after a Russian missile crashed in Poland about 60 km from Ukraine; Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it an "intentional provocation," while President Trump said it came down "by mistake." The EU proposed a 22nd sanctions package that would bar transactions with about 60 more Russian banks and target over 70 shadow-fleet tankers, and leaked Russian statistics office records reviewed by the BBC and Mediazona showed at least 490,334 Russian soldiers killed since 2022.

**US tariff plan:** The US proposed tariffs on 14 trading partners on November 25, with maximum rates of 15% for the EU, 20% for China, 25% for India and 35% for Vietnam. It is a proposal only: comments are due by January 5 and hearings start on January 25. Egypt is not on the list.

## Markets This Week

Wall Street recovered most of its rout: after the worst week since 2020, the S&P 500 gained 5.6%, the Nasdaq 6.7% and the Dow 5.5%, closing the shortened Black Friday session at 7,297.49, 24,129.50 and 50,774.94, as AI and chip stocks bounced, Reuters reported. Nvidia jumped about 31% on Monday when Jensen Huang returned as chief executive, then gave much of it back. Chinese shares had their biggest daily gain in two years on Friday, with the Shanghai Composite up 4.2% after Beijing promised more market support, and Dubai's index rose 5.6% on the week. Gold rebounded from a 15-month low to $3,653.50 an ounce. Egypt moved the other way: the EGX30 fell 2.2% between the Thursday closes, the EGX70 lost 3.5%, and market value fell by EGP 103 billion to EGP 3.96 trillion, according to the EGX's weekly report, as the pound weakened and bill yields rose.
